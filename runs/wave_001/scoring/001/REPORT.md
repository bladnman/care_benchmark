## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 72.0%; affective whys recovered at 58.5%. Architecture-heavy rationales such as S7, S8, F17, F18, F36, and F26 survived better than relationship-register rationales such as F22, F23, F33, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys did slightly better (65.4%) than weight-2 whys (57.5%). The high-weight whys were usually load-bearing enough to be encoded, but secondary and consequence layers still dropped.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved philosophy better than specific feature whys: 82.1% system-level vs 58.6% feature-level. The reconstruction reads the plan's architectural worldview well, then loses several targeted feature-level headroom whys.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanisms often survived. Missing layers were usually the secondary temptation or downstream relationship consequence: F3 lost the return-after-absence consequence; F4 lost the audio-spine layer; F16 lost the retrofit warning; F31 lost the retroactive-relationship collapse.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, privacy, performance, and accessibility are strongest. Social/visit whys are weakest: the plan implements invites, logs, projections, and revocation, but the reconstruction rarely recovers why these avoid publication, attention loops, or show-off rendering.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The v06 operator denied credit where a rule was present without the why. Clear examples are F19, F22, F23, F33, F34, and F37-F39.

What this suggests: the candidate is strong at turning the PRD into an executable architecture, but still compresses fragile affective rationale into operational bans and invariants.
