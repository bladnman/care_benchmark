## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional and affective recovery were close in aggregate, but the failure shape differed. Functional losses were usually missing calibration layers (F1, F2, F13, F16, F20, F40). Affective losses were more often rule-only surfaces: no welcome toast, no friend-visited notification, no leaderboards, per-invite sharing, visit log, and actual-aviary visits.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered better (76.0/108.0) than weight-2 whys (21.0/44.0), mainly because the plan and reconstruction emphasized load-bearing architecture and accessibility. Lower-weight feature exceptions were often named without rationale.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was much higher than feature-level fidelity. The plan encoded the philosophy strongly, but the reconstruction compressed many feature-level exceptions into mechanisms.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary causes often survived. Secondary and downstream layers leaked: signal-by-signal presence precision, phase-cancel audio rationale, design-time-vs-retrofit privacy rationale, and screen-reader queue overload were typical omissions.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, accessibility, and telemetry were strongest. Social/visit and account-management whys were weakest: F22, F37, F38, F39, and F35 were captured as features but not as reasons.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied likely v1-style credit where rules survived without rationale. There were 13 rule-without-why cases and no ungrounded-reconstruction cases.

What this suggests: the candidate plan is operationally excellent, but phase-2A reconstruction preserved architectural intent more reliably than product-affective exception rationale.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add or preserve explicit rationale fields for quiet exclusions and social/privacy boundaries. This run implemented those areas but often dropped the why.
- Keep targeted headroom whys like F29-F40. They exposed real differentiation despite a near-complete plan.
- Consider adding a small number of social/account-management multi-layer whys. The benchmark currently caught those with single-layer rows, but the failure shape suggests these areas deserve more diagnostic resolution.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar, but require scorers to list the concrete inheritance sites as this report does; that made the subjective S2/S4 calls auditable.
- Keep the strict no-backfill rule. Several rows marked NOT RECOVERABLE or rule-only were tempting to infer from the plan, but scoring the frozen reconstruction is what makes the benchmark meaningful.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The frozen reconstruction was treated as read-only and was not modified. No subagents or background agents were used in this scoring turn.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one candidate run at one temperature/effort configuration; there is no within-model variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Feature 43 was counted captured because settle/undo/session-end was present, though evening lighting was missing. Feature 53 was counted captured because single-screen was taken as equivalent to no panning. These calls do not materially alter the conclusion.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2 and S4 were partial, not full, because the reconstruction preserved structural restraint but not all gold affective layers.
- **Confabulation.** No scored recovery was treated as ungrounded/confabulated; validity audit verdict is PASS.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F5, F6, F8, F10, F11, F22, F23, F29, F34, F35, F37, F38, and F39 were mechanism-only or unrecovered why cases.
- **Timing.** TIMING.json contained phase1 and phase2a for run 001; no phase2b timing was available at score-write time, so the score JSON includes only available bounded timing fields.
