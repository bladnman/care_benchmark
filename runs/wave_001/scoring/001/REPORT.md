## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 44.0/54.0 weighted points (81.5%); affective whys recovered 85.0/98.0 (86.7%). Functional losses clustered around exact privacy/identity data rationale (F16, F34) and presence downstream consequences (F1, F13). Affective losses clustered around anti-engagement exceptions whose rule was preserved but whose social/product consequence was compressed (F8, F22, F23, F27, F30).
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys survived better: 96.0/108.0 (88.9%) versus 33.0/44.0 (75.0%) for weight-2. The plan made most load-bearing systems explicit enough for the reconstructor to recover them.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System philosophy was stronger than feature-level rationale: 96.4% vs 82.3%. The main system miss was S3, where specificity stayed visible in many feature passages but was not named as an independent system principle.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** The most frequently dropped layer was the downstream consequence: silent population corruption for presence, retrofit impossibility for email-derived IDs, gamification erosion, and reward-economy drift for age-based offers.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, accessibility, and rendering were especially strong. Social/engagement refusals and account-export rationale were weaker because the reconstruction often summarized the prohibition without the reason the prohibition exists.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Apparent recoveries were denied or limited when the reconstruction carried only a rule. F6, F8, F22, F23, and F34 were mechanism-only; partial rows such as F1, F13, F14, F16, F27, and F30 lost credit for missing layers.

The failure shape suggests a very strong implementation plan whose carry-through compression mostly risks future maintainers retaining decisions while losing the arguments that keep later product drift from reintroducing adjacent patterns.
