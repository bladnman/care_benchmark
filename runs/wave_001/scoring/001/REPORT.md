## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered 67.0 / 98.0 weighted points (68.4%); functional whys recovered 32.0 / 54.0 (59.3%). Functional losses were concentrated in implementation-edge whys where B preserved architecture but dropped failure scenarios: F1, F2, F16, F17, F18, and F20.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys did better (76.0 / 108.0 = 70.4%) than weight-2 whys (23.0 / 44.0 = 52.3%). The richer PLAN language helped with primary causes, but many downstream layers were still compressed out.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was very high at 96.4%; feature-level fidelity was 58.1%. The planner encoded philosophy better than it encoded every feature's specific rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** The most commonly dropped layer was the downstream consequence: F1, F2, F9, F13, F16, F17, F18, F20, F27, and F30 lost the failure-mode story while keeping mechanisms.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio and accessibility were strongest (F4, F24, F25, F26, F40 full). Social visit details and targeted relationship-ownership whys were weakest (F22, F23, F37, F38, F39 none).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F6, F8, F11, F22, F23, F29, F33, F37, F38, and F39 are the clearest rule-without-why rows. F35 was an explicit `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN` and scored none.

What this suggests: the candidate is excellent at building a comprehensive implementation plan and preserving high-level product philosophy, but the encoding channel compresses away feature-local reasons, especially reasons framed as social/relationship boundary protection or future-failure prevention.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Preserve targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They were the main place this otherwise saturated plan showed meaningful differentiation.
- Add a layer-type diagnostic to the report schema: primary cause vs secondary contributor vs downstream consequence. This run mostly lost downstream consequence layers, which is more informative than a generic partial label.
- Clarify whether `plan_only_not_reconstructed_count` is row-level or layer-level for partial multi-layer rows. I counted row-level omissions where at least one gold layer was grounded in PLAN but missing from B.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar at three inherited features. S4 shows that the bar finds a real distinction between preserved mechanics and explicitly recovered principle.
- Consider a small set of targeted whys for implementation exceptions that good planners may defend differently, such as the personality-vector export call in this PLAN.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the frozen reconstruction as read-only and scored only the assigned allowlisted inputs. I did not read `prd/`, peer slots, other waves, or checked-in harness-specific agent folders.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run and one frozen reconstruction; it has no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive for feature 1 (headline concept) and feature 7 (glossary/domain terms), because the plan operationalizes those concepts throughout scope, data model, API, and module map. All F1-F40 feature-level anchors were independently captured, so this does not affect fidelity reachability.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 is the most subjective system call. PLAN preserves restraint across bird cap, single scene, no chrome, and slow ramp; B did not clearly identify the depth-over-variety rationale, so it scored partial.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no material ungrounded reconstruction claims; losses were omission/compression, not invented rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The strict evidence gate denied several plausible v1-style recoveries where the reconstruction had only the rule, not the why.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 12 such cases, mostly around social boundaries, starter/adoption framing, notebook read-only status, and hidden personality numerics.
