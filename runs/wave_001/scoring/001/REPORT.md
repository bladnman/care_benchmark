## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** The weighted rates were almost identical: functional 31/54 and affective 56/98. The failure shape differed. Functional leakage concentrated around privacy/identity/account whys (F16, F20 partial, F34, F35, F36 recovered); affective leakage concentrated around relationship-frame reasons behind familiar refusals (F8, F19, F21, F23, F38, F39).
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys did only slightly better than weight-2 whys (62/108 vs 25/44). The multi-layer structure exposed compression: many rows recovered the main rule but dropped the secondary calibration or downstream failure case.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved philosophy better than feature rationales. System-level fidelity was 75.0%, while feature-level fidelity was 53.2%. S1, S5, S7, and S9 were full; many F rows became rule-only.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary layers survived most often. The common losses were downstream consequence layers (for example F3, F18, F27, F31) and calibration/exception layers (F1/F13, F24, F40).
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, audio, accessibility, and first-frame rendering were strongest. Accounts/privacy/social sharing had good mechanisms but weaker recovered whys, especially F16, F20, F34, F35, F37-F39.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Rule-only denials materially lowered fidelity. F8, F16, F19, F21, F23, F29, F34, F35, F38, and F39 would look better under a looser mechanism-matching rubric, but v06 properly denied why credit.

The failure shape suggests a strong implementation planner whose plan was broad and often principled, but whose reconstruction lost the deeper product-rationale layer whenever the rule looked self-explanatory.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted headroom additions. F29-F40 separated high coverage from real rationale preservation, especially export, deletion, sharing, visit-log, and narration-cadence whys.
- Add more exception whys where the implementation rule is tempting to treat as obvious. This run shows those are the best discriminators: hidden vectors, synthetic IDs, matter-of-fact error tone, account export, deletion, and visit transparency.
- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It prevented rule-only matches from inflating fidelity and made the numerator/denominator distinction clear.
- Make system-level scoring guidance ask explicitly whether the reconstruction articulated the *why*, not just a neighboring design principle. S3, S4, and S8 were the most subjective calls.
- Consider adding a small per-layer summary requirement for reconstructions in future phase 2A prompts, if the goal is to measure whether the PLAN encodes multi-layer rationale rather than whether B can organize it after the fact.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** From the artifacts, the freeze contract appears intact. The reconstruction follows the PLAN's section order, uses no gold IDs, and contains honest NOT RECOVERABLE entries.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run, so the score has no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on #44 settle-is-opt-in and #84 account deletion. Both remained low-credit because the reconstruction did not recover their whys.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The strict three-feature bar was applied. S3, S4, and S8 had strong plan grounding but thinner reconstruction articulation, so they scored partial.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were found. The reconstruction was more often conservative than overclaiming.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several mechanism-only cases were denied, especially F8, F16, F19, F21, F23, F34, F35, F38, and F39.
- **Timing.** TIMING.json provided phase 1 and phase 2A timing only for run 001. I did not fabricate phase 2B timing.
