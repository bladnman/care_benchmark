## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered slightly better: **40.0 / 54.0** weighted versus affective **68.0 / 98.0**. The strongest functional survivals were S7, S8, F17, F18, and F36. Affective leaks concentrated in F5, F8, F19, F29, and F33, where the plan/reconstruction preserved the rule but not the felt reason.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered **78.0 / 108.0** and weight-2 whys recovered **30.0 / 44.0**. High-weight items did not saturate: the extra layers exposed missing downstream failure stories in F1, F2, F4, F7, F9, F12, F13, F14, F16, F20, F27, F30, and F31.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was high at **85.7%**, because the reconstruction recovered the main product posture. Feature-level fidelity was lower at **67.7%** because local rationales were often compressed into implementation rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes survived most often; secondary contributors and downstream consequences leaked first. The common shape was "rule plus immediate purpose, missing failure mode."
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and privacy were strongest. Audio and accessibility had strong implementation capture, but audio lost some affective rationale around why loops break aliveness. Social/visit whys were mostly recovered except where the rationale depended on subtle attention-loop framing.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The operator denied full credit to mechanism-only rows. F5, F6, F8, F19, F29, and F33 are the clearest feature cases; S4 is the clearest system case.

What the failure shape suggests: the candidate wrote an excellent build plan, and the reconstruction was faithful to that plan, but implementation plans still tend to compress poetic or product-psychology rationales into prohibitions and architecture.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound requirement. It sharply separated feature capture from why recovery in a high-planning-quality run.
- Add or retain more affective exception whys, especially where a strong planner can easily write the rule: hidden trait numbers, starter-bird arrival, notebook read-only, matter-of-fact error copy, and seven-bird cap.
- For multi-layer whys, consider a scorer note field that names the most commonly lost layer. In this run, downstream consequence was the repeated leak.
- Keep the cross-cutting system bar, but define how much mechanism-only evidence can satisfy a system principle. S3 and S4 were preserved in PLAN but under-named in reconstruction.
- Maintain targeted headroom additions. F29-F40 avoided score saturation and revealed meaningful omissions even with 100% feature capture.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scorer used the assigned plan, metadata, bounded timing, frozen reconstruction, and phase-two packet only. The frozen reconstruction was not modified.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one sample; it does not measure run-to-run variance for `gpt-6-luna` at this effort setting.
- **Borderline capture calls.** None changed the planning score. The closest conceptual call was the glossary/domain-terms feature, counted as captured because the plan defined the domain through persistent model/API/simulation concepts.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3 and S4 were the most subjective: the plan preserved both across at least three decisions, but B did not articulate them as full system-level rationales.
- **Confabulation cases.** No ungrounded reconstruction was credited; ungrounded reconstruction count is zero.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several rows that would look adequate under a rule-only scorer received no or partial recovery because the gold why was absent from B.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Seven rows preserved a mechanism without the rationale. These counted for capture but not for why recovery.
