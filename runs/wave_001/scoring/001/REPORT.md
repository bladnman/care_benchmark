## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional intent recovered **24.0/46.0** weighted points, while affective intent recovered **31.0/82.0**. The planner was strongest where architecture carried the rationale directly, especially S7/F17 server-side simulation and F36 aggregate telemetry. Affective exceptions often collapsed into prohibitions: F14 no streaks, F22 no visit notification, F23 no leaderboards, F27 no gamification, and F28 no Tamagotchi all preserved rules without why.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered **40.0/92.0**, only slightly above weight-2 whys at **15.0/36.0**. High-weight architecture whys helped, but high-weight affective whys such as F3, F4, F9, F14, and F27 were mostly mechanism-only.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (**60.7%**) was much higher than feature-level fidelity (**38.0%**). The reconstruction recovered broad principles like server-authored state, first-class accessibility, and aggregate privacy, but feature-specific exceptions often lost their rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary implementation layers survived more often than secondary temptation/failure-mode layers. F2 kept low-pass calibration but lost the full Tamagotchi/screensaver contrast; F30 kept age-based deepening but lost the rejected reward-loop economy; F40 kept slow cadence but not screen-reader queue overwhelm.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and accessibility were comparatively strong. Interactions, social optional, and non-goal exclusions were much weaker because the PLAN usually named the boundary without explaining the relationship it protected.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied many plausible v1-style credits. F3, F4, F14, F21-F23, F27-F29, F31, F33, F34, and F39 are clear rule-without-why cases.

What this suggests: this candidate can produce a broad build plan, but its plan is an implementation checklist more than an intent carrier. The frozen reconstruction honestly reflected that compression instead of inventing missing rationales.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated broad planning coverage from intent transfer and made mechanism-only cases visible.
- Add or preserve more targeted feature-level exception whys around common shortcuts: return greetings/no toast, social visit notification/log behavior, visitor actual-aviary treatment, and account deletion/export. This run showed those are easy to name and easy to lose.
- Consider adding a small secondary metric for "mechanism preservation" only as a diagnostic, not as intent fidelity. The rule-without-why count was high enough to be informative.
- Keep multi-layer whys for affective features. The missing layer was often the downstream consequence, which is exactly where implementation plans tend to compress.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring pass read only the allowed phase-two files, assigned PLAN, candidate metadata, run 001 timing, assigned frozen reconstruction, and assigned scoring folder. The reconstruction was not modified.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run, so there is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Borderline inclusive calls were made for starter birds (#25), no-per-bird telemetry (#85), and visitor actual-aviary viewing (#97). These increase planning quality slightly but do not create recovery without reconstruction evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 and S6 were the hardest calls. S4 is visible in the PLAN but not identified by the reconstruction; S6 appears as presence-time plumbing without the precise real-interaction rationale.
- **Confabulation cases.** No scored ungrounded reconstruction was found. The reconstruction often used `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN` rather than inventing rationales.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several rows would look better under a looser semantic scorer, especially no-gamification/social/no-notification rows. v06 correctly denied why recovery where only the rule survived.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 17 included rule-without-why cases, the largest diagnostic pattern in this run.
