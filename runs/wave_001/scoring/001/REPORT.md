## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered better by weight (89/98 = 90.8%) than functional whys (36/54 = 66.7%). Functional losses clustered around precise data/sync consequences: F1/F13, F16, F18, and F35.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 86/108 = 79.6%, while weight-2 whys recovered at 39/44 = 88.6%. The high-weight rows discriminated because downstream failure modes were often compressed away.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level principles survived strongly (25/28 = 89.3%); feature-level fidelity was lower (100/124 = 80.6%). The planner preserved philosophy well, and the reconstructor compressed some detailed whys.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary rules usually survived. The most common missing layer was downstream consequence: silent drift corruption, client-sync failure examples, and the slow-rhythm/accessibility consequence.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio, accessibility, privacy boundary, and social refusals were strong. Presence/accounting and account lifecycle were more lossy.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F29 and F34 were clear rule-without-why cases. Several plausible v1-style partial recoveries remained partial under v06 because one or more layers lacked exact reconstruction evidence.

What this suggests: this plan is unusually good at encoding constraints as mechanisms, but very compact rationales still lose tail-risk explanations when reconstructed.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They exposed narrow but meaningful why loss even in a near-complete plan.
- Keep multi-layer high-weight whys. Most useful discrimination came from missing secondary/downstream layers rather than missing features.
- Clarify whether system-level recovery may cite per-feature reconstruction text when the system principle is not clearly named in the system section. S6 was the hardest call.
- Add an optional layer-level evidence count to future score JSON, still aggregate-only, so v06 layer loss can be analyzed without leaking feature identities.
- For future instances, include a few small surface-level non-why features like #87; they catch plans that cover architecture but miss literal settings-surface obligations.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scorer worked from the frozen reconstruction and did not modify it. Fresh isolation is assumed from the harness prompt.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** One run gives no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** #51 and #104 were marked captured but borderline; changing them would move planning by 1.7 points total, not fidelity.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The strict 3-feature bar was straightforward for most S rows but subjective for S3 and S6.
- **Confabulation cases.** No scored ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F29 and F34 had rules/features but not the gold rationale. Multi-layer partials often reflected plan-only or compressed-away downstream consequences.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** 2 included whys were marked rule_without_why.
