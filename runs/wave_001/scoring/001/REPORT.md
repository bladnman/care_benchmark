## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional rationale survived better: 34/54 weighted points (63.0%) versus affective 39/96 (40.6%). The plan and reconstruction carried architecture well (S7, F17, F18), but relationship-protecting exceptions such as F8, F14, F29, F31, F33, F34, and F39 mostly collapsed into rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys recovered 58/108 (53.7%) versus weight-2 at 15/42 (35.7%). The larger whys were more visible, but many still lost secondary and downstream layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was 60.7%; feature-level fidelity was 45.9%. The planner preserved broad philosophy and architecture better than feature-specific why exceptions.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary rules often survived; secondary temptation/failure-mode layers were the common losses. Examples: F4 recovered chorus/cascade but not looped-audio-as-dead-software; F16 recovered PII containment but not impossible-to-retrofit urgency; F40 recovered cadence but not queue-overwhelm rationale.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and simulation were strongest. Social and interaction boundaries were weaker: visits, visit notifications, show-off mode, field notebook read-only, and no-streak rationale were mostly mechanism-only. Accessibility was mixed: screen-reader prose recovered strongly, reduced-motion and narration cadence partially.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter operator denied credit for 16 included rule-without-why cases. The reconstruction did not confabulate much, which is good for validity, but its caution left many feature whys unrecovered.

What the failure shape suggests: this candidate can produce a buildable and architecturally coherent plan, but the plan/reconstruction channel is lossy for rationale that explains why tempting alternative products must be refused.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep evidence-bound scoring. It cleanly separated feature capture from intent recovery and exposed mechanism-only preservation.
- Add or retain targeted feature-level exception whys around interaction/social surfaces; they discriminated strongly in this run.
- Preserve multi-layer treatment for high-risk functional areas such as sync, privacy, calibration, and accessibility, because layer drops were diagnostically useful.
- Consider asking phase-2A reconstructors to distinguish "rule recovered" from "why recovered" explicitly; this would reduce ambiguity without exposing gold IDs.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar, but require the scorer to list inherited decisions as done here; S3-S5 show why the audit trail matters.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scorer used only the allowlisted phase-two instructions/templates, gold/rubric/schema/constants, PLAN, candidate metadata, bounded timing for run 001, frozen reconstruction, and scoring slot. No PRD, peer slot, other wave, memory, subagent, or background agent was used.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one low-effort candidate run; no variance signal is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls were applied to features 7, 15, 31, 47, 51, 57, 63, 64, 69, 84, 90, and 97. They raised planning quality but also kept denominator pressure on feature-level why recovery.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3-S5 were the hardest calls: each was preserved in PLAN across at least three decisions but not directly identified in reconstruction, so each received partial recovery.
- **Confabulation cases.** None found. The reconstruction mostly stayed close to PLAN text and often chose NOT RECOVERABLE rather than inventing rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The largest denial category was rule-without-why. Single-layer whys such as F5, F6, F8, F19, F21-F23, F29, F32-F35, and F39 had captured mechanisms but no scoreable reconstructed rationale.
