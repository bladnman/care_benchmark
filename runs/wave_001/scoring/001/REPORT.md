## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered slightly better by weight (72.4%) than functional whys (66.7%). Functional misses were mostly second/third-layer calibration or data-governance consequences (F1, F2, F16, F20, F35), not the basic mechanisms.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-2 whys recovered strongly (84.1%). Weight-3 whys were weaker (64.8%) because the reconstruction often kept the primary rule and one consequence while dropping a secondary rationale.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The system spine was strong (82.1%): server-owned simulation, privacy boundary, accessibility, and voice split all survived. Feature-level fidelity was lower (67.7%) because several exact feature whys became rule-only rows.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 usually survived. Layer 2 was the most fragile for affective whys (for example S2, F14, F31), while layer 3 was the most fragile for calibration/architecture whys (F1, F2, F13, F16).
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio, reduced-motion, server tick, no-LWW sync, and age-based bird offers were very strong. Return greeting, welcome toast, settle opt-in, leaderboards, account deletion, and visitor show-off mode were the main feature-level losses.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The v06 evidence gate denied credit where the plan captured the rule but the reconstruction did not carry the why: F9, F10, F11, F23, F35, and F39. No confabulated gold why received credit.

What this suggests: the candidate plan is excellent as an implementation artifact and robust at architectural principles. The remaining loss is mainly in feature-level affective intent where the plan encoded a refusal or mechanism but the blind reconstruction did not reproduce the deeper relationship rationale.
