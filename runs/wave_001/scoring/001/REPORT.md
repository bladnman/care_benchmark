## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better (19.0/48.0) than affective whys (21.0/80.0). The plan explicitly explained server continuity, no last-write-wins, drift calibration, and telemetry boundaries, while affective exceptions often reduced to feature names.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 28.0/96.0; weight-2 whys recovered 12.0/32.0. High-weight whys did not consistently survive better; multi-layer consequence layers were commonly dropped.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (50.0%) substantially exceeded feature-level fidelity (26.0%). Broad philosophy survived better than precise feature rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes were most likely to survive. Secondary contributors and downstream consequences, such as silent drift corruption, engagement-feature footholds, and accessibility exclusion consequences, were usually lost.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and performance fared best. Social, onboarding, announcement refusal, and layout/arrival affect were weakest.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Eighteen included feature whys were rule-without-why cases. The stricter evidence gate denied likely v1-style credit for F4, F6, F14, F16, F30, F31, F32, F37, F39, and F40.

What the failure shape suggests: this planner is competent at producing an implementation skeleton, but it compresses product intent into mechanisms. The blind reconstruction largely preserved that compression instead of hallucinating missing whys, which is good for validity but costly for fidelity.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound ledger. It cleanly separates captured features from recovered intent and exposes rule-without-why failures.
- Retain targeted feature-level headroom for social, onboarding, and accessibility cadence. These areas caught losses that a surface implementation checklist would miss.
- Consider asking phase-1 plans for short rationale bullets under high-risk clusters, then keep phase-2A blind. That would test whether the candidate can preserve why without needing verbose prose everywhere.
- Keep multi-layer whys for product-defining features, but review whether layer scoring should separately report primary/secondary/consequence recovery. The dominant loss here was consequence-layer compression.
- The system-level cross-cutting bar worked, but several partials depend on subjective interpretation; future instances could add explicit examples for partial broad-theme matches.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** From the phase-2B scorer perspective, the frozen reconstruction appears isolated: it mirrors PLAN sections and uses no gold IDs or rubric vocabulary.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only; no variance signal is available here.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on presence definition, drift calibration, read-only notebook implication, and visitor interaction limits. These raised planning quality but also increased the feature-level denominator.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The preserved-in-plan judgment was most subjective for S1, S4, S6, S8, and S9; the appendix lists the exact inheritance points used.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted. Some reconstruction wording is interpretive, but it stays close to the plan.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Most denied recoveries were feature rows where an implementation rule existed but neither PLAN nor reconstruction carried the gold rationale.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 18 included rule-without-why cases, concentrated in social, accessibility cadence, presence precision, and bird identity/onboarding.
