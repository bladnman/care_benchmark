## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 34.0/54.0 weighted points (63.0%), while affective whys recovered 54.0/96.0 (56.3%). Server ownership, no-LWW, read-only visits, and telemetry boundaries survived better than the more relational whys around being seen, not managed, not compared, or not turned into a journal.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 62.0/108.0 (57.4%), below weight-2 whys at 26.0/42.0 (61.9%). Multi-layer rows often kept L1 and lost L2/L3: F1, F2, F9, F14, F25, F27, F31, F35, and F40 show this pattern.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was much stronger (78.6%) than feature-level fidelity (54.1%). The plan carried big architecture and posture clearly, but feature-level exceptions like F8, F19, F29, F33, F34, and F39 often reduced to the rule.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Sync/account architecture was strongest where the plan gave concrete failure modes. Social was mixed: read-only visits and notifications-off survived, but no leaderboards and no show-off mode lost the relationship-specific why. Accessibility was strong at the system level, with thinner layer recovery for reduced motion and narration cadence.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter operator denied credit where a v1-style semantic pass might have been generous: hidden stats, matter-of-fact errors, starter birds, read-only notebook, export, and no show-off mode all had operational rules but not recoverable whys.

Overall, this run suggests the candidate plan is excellent at preserving build scope and architectural invariants, but it compresses many affective product reasons into broad negative-space principles such as no gamification, no notification patterns, and quiet social.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the F29-F40 targeted additions. They exposed meaningful headroom even when planning quality was high, especially around starter birds, age-based unlocks, export, deletion, and visit-sharing rationale.
- Add layer-level aggregate reporting for partial recoveries. Several rows had one plan-only omitted layer; a row-level plan-only count is too blunt for analyzing those misses.
- Add more examples for broad refusal principles like S2. A plan can clearly refuse notifications and gamification while still not carrying the exact affective distinction between announcing and noticing.
- Preserve the cross-cutting system bar. It separated robust architecture-level carry-through from one-off feature mentions, but future rubrics should give more examples for negative-space product philosophies.
- Continue mixing functional and affective whys. Functional whys did not saturate: precision-presence, synthetic IDs, telemetry boundaries, and no-LWW each exposed different kinds of compression.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scorer treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. The user explicitly supplied a fresh-context phase-2B assignment; no subagents or background agents were spawned.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run with no variance estimate.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls were made for presence definition, bird naming, no-welcome toast, settle gesture, presence accounting, evening palette, and visits default-off. These lifted planning quality but did not create fidelity credit without reconstruction evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The hardest calls were S2, S3, S4, and S6, where PLAN inheritance existed but B either generalized the rationale or dropped layers.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction assertions were found; most denials were compression or rule-without-why, not invented rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The main denials were F8, F10, F19, F23, F29, F30, F33, F34, and F39.
