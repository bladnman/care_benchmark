## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered **40/54** weighted points (74.1%). Affective whys recovered **64/98** (65.3%). The functional architecture was strongly encoded: server-owned state, additive deltas, privacy boundaries, synthetic IDs, and aggregate telemetry. Affective rows leaked more often when the mechanism was easy to state but the relationship reason was subtler: F8, F23, F30, F33, F37, F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered **78/108** (72.2%), outperforming weight-2 whys at **26/44** (59.1%). The planner/reconstructor retained load-bearing systems better than many small exception rationales.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was **85.7%**, feature-level **64.5%**. The plan's invariants made broad philosophy durable, but feature-level whys often collapsed to rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes often survived; downstream consequences were the common loss. Examples: F1/F13 lost silent population-wide drift corruption, F2 lost Tamagotchi-vs-screensaver endpoints, F9/F10 lost the notice-not-announce payoff, and F27 lost the intention-rotation path.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, audio, accessibility rendering, and privacy architecture scored well. Social/share surfaces and quiet relationship-rights surfaces were weaker: F37-F39 were rule-level only, and F34 export lost the relationship-copy framing.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter v06 gate mattered. Several rows would look strong under feature coverage alone, but recovered no why credit because the reconstruction preserved only the operational rule: hidden vector values, sync-conflict tone, no leaderboards, age-based offers, read-only notebook, export, per-invite sharing, visit log, and actual visitor view.

What this suggests: this candidate is an excellent implementation planner and a good system-intent encoder, but it compresses specific affective reasons into enforceable mechanisms. The product would likely be built close to spec, but a downstream team reading only the plan might not know why some quiet constraints are non-negotiable.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep v06 evidence-bound scoring. It produced useful separation between feature capture (98.3%) and why recovery (68.4%).
- Add more targeted headroom rows for social/privacy/export cases. F34 and F37-F39 show that strong plans can implement the right thing while losing the relationship rationale.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting bar, but require explicit layer notes for partials. S3 and S4 were preserved in PLAN but weakly identified in reconstruction; layer notes made the difference auditable.
- Consider adding a small set of single-layer affective exceptions around UI absence: no badge, no toast, no show-off mode, no journal editing. These are easy to implement as rules and easy to lose as reasons.
- Keep the 120-feature planning denominator. It caught two small misses without distorting the run: glossary and explicit browser-support-matrix policy.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scorer was instructed to treat the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. The validity audit found no significant contamination signs.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run for one candidate at one effort setting. It gives no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Two rows were borderline but counted captured per rubric: #52 settle undo captures the 5s undo but not exact click-anywhere UI, and #57 evening palette/call quieting is captured through lighting and dusk behavior rather than exact wording. These calls did not affect feature-level why reachability.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3 and S4 were partial because PLAN preserved them cross-cuttingly while the reconstruction did not directly identify the gold principle. This is the most subjective area.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were found; the audit count is 0.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Nine reachable feature whys were denied full recovery because they were rule-only or explicitly not recoverable from PLAN: F8, F19, F23, F30, F33, F34, F37, F38, F39.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Eleven rows were marked rule-without-why, including S3/S4 plus the nine feature rows above.
