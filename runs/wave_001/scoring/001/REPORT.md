## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better (50.0% weighted) than affective whys (37.6%). The strongest functional preservation was server/sync: S7, F17, and F18. Affective losses were concentrated around why a surface should feel quiet or specific rather than merely implementing the quiet rule.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys recovered at 43.6%, close to the overall score, because many recovered one or two layers but dropped consequences. Weight-2 whys recovered at 40.6%; they were often all-or-nothing and several became rule-without-why none rows.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System philosophy was more recoverable than feature intent: 60.7% vs 38.2%. The plan carried architecture and broad posture better than fine-grained PRD rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** The most common missing layer was L3: the downstream consequence or product-collapse reason. Examples: S1, S2, F2, F3, F14, F30.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync was strongest; social and accessibility were mixed; interaction/aviary-layout details leaked heavily, especially greeting and loading-state choices.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F4, F8, F19, F26, F31, F33, and F40 would look like plausible mechanism recoveries, but v06 correctly denied why credit because the rationale was not present in the frozen reconstruction.

The failure shape suggests the candidate was a strong implementation planner but a lossy intent compressor: it preserved most buildable surfaces, then translated many reasons into architecture or feature lists.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It made the mechanism-vs-why gap visible without rewarding plausible reconstruction.
- Add more targeted affective exception whys like F29-F40. The run saturated ordinary implementation coverage but leaked non-obvious rationales.
- Consider reporting a separate mechanism-preservation diagnostic beside intent fidelity. This candidate would be useful to builders, but the benchmark is measuring why transmission.
- Keep the system cross-cutting bar but require concise counts in reports. S4 showed that a population cap can masquerade as the whole restraint principle.
- For future instances, include more features where the implementation rule is cheap but the product consequence is subtle; those best separated rule following from intent recovery here.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The frozen reconstruction appears plan-derived, and this scoring pass did not modify it. No explicit gold ID leakage or rubric vocabulary was found.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one candidate run; no variance signal is available inside this score.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on settle semantics (#10/#44), evening palette (#57), top-bar/no-chrome (#63/#64), no-chat implicitness (#93), and mood persistence (#29). These raised planning quality but had limited benefit to fidelity because why evidence was still thin.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 was the hardest system call: the plan preserved cap/ramp rules but not enough restraint-over-richness surfaces for the cross-cutting bar.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not count any included row as a clear ungrounded reconstruction; most lost credit for absence or rule-only evidence, not invented rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The main denials were rule-without-why rows: the plan/reconstruction knew what to build but not why the product needed it that way.
