## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 32.0 / 54 weighted points (59.3%) versus affective whys at 55.0 / 96 (57.3%). The split was close; architecture helped functional rows, while relationship-frame exceptions leaked affective credit.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 62.0 / 108 (57.4%), while weight-2 whys recovered 25.0 / 42 (59.5%). High-weight whys often lost downstream consequence layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The plan preserved philosophy better than specific feature rationales: system-level fidelity was 67.9%, feature-level fidelity was 55.7%. B named most broad principles, but often flattened feature exceptions into operational rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanisms survived most often. Secondary calibration/architecture layers survived when functional and explicit. Downstream consequence layers were the common loss: S1, S2, S6, S9, F1, F2, F9, F10, F14, F16, F27, and F30 all dropped at least one downstream consequence.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync did well on canonical server state, no LWW, synthetic IDs, and telemetry boundaries. Accessibility did well on narration cadence and reduced motion. Social and relationship-copy features were weaker: the plan specified quiet sharing/export/log mechanics but rarely reconstructed the deeper relationship-protection reasons.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** `rule_without_why` denied credit for F5, F8, F26, F29, F31, F32, F33, F34, F35, and F39. F33 was the clearest ungrounded-reconstruction case: B asserted a journal/achievement-feed rationale, but the PLAN only grounded read-only notebook behavior.
