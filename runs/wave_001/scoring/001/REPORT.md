## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys were hurt by missing privacy/account lifecycle detail: S8, F16, F20, F34-F36, and F18/F19 are absent or rule-only. Affective whys did slightly better because no-punishment, no-gamification, naturalist prose, and reduced-motion rules were present, but many were shallow.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 30.0/88.0 weighted points; weight-2 whys recovered 9.0/26.0. High-weight whys did not strongly outperform because most multi-layer secondary/downstream consequences were missing.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was somewhat higher than feature-level fidelity. The plan communicates broad posture, but feature-specific exception rationales such as F14, F16, F23, F26, F31, F37, and F40 usually collapse into mechanism-only statements.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers survived most often. Secondary calibration layers survived for drift and server-side sync. Downstream consequence layers were usually dropped: no staleness leakage, no unlock-economy erosion, no screen-reader queue overwhelm, no privacy back-door framing.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Bird engine and accessibility had the best partial recovery. Accounts/privacy and social transparency were weakest. Audio preserved procedural/phase-canceling concerns but not the deeper no-loop/dead-software rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The evidence gate denied credit where a rule was present without a why: seven-bird cap, no streak counter, synthetic UUID, first-bird TTFB, per-invite sharing, stable bird identity, and slow narration cadence.

The failure shape suggests the phase-1 plan is useful as a build skeleton, but weak as an intent carrier. It preserves many implementation nouns and a few risks, while compressing away why-specific exceptions.
