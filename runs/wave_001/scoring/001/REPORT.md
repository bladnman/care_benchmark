## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 40/54 weighted points (74.1%). Affective whys recovered 76/98 (77.6%). The gap is modest, but the most visible misses are affective social/relationship rationales: F22, F29, F37, F38.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 86/108 (79.6%), slightly above weight-2 (30/44, 68.2%). The plan's load-bearing architecture helped high-weight simulation, sync, and accessibility whys survive.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (85.7%) outpaced feature-level fidelity (74.2%). B reconstructed the dominant architecture and product stance, while losing several narrow exception whys.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Dropped layers most often were secondary/downstream affective consequences: why a catalog changes first encounter (F29), why visit badges create social loops (F22/F38), why identity reset retroactively erases presence-time (F31), and why age-based offers must not become an economy (F30).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Seven rows were rule-without-why cases. The reconstruction honestly used NOT RECOVERABLE for some features, and those received no recovery even when the plan had the operational mechanism.

Overall, this is a very strong planning run and a good-but-compressed reconstruction: the product would largely be buildable from the plan, but some relationship-shaping whys did not survive as explicit intent.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated feature capture from rationale transfer, especially for F11, F22, F29, F37, and F38.
- Add more targeted social/privacy exception whys. This run shows that social affordance rationales are easy to compress into endpoints and abuse controls.
- Consider making voice/tone exceptions multi-layer. S5/F19 were hard to judge as single-layer because the rule survived while the evasive-error rationale did not.
- Ask reconstructors to synthesize system-level intent without simply mirroring plan headings. This would make S3/S5/S6 system recovery less dependent on scorer interpretation.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. The prompt and file separation indicate phase-2A was fresh-context; I cannot independently prove runtime isolation beyond the artifact boundaries.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one candidate plan and one reconstruction. No variance signal is available in this slot.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on starter-bird onboarding, offer reaction, settle optionality, bird-chosen perches, visits default-off, no chat/avatar visits, and browser-support matrix. These calls affect only a few planning-denominator points.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3, S5, and S6 were the main judgment calls: the plan preserved them cross-cuttingly, but B did not state them as system-level principles.
- **Confabulation.** I found no ungrounded reconstruction cases. The reconstruction mostly follows the plan's own headings and vocabulary.
- **Rule-without-why.** Seven included whys kept the rule/mechanism without the gold rationale; they counted for capture but not fidelity.
