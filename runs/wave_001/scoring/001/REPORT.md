## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys survived better: 92.0 / 98.0 weighted points (93.9%) versus functional 44.0 / 54.0 (81.5%). The functional losses were mostly architecture consequence layers: F16 retrofit risk, F18 silent last-write loss, F20 private-relationship/data-product framing, and F35 regret-before-erasure.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys remained strong but lower than weight-2: 96.0 / 108.0 (88.9%) versus 40.0 / 44.0 (90.9%). Multi-layer whys expose the compression: primary rules survived, but secondary temptation or downstream failure-mode language often thinned.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was 26.0 / 28.0 (92.9%); feature-level was 110.0 / 124.0 (88.7%). The plan carried both philosophy and details, but the reconstruction smoothed some feature-specific counterfactuals into broader implementation language.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** The most frequently dropped layer was the secondary or consequence layer that explains why an obvious shortcut is dangerous. Examples: S2 lost the processed-vs-seen affective register; F10 lost the well-meaning-toast temptation; F18 lost the silent deletion story; F35 lost the accidental-regret cause.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accessibility, audio, visits, and product behavior were especially faithful. Accounts/sync/privacy were implemented strongly but lost some gold rationale nuance around retrofit difficulty, last-write-wins failure, and private relationship data.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F6 and F23 are the clearest rule-without-why cases. The evidence-bound operator also kept F10, F16, F18, F20, and F35 at partial instead of granting full credit for plausible but incomplete semantic overlap.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Feature 7 (glossary) and feature 57 (evening calls quieter) were captured inclusively; changing both would move planning from 100.0% to 98.3% but would not change the why denominator.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound ledger. This run would look nearly saturated under a looser rubric, but the per-layer evidence requirement revealed meaningful compression in F10, F16, F18, F20, and F35.
- Add more targeted counterfactual layers for high-performing planners. The current headroom came from layers that name the tempting shortcut and its failure mode: small welcome toasts, last-write-wins, telemetry back doors, and reward loops.
- Preserve the functional/affective mix but add a few more functional whys about lifecycle and data architecture. Functional fidelity lagged affective fidelity mostly in accounts/sync/privacy, where a planner can specify the mechanism while omitting why the shortcut is dangerous.
- Clarify capture treatment for document-like rows such as the glossary and for compound rows such as evening palette plus quieter calls. They did not affect the headline here, but cross-scorer consistency would improve.
- Keep system-level cross-cutting at the three-feature bar. It worked well on this comprehensive plan; the only non-full system why, S2, was partial because one affective layer was missing, not because cross-cutting evidence was hard to find.
