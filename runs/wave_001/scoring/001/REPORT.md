## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 30.0/54.0 weight (55.6%); affective whys recovered 50.0/98.0 (51.0%). Architecture and performance rationales survived better than relationship/social rationales.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 rows recovered 56.0/108.0 (51.9%), slightly below weight-2 rows at 24.0/44.0 (54.5%). Multi-layer rows often preserved L1 while dropping failure consequences.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved philosophy more effectively than the reconstruction preserved feature rationales: system-level fidelity was 64.3%, feature-level fidelity was 50.0%.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Downstream consequences leaked most often: S1, S2, F10, F27, and F30 all lost the slippery-slope or product-collapse layer even where the immediate rule survived.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, and performance were strong (F2, F15, F17-F18, F26). Social/private relationship whys were weak where the plan named surfaces but not their affective stakes (F23, F38, F39).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Twelve included feature rows were marked `rule_without_why`: F5, F8, F11, F23, F29, F31, F32, F33, F34, F35, F38, and F39. No ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted.

What this failure shape suggests: the candidate can produce broad, buildable implementation plans, but it compresses many human-facing product rationales into operational rules. The reconstruction then inherits that compression and often cannot recover why the rule exists.
