## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional and affective recovery are almost tied: functional 30.0/54.0, affective 54.0/98.0. Functional server/sync items such as S7, F17, F18, and F36 survive best; affective identity and relationship-rights items such as F29, F31, F33, F39 leak.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recover slightly better (64.0/108.0) than weight-2 whys (20.0/44.0). High-weight entries usually preserve L1 and lose L2/L3 nuance.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level recovery is much stronger (78.6%) than feature-level recovery (50.0%). The plan carries broad philosophy better than feature-specific exceptions.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanisms survive most often. Secondary contributors and downstream consequences are frequently dropped.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Server/sync, audio, and accessibility score best. Social/share presentation, account relationship rights, and bird identity/intimacy are weaker.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F5, F8, F19, F23, F26, F29-F31, F33-F35, and F39 are the clearest rule-without-why denials. S3 and S4 are plan-only system principles.

What this suggests: the candidate produced a highly executable implementation plan, but it often encoded product taste as constraints rather than preserving the underlying why.
