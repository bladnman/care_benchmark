## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 42/54 weighted points (77.8%) and affective whys at 72/98 (73.5%). The difference is modest; the larger split is system-vs-feature rather than affective-vs-functional.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered better: 86/108 (79.6%) versus weight-2 whys at 28/44 (63.6%). The major product-spine rationale was more visible than smaller exception rationale.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level recovery (85.7%) was stronger than feature-level recovery (72.6%). B captured the plan philosophy, but compressed away individual whys for F5, F11, F19, F21, F29, F34, and F39.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Partial multi-layer rows usually retained L1 and lost L3: F1, F3, F9, F13, F16, F20, F30, and F40 show this pattern. Consequence language is the most brittle layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Architecture/sync, audio, accessibility, privacy deletion, and no-gamification were strong. Social-option nuance and small voice/UX exceptions leaked more often.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several mechanisms were captured but denied recovery because the why was not reconstructed: F5, F11, F19, F21, F29, F34, F39. This is the clearest value of v06 scoring.

The failure shape suggests that a very strong implementation plan can still be lossy as a rationale carrier when small exceptions are encoded as rules rather than as reasons.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted exception whys like F29/F34/F39; these are where implementation plans most easily preserve the rule while losing intent.
- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It prevented rule-only rows from receiving why credit.
- Make downstream-consequence layers more explicit in reconstruction prompts or scoring comments; they were the most common missing layer.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting check, but consider adding a clearer partial-identification category for cases like S3/S4 where PLAN inherits the principle but B names only part of it.
- For future products, include a few non-gold denominator features that are easy to miss (like responsive no-crop or policy-link details) to keep planning quality from saturating completely.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The reconstruction was treated as frozen and plan-derived. The validity audit found no significant contamination signs.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one candidate/reconstruction pair, so no within-model variance is available here.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Features 7, 44, and 52 were counted inclusively. The misses were feature 70 and feature 87; neither affects the F1-F40 denominator.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3, S4, and S6 were the main subjective calls. In each, PLAN preserved enough cross-cutting evidence, but B did not fully articulate the gold principle or all layers.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were scored; weak rows were mostly omissions or rule-without-why, not invented rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F5, F11, F19, F21, F29, F34, and F39 are the clearest denials: feature/rule survived, gold why did not.
- **Timing availability.** TIMING.json contained phase1 and phase2a for run 001 only. Phase2b timing was not present, so no phase2b duration was fabricated.
