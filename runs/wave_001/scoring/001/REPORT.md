## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional and affective recovery were nearly tied by weight: functional recovered 36.0/54.0 and affective recovered 63.0/94.0. Functional losses tended to be downstream calibration or privacy rationale (F2, F16, F20, F35), while affective losses tended to be relationship-framing language (F22, F23, F29, F33, F34).
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 68.0/104.0, slightly below weight-2 whys at 31.0/44.0. High-weight rows often kept primary mechanism but lost secondary or downstream layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level recovery was stronger (23.0/28.0) than feature-level recovery (76.0/120.0). The plan carried philosophy well, but reconstruction compressed feature-specific reasons into mechanisms.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 was usually preserved. Layer 2 and Layer 3 dropped when they were affective consequences rather than implementation constraints: F2, F14, F27, F30, F31, and F40 are representative.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Server architecture, sync, and audio were strongest (S7, F4, F17, F18). Social/refusal surfaces were weaker: F22 and F23 preserved rules but lost why. Account export and notebook editability similarly became mechanism-only.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F5, F22, F23, F29, F33, and F34 were marked `rule_without_why`; they counted for planning when captured but received no why recovery credit.

What the failure shape suggests: this candidate is excellent at implementation coverage and architectural intent, but less reliable at carrying quiet affective exceptions unless the plan names the consequence explicitly.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound ledger; it exposed the difference between broad capture and rationale recovery.
- Add more targeted feature-level whys for negative constraints and opt-out surfaces, since those showed rule-without-why leakage.
- Require or encourage planners to state downstream consequence clauses for multi-layer whys. This run usually preserved the primary rule but lost why-it-matters language.
- Consider a small validation script for `run_MMM.json` and HTML data shape so scorers spend less attention on schema bookkeeping.
- Keep the system cross-cutting bar, but clarify whether system-level evidence can come from the reconstruction's per-feature section. S6 was recoverable across the reconstruction but not elevated in `## System-level intent`.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The prompt asserted fresh context and no subagents/background agents; this scorer used no subagents or background agents.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; no variance signal is available inside this slot.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Starter-bird auto-selection, evening palette/call quieting, and no avatars during visits were captured inclusively. The missed calls were species-pool size, no welcome toast/banner/modal, and privacy-policy link.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2, S4, and S6 required the most subjective cross-cutting judgment. The plan preserved them broadly, but reconstruction did not always label them as system-level principles.
- **Confabulation cases.** No ungrounded reconstruction cases were found; the reconstruction reads plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several rows that a looser scorer might credit were denied because only the rule survived: F5, F22, F23, F29, F33, and F34.
- **Operational issue.** `TIMING.json` had phase1 and phase2a for run 001 but no completed phase2b entry in this scorer context, so the strict score JSON omits `timing` for the orchestrator to enrich later.
