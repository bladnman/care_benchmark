## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better overall. The plan/reconstruction pair was strongest on server-side simulation, sync, deletion, telemetry, and performance. Affective exceptions leaked more often, especially F8, F23, F29, F37, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight rows were not automatically safe. They often recovered primary mechanism but dropped downstream consequences: F2, F30, F31, and F40 are representative partials.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was much higher than feature-level fidelity. The candidate encoded the product philosophy well, but the blind reconstruction compressed several local whys into mechanism summaries.
- **Multi-layer pattern.** Layer 1 survived most often. Layer 3 was the common loss: what future wrong product the decision prevents.
- **Subdomains.** Accounts/sync, telemetry/privacy, deletion, reduced motion, and server tick were strongest. Social affective whys and starter/onboarding rationale were weakest.
- **Evidence-bound effect.** F1/F13 were captured by plan but explicitly not recovered in reconstruction. S4 was preserved in PLAN but unnamed by B. These are exactly the cases v06 is meant to distinguish.

Planning quality was near-saturated. The plan captured 119 of 120 features. The one miss I found was the explicit privacy-policy link in account settings; the plan has a strong privacy architecture, but not that particular user-facing link. Fidelity losses were mostly feature-level and mostly rationale-level. The reconstruction preserved the architectural spine very well: S7, S8, S9, F17, F18, F25, F35, and F36 were strong. The weaker rows were places where the rule survived without the deeper affective reason: F5, F8, F11, F19, F23, F29, F37, and F39. Functional whys outperformed affective whys. Server-side simulation, sync, deletion, telemetry, and performance were reconstructed more cleanly than affective exceptions such as no public comparison, no catalog-style starter choice, and no show-off visitor rendering. Multi-layer rows usually lost the downstream consequence. Partial recoveries often had layer 1 and sometimes layer 2, but dropped why the mistake would collapse the product later. Examples: F2 missed the Tamagotchi-vs-screensaver failure mode, F30 missed the unlock-economy erosion, and F31 missed the retroactive relationship-erasure consequence. The v06 evidence gate mattered. Several rows would have looked stronger under a looser read because the PLAN had excellent rationale, but the frozen reconstruction either said NOT RECOVERABLE or only reconstructed the mechanism. F1/F13 and S4 are the cleanest examples.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator; it prevented the PLAN's quality from papering over missing frozen-reconstruction rationale.
- Add targeted affective exceptions around social sharing, visitor display, catalog/onboarding, and comparison surfaces.
- Consider requiring reconstruction rows to name plan anchors for each NOT RECOVERABLE statement so scorers can distinguish F1-like and F13-like misses more consistently.
- Keep system-level cross-cutting appendix requirements; they were useful for S4 and S6.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated this as fresh context and did not use prior run memory, peer slots, other waves, or PRD files.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one scored run only; no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture.** Planning capture was inclusive. Feature #87 is the only miss; settle opt-in and visitor actual-aviary were counted captured because the plan encoded enough mechanism to build them, even though their whys scored low.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 and S6 depended on the 3-feature bar and are documented in §2.2.
- **Confabulation.** I found no clear ungrounded reconstruction for scored whys; losses were mostly omission or rule-without-why.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F1, F13, F6, and S4 are the main plan-only or mechanism-only denials.
- **Rule-without-why.** 11 rows were marked rule-without-why; those rows remained in the denominator when reachable.
