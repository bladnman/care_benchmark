## 3. Diagnostic patterns

Planning quality was very high: the plan addressed 115 of 120 features. The missed rows were mostly specification surfaces rather than central product mechanics: glossary, click-anywhere undo specificity, parallax, privacy-policy link, and browser support matrix. Feature 57 was counted captured as a borderline semantic-equivalence call because the plan carried continuous day/night palette shifts and night/settle call quieting, though not warm-evening detail.

Affective and functional recovery were almost even by weighted rate: functional 46.0/54.0 (85.2%) and affective 84.0/98.0 (85.7%). That balance is informative because the plan was not merely an architecture checklist; it carried a lot of affective rationale through audio, accessibility, no-gamification, and scene-load behavior.

System-level intent survived better than feature-level intent. System recovery was 26.0/28.0 (92.9%); feature recovery was 104.0/124.0 (83.9%). The main system leak was S2: no-announcement mechanics survived, but the reconstruction did not fully recover the subtle processed-vs-seen emotional layer.

Weight-3 rows did not clearly outperform weight-2 rows: weight-3 recovered 92.0/108.0 (85.2%) and weight-2 recovered 38.0/44.0 (86.4%). The high-weight losses were mostly partial, not total: F14, F16, F27, F30, and F31 each carried one or two layers while dropping a sharper consequence.

The clearest rule-without-why cases were F5, F11, F34, and F35. In each, the plan and reconstruction preserved the operational mechanism, but the held-out why was not present enough to score recovery under v06.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

Keep the evidence-bound operator. This run is a good example of why: a surface-level score would over-credit several rules that survived as mechanisms but not as rationale.

Add more lifecycle and account-data whys in future instances. Account export and deletion were captured as features, but their relationship-ownership and regret/privacy-residue reasons did not survive.

Consider splitting announcement restraint into mechanical and affective sub-whys. S2 was easy to preserve as a rule cluster but harder to judge for the seen-vs-processed layer.

Keep targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They exposed meaningful differences in adoption, identity, export, deletion, sharing, and narration that the older canonical set would likely have hidden.

## 5. Methodology caveats

Fresh-context fidelity held for this scoring pass: I treated RECONSTRUCTION.md as frozen, did not modify it, and stayed within the user-provided read/write allowlists. I did not read prd/, peer slots, or other waves.

This is a single run, so there is no variance estimate. Model-level claims should wait for wave-level aggregation.

The most subjective calls were S2 and the partial multi-layer feature rows. I applied the strict three-layer rule and only counted layers supported by both reconstruction evidence and PLAN grounding.

Evidence-bound denials affected four rows: F5, F11, F34, and F35. No ungrounded-reconstruction or plan-only-not-reconstructed cases were counted.
