## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered slightly better (36/50 = 72.0%) than affective whys (66/98 = 67.3%). Functional architecture around server ownership, sync, privacy boundaries, and telemetry was especially explicit; affective relationship whys such as F8, F22, F23, F34, and F39 often collapsed into rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys did better (76/104 = 73.1%) than weight-2 whys (26/44 = 59.1%). The plan and reconstruction preserved many architectural multi-layer stories, but lower-weight exceptions were easy to state as prohibitions without the reason.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (71.4%) is close to feature-level fidelity (68.3%). The planner carried the product philosophy, but the reconstructor often compressed it to implementation language.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers usually survived. Secondary calibration or temptation layers often survived when numeric or architectural. Downstream consequence layers were the common loss: examples include S1, S2, F4, F9, F16, F18, and F30.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Sync/server architecture, reduced motion, screen-reader narration, and telemetry did well. Social sharing and relationship-copy rationales leaked: F22, F23, F34, and F39 were mechanisms without the deeper relationship why.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter v06 gate denied credit where a v1 semantic scorer might have been tempted: no-numeric traits (F8), matter-of-fact sync errors (F19), starter birds as arrivals (F29), export as relationship copy (F34), and actual-aviary visiting (F39).

Overall failure shape: the plan is broad and unusually complete, but the frozen reconstruction is better at recovering product rules than recovering why those rules are emotionally or ethically load-bearing.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It separated strong planning coverage from compressed intent recovery, especially on F8, F22, F23, F34, and F39.
- Add more targeted exception whys around social/privacy relationship boundaries. This run implemented most social rules, but several social whys disappeared into generic quietness or access-control language.
- Preserve multi-layer whys for high-stakes architecture, but consider adding explicit downstream-consequence prompts to reconstruction. The most frequent loss was layer 3, not the rule itself.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar, but require scorers to distinguish plan-preserved from reconstruction-identified principles. S3 and S4 were present in the plan but not elevated by B as system intent.
- Maintain a mix of affective and functional whys. Functional whys were not saturated: F16 and F18 still lost deeper layers despite strong implementation details.
