## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 55.6% (30.0/54.0); affective whys recovered at 63.8% (60.0/94.0). Engineering-causal rules such as S7/F17/F36 survived better than relationship-framing rules such as F29, F33, F37, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 57.4% (62.0/108.0), while weight-2 whys recovered at 70.0% (28.0/40.0). Multi-layer whys often kept L1 and lost L3.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System philosophy was strong at 85.7%; feature why recovery dropped to 55.0%. The plan carried enough global language for B to reconstruct principles, but local rationales compressed into implementation bullets.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes usually survived; secondary traps and downstream product-collapse consequences were most often dropped. Examples: F2 lost Tamagotchi-vs-screensaver calibration, F18 lost the cross-device overwrite story, and F40 lost same-rhythm-as-visual framing.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and accessibility were stronger where the plan spelled out architecture and product quality. Social and headroom onboarding/export/deletion whys were more brittle because the plan treated them as endpoint or policy bullets.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Nine included whys were rule-without-why cases. These would look better under loose semantic scoring, but v06 denied recovery where the frozen reconstruction only had the operational rule.

The failure shape suggests a planner that can enumerate and architect the product well, but often compresses the reason for non-obvious constraints into generic quiet/no-gamification/no-social-network language.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the F29-F40 headroom additions. They exposed several mechanism-only recoveries that the original canonical list would undercount.
- Ask phase-2A reconstructors to label per-layer evidence for multi-layer whys. This would reduce scorer inference on whether an L2 or L3 layer was really recovered.
- Keep `rule_without_why` as a first-class report field. It was the dominant diagnostic for this run and clarified why high planning did not imply high fidelity.
- Clarify capture guidance for implicit exclusions such as no top-bar chrome, quiet loading, and visit default-off behavior; these borderline calls are easy to vary across scorers.
- Consider adding more affective single-layer exceptions in social/account areas, where implementation rules are easy to plan and product reasons are easy to drop.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scorer used only the allowlisted phase_two files, assigned PLAN/metadata/timing slice, frozen RECONSTRUCTION, and scoring output paths. No PRD, peer slots, other waves, memory, or subagents were used.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; no variance signal is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls were made for features 7, 27, 47, 51, 55, 57, 63, 67, 90, and 97. These affect planning quality, but fidelity still required exact evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2 and S6 were partial because they missed downstream layers despite broad plan inheritance. Other system whys met the three-feature cross-cutting bar.
- **Confabulation cases.** No included recovery was scored from ungrounded reconstruction; ungrounded_reconstruction_count is 0.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F16, F23, F29, F30, F33, F34, F35, F37, and F39 were denied why recovery because only the rule or endpoint survived.
