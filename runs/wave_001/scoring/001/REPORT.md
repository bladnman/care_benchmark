## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 70.4% weighted recovery, versus 54.1% for affective whys. Server-side simulation, no-last-write-wins, telemetry boundaries, and presence mechanics traveled well; anti-gamification, no-leaderboard, no-show-off, and no-number relationship rationales leaked.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 63.0%, slightly above weight-2 whys at 52.3%. High-weight functional architecture helped, but high-weight affective exceptions such as F4, F14, and F27 still failed.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (82.1%) was much higher than feature-level fidelity (54.8%). The plan preserved cross-cutting philosophy, but the reconstruction often compressed feature-level reasons into implementation rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary implementation layers were most likely to survive. Secondary and downstream layers about failure modes, user relationship shifts, and future-product drift were usually the first to disappear.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and accessibility were strongest; social and anti-gamification were weaker because the reconstruction kept the prohibitions but dropped the relationship-protection rationale. Audio split: synthesis mechanics survived, but the looped-audio/affective-spine why for F4 did not.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** V06 denied several plausible v1-style recoveries: F6, F8, F19, F22, F23, F26, F33, and F39 all had enough rule capture to build the right surface, but not enough frozen-reconstruction rationale to score recovery.

The failure shape suggests a strong implementation planner whose plan is good enough to build from, but whose rationale encoding is uneven: operational and privacy mechanics are durable, while affective product philosophy often survives only as rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They exposed exactly the distinction the benchmark is meant to measure: rules can be present while the reason is not.
- Add more affective exception whys around social/private relationship boundaries. F23, F37-F39 separated private-sharing intent from ordinary social-product rules.
- Consider a report-side flag for system-level plan-only partial credit. S1 and S4 received partial credit from plan preservation even though reconstruction evidence was absent; that is useful but easy to misread.
- Preserve the rule_without_why diagnostic. In this run it identified 16 cases and explained most of the gap between planning quality and fidelity.
- Keep multi-layer whys concentrated on high-risk design ideas. They made it visible that primary implementation rules survived more often than downstream consequences.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring turn began from fresh context and read only the allowed phase-two package, assigned plan/metadata, run-001 timing, frozen reconstruction, and scoring slot. I did not read prd/, peer slots, or other waves.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run, so there is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I counted feature 7 as captured because the plan defines the domain concepts substantively, and feature 68 as captured but borderline because the plan references quiet empty-state handling without expanding it. The only non-captured feature was 87, the privacy-policy link.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The cross-cutting judgment was most subjective for S1 and S4; in both cases the plan preserved the principle through multiple decisions, but B did not recover the full rationale.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not identify ungrounded reconstruction claims that warranted the confabulation guard. The reconstruction reads closely plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Many rows had mechanism capture but no rationale evidence, especially F4, F6, F8, F14, F19, F22, F23, F26, F27, F33, and F39.
- **Operational timing.** TIMING.json contained phase 1 and phase 2A data for run 001, but no phase 2B timing entry at scoring time; I did not fabricate one.
