## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was stronger: 38.0/54.0 weighted points (70.4%) versus affective recovery at 58.0/92.0 (63.0%). Concrete architecture made S7, F17, and F36 sturdy; affective exceptions such as F29, F33, F38, and F39 became rule-only or absent.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 70.0/104.0 (67.3%), modestly better than weight-2 whys at 26.0/42.0 (61.9%). High-weight whys were often partially recovered because the primary mechanism survived while secondary calibration or downstream consequence was dropped.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (85.7%) was much higher than feature-level fidelity (61.0%). The reconstruction could infer product-wide architecture and accessibility posture, but feature-local rationales were more brittle.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Secondary layers were the common loss point: F2 lost the one-week-instrument/three-week-user distinction; F13 lost individual signal failure modes; F18 lost the silent overwrite example; F31 lost identity-vs-vector distinction.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Bird engine, sync architecture, accessibility, and telemetry boundaries were strongest. Social/account settings and quiet relationship-right features were weakest: F34/F35/F38/F39 all landed at none despite captured endpoints.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Ten included whys were marked `rule_without_why`: F9, F16, F22, F23, F29, F33, F34, F35, F38, and F39. These are the clearest places where the implementation plan would let a builder build the surface while losing the product reason.

What the failure shape suggests: the candidate is a strong implementation planner that preserves major architecture and many product constraints, but it compresses the quieter affective/account/social rationales into endpoint lists.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted feature-level headroom. F29-F40 are doing real work here: without them, this run would look more saturated than it is.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for calibration and exception cases. The candidate often retained layer 1 while losing layer 2 or 3, which is exactly what the operator is meant to expose.
- Add or retain quiet account/social rights in future instances. Account export, deletion, visit logs, and visitor presentation created useful discrimination without requiring obscure implementation knowledge.
- Consider splitting S5 in future gold lists. Naturalist voice and matter-of-fact system exception behaved differently in this run; scoring them separately may improve consistency.
- Keep the strict system cross-cutting appendix. S2, S4, and S5 depended on the three-feature bar and benefited from an explicit trail of PLAN inheritance.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scorer used only the allowlisted phase-two files, assigned plan, candidate metadata, assigned timing entry, and frozen reconstruction. No PRD or peer slots were read.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run, so there is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive for settle lighting (#43), bird-chosen perch (#55), evening palette shift (#57), no friend-visited notification (#94), actual visitor aviary (#97), and call-caption toggle (#104). These calls mostly affect planning quality, not the main fidelity pattern.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2, S4, and S5 were the subjective cases. I awarded partial where the plan carried enough cross-cutting mechanics but the reconstruction or plan did not preserve the full gold rationale.
- **Confabulation cases.** No material ungrounded reconstruction was credited. A few broad phrases were treated conservatively when PLAN grounding was insufficient.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several v1-style apparent wins became none because the reconstruction preserved an endpoint but not the why, especially F29, F33, F34, F35, F38, and F39.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** The ten cases listed in section 3 are the central diagnostic for this run.
