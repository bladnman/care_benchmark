## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was **42.0/54.0** and affective recovery was **77.0/98.0**, so neither type collapsed. Misses concentrated where relationship framing mattered more than mechanism: F23, F34, and parts of S2/F10.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered **82.0/108.0**; weight-2 whys recovered **37.0/44.0**. Many high-weight items were partial because layer 1 survived while downstream consequences were compressed.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved philosophy better than specific whys: system-level fidelity was **89.3%**, feature-level fidelity was **75.8%**.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary causes usually survived. Secondary and downstream layers were common losses: S2, F4, F30, and F31 are representative.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, privacy, accessibility, and performance were strongest. Social/account surfaces were weaker when the gold why was relational rather than technical.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F19, F23, and F34 were denied recovery because the reconstruction preserved the rule but not the gold rationale.

What this suggests: the plan is excellent at preserving product scope and mechanics, and the reconstruction is honest, but dense implementation prose still loses some non-obvious relationship whys unless the plan states them as explicit rationale.
