## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered better: 78/98 vs functional 36/54. Functional misses clustered around precision/failure mechanics in F1, F13, F16, and F18.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-2 whys were strong (86.4%), while weight-3 whys exposed compression (70.4%). Primary causes survived more often than secondary and downstream consequences.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The plan preserved philosophy very well, and the reconstruction recovered most system-level whys. Feature-level fidelity fell because several targeted exceptions were present as build rules without the gold rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Missing layers were usually downstream consequence or secondary failure-mode examples: F2 lost Tamagotchi-vs-screensaver calibration, F9 lost the generic-arrival-animation failure mode, F16 lost the retrofit rationale, and F35 lost the accidental-regret layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio, accessibility, privacy, and server authority were strong. Presence accounting and sync correctness were implemented well but reconstructed with less of the rationale detail. Social/anti-gamification had strong rules but weaker relationship-comparison rationale in F23.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F6, F23, and F29 would look good under a pure feature-capture lens, but v06 correctly denies why credit because the reconstruction does not carry the specific gold rationale.

The failure shape suggests a high-capability planner that builds nearly everything, paired with a reconstruction that summarizes intent competently but loses the tail of multi-layer reasons.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted headroom additions. F29-F40 caught meaningful compression that the original 28 feature whys would not isolate as clearly.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for weight-3 whys. This run shows why: the first layer often survives, while the rationale that prevents future product drift is exactly what gets compressed.
- Add a few more functional whys that include concrete failure modes. Presence, sync, privacy, and deletion produced the clearest discrimination between build-rule capture and why recovery.
- Retain the system cross-cutting bar, but keep requiring the evidence appendix. S6 shows how easy it is for a principle to be preserved by the plan while the reconstruction names only one facet of it.
- Consider one more category for "alternate rationale present" if future analyses want to distinguish F6/F23-style wrong-rationale rows from pure absence. For scoring, the current no-credit rule is right.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not edit it. No contamination signs were found in the validity audit.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one sample; it gives no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Feature 7 was the only mild borderline: the plan lacks a standalone glossary but defines the domain model terms well enough for a builder. Counting it captured does not materially alter the result.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The subjective call is S6. The PLAN preserves precise presence accounting cross-cuttingly, but the reconstruction foregrounds non-punishment more than the full idle-attention definition, so S6 is partial.
- **Confabulation cases.** None counted. Some rows use alternate/shallower rationale, but I did not find ungrounded reconstruction claims that should trigger the confabulation guard.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The strict evidence gate denied F6, F23, and F29, and reduced many multi-layer whys to partial where only the primary rule survived.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Three included whys are marked rule-without-why: F6, F23, and F29.
