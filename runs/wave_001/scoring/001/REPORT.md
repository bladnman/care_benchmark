## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys were stronger: server-side tick (S7/F17), no-LWW (F18), privacy telemetry (S8/F20/F36), and presence precision (S6/F1/F13) were almost fully recovered. Affective leaks clustered in relationship-framing exceptions: F29 starter birds as arrivals, F34 export as relationship copy, and F39 no show-off mode.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys mostly survived because the plan repeated them in architecture, risks, and CI gates. Partial weight-3 losses were F14, F16, F30, and F40, where a primary mechanism survived but a secondary temptation or downstream consequence was compressed away.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The plan preserved philosophy very well. The reconstruction recovered system-level intent at 96.4%, but feature-level fidelity fell to 83.9% because several local exceptions were recorded as rules without their deeper product rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Layer 1 was nearly always recovered. Lost layers were usually L2 or L3: intention rotation for streaks (F14), retrofit impossibility for synthetic IDs (F16), unlock-economy erosion (F30), and screen-reader queue overload (F40).
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, privacy, audio, and accessibility were strong. Social was mostly strong but missed the visitor actual-aviary/show-off rationale. Account lifecycle captured operations but lost export/deletion whys.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F11, F29, F34, F35, and F39 were denied recovery because the reconstruction preserved only the operational rule or explicitly said NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN.

The failure shape suggests an excellent implementation plan that still compresses a few quiet affective rationales when moving from plan to reconstruction.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound ledger. It distinguished 100.0% planning from 86.2% intent fidelity without relying on qualitative scoring prose.
- Add more targeted headroom around quiet exception rationales. F29, F34, and F39 show that strong plans preserve rules but can drop why the rule protects the relationship.
- Make downstream-consequence layers more explicit in future gold entries or scorer examples. Partial losses most often dropped the long-term product-shape consequence rather than the implementation mechanism.
- Clarify S4-like cases in the rubric: technical minimalism is not automatically product restraint. This run needed a partial call because the PLAN had product restraint but the reconstruction emphasized engineering simplicity.
- Keep a mix of functional and affective whys. Functional whys did not saturate because privacy/presence/sync still test real architecture, while affective whys expose relationship-design compression.

- **Confabulation cases.** No ungrounded reconstruction confabulations were found.
