## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 72.2% (39.0/54), while affective whys recovered at 65.3% (64.0/98). The strongest functional recoveries were S6/S7, F1/F2/F13/F17, and F36. The weakest affective areas were relationship-framing exceptions such as F29, F33, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 74.1% (80.0/108), ahead of weight-2 whys at 52.3% (23.0/44). The plan and reconstruction carried high-load-bearing architecture better than small single-layer product exceptions.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (89.3%) substantially exceeded feature-level fidelity (62.9%). B reconstructed broad principles like server authority, anti-gamification, presence precision, and accessibility-as-real-surface, but often dropped why detail for individual features.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanisms usually survived. Secondary temptation layers and downstream product-shift consequences were the common losses: examples include F4 chorus/artifact and affective-spine cascade, F16 retrofit/log-leak consequence, F18 silent LWW drift deletion, and F40 screen-reader queue overwhelm.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, and accessibility had the strongest recovery. Social sharing and account lifecycle surfaces were weaker: F23, F34, F35, F38, and F39 preserved rules but not the relationship-level rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The v06 operator denied credit for mechanism-only rows including F5, F8, F19, F23, F29, F33, F34, F35, F38, and F39. These would look healthier under a rule-capture-only read, but the frozen reconstruction did not carry the why.

What the failure shape suggests: the candidate plan is operationally thorough and strong on architecture, but it encodes many affective exceptions as constraints rather than as reasons. A downstream agent can rebuild the product shape, but the subtler relationship logic leaks first.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It exposed a meaningful gap between captured implementation rules and recovered product intent.
- Add or retain targeted affective headroom rows like F29, F33, F38, and F39. They are useful because strong planners often implement them while dropping the rationale.
- Ask planners to include an explicit rationale column for non-goals, social/privacy/account lifecycle features, and accessibility surfaces. The current plan captured those features, but B often had only the rule to reconstruct.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting bar, but add calibration examples for cases like S3 and S4 where a principle is distributed across many small design choices and easy for B to compress into a neighboring principle.
- Consider adding a diagnostic for "rule-rich, why-thin" plans, since this run shows high planning quality can coexist with much lower feature-level fidelity.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The provided prompt and frozen artifacts indicate a fresh phase-2A reconstruction. The validity audit found no gold IDs, no rubric vocabulary, and headings that mirror the PLAN rather than the gold list.
