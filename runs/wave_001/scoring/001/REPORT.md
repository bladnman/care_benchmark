## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered **19.0 / 50.0** weighted points (38.0%); affective whys recovered **33.0 / 90.0** (36.7%). The plan preserved enough system architecture to help functional whys, but privacy/identity/account whys still often collapsed to rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered **42.0 / 100.0** (42.0%), while weight-2 whys recovered **10.0 / 40.0** (25.0%). The heavier whys survived better because they were more often named in architectural sections, but most lost secondary/downstream layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (**64.3%**) is much higher than feature-level fidelity (**30.4%**). The planner encoded philosophy and architecture more reliably than feature-specific rationales.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes survived most often: presence precision, slow drift, server tick, procedural calls, and accessibility surfaces. Secondary and downstream consequences were the frequent loss: e.g. S2 loses processed-vs-seen and one-toast cascade; F17 loses the divergent-client failure story.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio and accessibility were the strongest pockets (F4 full; S9 full; F24/F25/F40 partial). Social, account/privacy, and interaction-detail whys were weak: F21-F23, F36-F39, F10, F14, and F27 mostly preserved prohibitions but not the relationship reasons.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Many v1-style apparent recoveries were denied because the reconstruction only repeated a mechanism. Examples: F8 hidden vectors, F16 synthetic account IDs, F19 matter-of-fact sync errors, F26 first-bird budget, and F37 per-invite sharing.
- **Borderline calls.** I leaned inclusive on a few capture calls (#24 naming/renameability, #40 offers, #57 evening palette, #83 export, #85 telemetry/ML, #97 actual visitor snapshot). These lifted planning quality slightly but did not inflate fidelity unless the frozen reconstruction carried why evidence.

What this suggests: the plan is a capable implementation compression, but not a strong rationale carrier. It keeps the product buildable; it does not consistently make the downstream builder understand why the constraints exist.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated this run's high rule capture from low why recovery; without it, mechanism-heavy rows like F8, F16, F19, and F37 would look better than they are.
- Add more targeted feature-level exceptions like F29-F40. They exposed headroom: the plan implemented age-gating, stable IDs, actual visitor snapshots, and aggregate telemetry, but rarely carried the reason.
- Keep multi-layer whys for high-risk product philosophy. The secondary/downstream layers did most of the discrimination here, especially for notice-never-announce, presence honesty, and server-side simulation.
- Consider making the phase-1 prompt ask for rationale preservation explicitly only if the benchmark wants less saturation. This run shows a strong planner can build most features while dropping many whys.
- Keep the system cross-cutting bar, but require scorers to record layer loss. Several system whys were cross-cutting yet only partial because the plan preserved a principle without the deeper consequence.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The task specified fresh context and no subagents. I used only the listed allowlist materials plus phase-two templates/constants. I did not read PRD files, peer slots, or other waves.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; there is no variance signal. The result should be read as one point in wave_001.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive capture calls affected planning quality but not most fidelity scores because the frozen reconstruction still needed why evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The strict >=3 inherited decisions bar was applied through the appendix in section 2.2. S3 is the hardest call: the plan has specificity-adjacent decisions, but the reconstruction does not identify the charm principle directly.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not mark clear contamination-style confabulation. The closest scoring issue is F30, where the reconstruction gives a metrics/ramp-control why grounded in the rollout text but not matching the gold anti-reward-loop rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The largest denominator losses are rule-without-why cases, not ungrounded reconstructions. The candidate often says what to build, not why the rule matters.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There are 21 included rule-without-why cases, concentrated in social/account/privacy exclusions, hidden values, gamification refusals, and performance/account surfaces.
