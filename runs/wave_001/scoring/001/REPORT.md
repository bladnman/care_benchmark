## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys survived much better: 40.0/54.0 weighted points (74.1%) versus 43.0/98.0 affective points (43.9%). The plan and reconstruction were strongest when the why was architectural or operational, such as S7, F17, F18, F20, F26, and F36. Affective exceptions frequently collapsed into rules: F8, F14, F22, F23, F27, F29, F33, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys did better overall (66.0/108.0, 61.1%) than weight-2 whys (17.0/44.0, 38.6%) because the high-weight functional architecture was repeatedly reinforced. The exception is affective weight-3 headroom: F27, F30, F31, and F35 were all captured as features but lost their deeper whys.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The system-level layer was strong at 82.1%; the feature-level layer was 48.4%. The candidate preserved the philosophy in broad strokes but often failed to encode why individual constraints exist.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers survived most often because they are close to the rule. Secondary and downstream consequence layers were the main losses, especially for S2, F1, F4, F9, F10, F13, F14, F27, F30, F31, and F35.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and privacy were strongest: S7, S8, F17, F18, F20, and F36 recovered well. Social-option and targeted-headroom features were weakest: F22, F23, F37, F38, and F39 all kept mechanics while losing the relationship/control rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied credit for many plausible v1-style recoveries because the reconstruction gave only the operational rule. The most important rule-without-why rows were F8, F14, F19, F22, F23, F27, F29, F30, F31, F33, F34, F35, F37, F38, and F39.

The failure shape suggests that an implementation-heavy plan can be excellent at capture while still compressing away product intent. The reconstructed plan would probably build most of Pocket Aviary, but a future maintainer would have weaker protection against small, reasonable-sounding changes that alter the product relationship.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It usefully separated feature capture (111/120) from why recovery (83/152 weighted), exposing rule-only preservation that a looser scorer would over-credit.
- Add more targeted affective exception whys like F29-F40. Those rows created useful headroom: the candidate often planned the feature but not the deeper reason.
- Consider making consequence-layer evidence easier to audit. Many losses occurred on downstream layers; requiring each multi-layer why to carry a short failure-mode clause in the plan would improve later reconstruction.
- Preserve the strict system cross-cutting bar, but ask scorers to list inheritance evidence as this report does. It made S2/S6 partial calls concrete rather than vibe-based.
- For future instances, include at least one explicit social/privacy/control cluster. The social rows were a high-signal area where mechanics were easy and intent was easy to lose.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scoring task was run from fresh context for this phase, and the frozen reconstruction was not modified. I saw no evidence that the reconstructor had access to gold IDs or the rubric.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run for candidate `gpt-5.4` at high effort. There is no variance estimate from this score alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on 11 feature-capture calls: #12, #15, #25, #37, #43, #44, #52, #85, #94, #97, and a few nearby semantic-compression cases. Even if a stricter scorer removed several, planning quality would remain high; feature fidelity is the main diagnostic.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The most subjective calls were S1 full, S2 partial, S3 partial, and S6 partial. The appendix lists the PLAN places used for the 3-feature inheritance bar.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not count any clear ungrounded reconstruction as recovered. F30 is a wrong-rationale case, but its calibration/performance rationale is grounded in the plan rather than invented.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Single-layer rows with only mechanism evidence were scored none even when the implemented rule was exactly right, including F8, F19, F22, F23, F29, F33, F34, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 18 included rule-without-why cases. This is the run’s main failure mode: the plan is buildable, but the rationale is not consistently portable.
- **Timing.** `TIMING.json` supplied phase1 and phase2a timings for run 001 only; phase2b timing was not available in-context and was not fabricated.
