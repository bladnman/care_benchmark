## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was slightly stronger (32.0/54.0 = 59.3%) than affective recovery (51.0/92.0 = 55.4%). Architecture-shaped functional whys such as S7, F17, and F18 survived especially well; relationship-tone whys such as F22, F23, F37, F38, and F39 often collapsed to operational refusals.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys survived much better (70.0/104.0 = 67.3%) than weight-2 whys (13.0/42.0 = 31.0%). The plan and reconstruction were strongest where the PRD's rationale was architecture-bearing and repeated.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (75.0%) outpaced feature-level fidelity (52.5%). The reconstruction recovered broad philosophies like server-owned state, privacy boundaries, and first-class accessibility, while many narrow feature exceptions became rules without the why.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes survived most often. Secondary contributor layers were the first to drop, especially the anti-gamification temptation stories (F14, F27, F30) and user-intention reframing. Downstream consequence layers survived when tied to architecture (F17/F18) and leaked when tied to affective relationship framing.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Sync/server architecture and accessibility were strongest. Social optionality and account lifecycle were weakest: export, deletion, visit log, per-invite sharing, and show-off-mode refusals were implemented or implied but not reconstructed with their relationship rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter v06 evidence gate denied likely v1-style semantic credit for several rows: F8, F16, F19, F23, F29, F30, F32-F35, and F37-F39 preserved mechanisms but not recoverable gold whys. No ungrounded reconstruction cases were found.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add targeted feature-level whys for quiet social/account surfaces in future instances. This run shows that operational privacy and sharing rules are easy to plan, but the relationship rationale behind export, deletion, visit logs, and actual-aviary visits is easy to compress away.
- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It sharply separated strong planning from faithful intent recovery, especially for F8, F16, and F30 where a plausible rule existed without the actual why.
- Consider adding more explicit capture guidance for meta-doc features such as glossary, palette spec, and browser support. They affect planning quality but are not symmetric with product behavior features, which makes the capture decision noisier.
- Preserve multi-layer whys for architecture-bearing items. F17 and F18 show that when all layers are implemented and narrated, the rubric can award full credit cleanly; the same structure exposed missing layers in F1, F3, F13, F14, F20, F27, and F40.
- Revisit whether system-level S2 should include a named no-welcome-toast anchor in the capture table. In this run the broad quiet-surface philosophy survived while the canonical welcome-surface feature did not, and that distinction is useful.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The reconstruction was already frozen before scoring. I did not modify it and did not read PRD files or peer slots.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run at the supplied candidate settings; no variance estimate is available from this artifact alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on several compressed implementation captures: no-panning via fixed viewport anchors, evening/night layout details, visit-avatar/chat absences via broader social-network refusal, and actual-aviary visitor view via the shared snapshot route. These calls raised planning quality but did not rescue fidelity without reconstructed why evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The cross-cutting bar was easiest for S1, S7, S8, and S9, and most subjective for S3-S5 where product voice and restraint were present but reconstructed unevenly.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no material ungrounded reconstruction. Some reconstructed whys were simply different from the gold target, especially F30, but they were grounded in the plan rather than invented.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Sixteen included feature whys were marked rule-without-why. The plan often carried the operational instruction strongly while the frozen reconstruction either wrote NOT RECOVERABLE or described only the mechanism.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing data contained phase 1 and phase 2A only for this slot; phase 2B timing was omitted from the score JSON rather than fabricated.
