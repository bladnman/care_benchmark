## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered **34.0 / 54.0** weight (63.0%), while affective whys recovered **49.0 / 98.0** (50.0%). Server-side continuity, LWW refusal, and technical privacy boundaries survived better than relationship-framing whys.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys recovered **58.0 / 108.0** (53.7%); weight-2 whys recovered **25.0 / 44.0** (56.8%). Multi-layer high-weight whys often lost secondary/downstream layers even when the primary rule survived.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (**82.1%**) was much higher than feature-level fidelity (**48.4%**). The plan encoded a coherent philosophy, but the reconstruction often compressed feature whys into mechanisms.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** L1 operational causes survived most often. L2/L3 losses include F4's chorus artifact, F10's bird-greeting-as-entire-welcome, F14's presence-for-birds-not-counter, and F27's foothold/product-erosion layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and simulation were strongest. Social and relationship surfaces were weaker: F23, F33, F39, and F31 are examples where the product should avoid comparison, curation, show-off mode, or identity breaks, but the why did not survive.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several rows were denied despite clear implementation rules: F8, F10, F14, F23, F29, F31, F33, F34, F35, and F39. These are exactly the rule-without-why cases v06 is meant to expose.

The failure shape suggests a strong implementer plan with a compressed reconstruction: what to build remained unusually complete, while non-obvious affective rationales leaked first.

- **Confabulation cases.** I found no clear ungrounded reconstruction assertions that required a confabulation penalty beyond ordinary evidence denial.
