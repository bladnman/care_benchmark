## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was 40.0/54.0; affective recovery was 76.0/98.0. Functional losses came mostly from missing downstream examples in F1, F2, F7, F16, F18, and F35. Affective losses came from targeted headroom rows where the plan or reconstruction kept the rule but not the relationship reason: F29, F38, F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 78.0/108.0, lower than weight-2 at 38.0/44.0. The candidate encoded primary causes well, but secondary contributors and downstream consequences were often compressed away.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System fidelity was much higher than feature fidelity. The plan's top-level product stance survived, but detailed feature whys like F10, F12, F14, F27, F30, and F31 lost one or more rationale layers.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Layer 1 was usually preserved. Layer 2 and Layer 3 were the common leak points, especially failure-mode examples and future-product consequences.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accessibility and privacy were strong. Social headroom was mixed: quiet visits were captured, but visit-log transparency and actual-aviary/no-show-off rationale were not recovered. Audio recovered procedural/no-loop intent but missed the chorus artifact layer.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F29 and F38 were forced to none because B wrote NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN. F39 stayed none because the reconstruction preserved the same-state visitor rule without the no-show-off rationale.

The failure shape suggests that this planner/reconstructor pair is excellent at preserving architecture and product invariants, but weaker at preserving the non-obvious affective reasons behind small exception rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted feature-level headroom additions. F29, F38, and F39 separated a strong plan from a full why-preserving plan.
- Preserve multi-layer whys, but make phase-2A output ask for primary, secondary, and downstream layers explicitly. This run often recovered the primary rule but not the consequence.
- Add more social/privacy exception whys. They exposed compression better than broad engine architecture, which this candidate handled well.
- Clarify S2 scoring examples. This run preserved no-announcement mechanics but not the processed-vs-seen distinction, making it a useful partial case.
- Keep evidence-bound scoring strict. It prevented plan-only or rule-only recoveries from inflating feature-level fidelity.
