## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was stronger (44.0/54.0) than affective recovery (71.0/98.0). The planner and reconstructor preserved architectural whys around S7, F16, F17, F20, and F36 better than subtle relationship whys such as F8, F23, F34, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 rows recovered 86.0/108.0, better than weight-2 rows at 29.0/44.0. Load-bearing rows survived better, but often partially rather than fully when downstream consequences were compressed.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was near-perfect at 27.0/28.0. Feature-level fidelity dropped to 88.0/124.0 because exact feature exceptions were often represented as implementation rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary implementation causes usually survived; secondary examples and downstream product-shift consequences were most often lost. F18, F30, F31, and F40 show that pattern clearly.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync/privacy and core simulation scored strongly. Social and relationship/ownership whys lost more: no leaderboard comparison, actual visitor witness, and export as quiet relationship copy were not recovered.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Fourteen rows were rule-without-why and fourteen had at least one plan-only layer. No ungrounded reconstruction was scored; when B over-compressed, it usually stayed conservative rather than confabulating.

What this suggests: the candidate plan is excellent as an implementation artifact, but the plan-to-reconstruction channel favors mechanisms, test gates, and architecture over non-obvious affective rationales.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound ledger. It prevented rule-heavy rows such as F8, F23, F34, and F39 from receiving credit for merely naming the feature.
- Add more targeted headroom whys around relationship semantics. This run saturated capture, so discrimination came from non-obvious reasons like starter birds as arrivals and export as relationship copy.
- Keep multi-layer whys for high-value features. The partials were informative: F7, F18, F30, F31, and F35 were not the same kind of miss.
- Clarify system-level restraint scoring. S4 was preserved in the plan but not named by B; v2 could give scorers more examples for structurally preserved but unnamed system principles.
- Consider adding a small audit field for phase-2A marker vocabulary such as NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN, so validity auditors do not confuse expected reconstruction notation with rubric leakage.

- **Borderline capture calls.** None changed the planning-quality number. All 120 canonical features were explicitly addressed by the plan.
