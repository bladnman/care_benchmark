## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys did better (53.7%) than affective whys (42.7%). Server authority, no LWW, synthetic IDs, telemetry boundary, and drift calibration survived more cleanly than relationship-protecting rationales such as no leaderboards, visit logging, no welcome toast, and starter-bird non-catalog framing.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys recovered better (54.0%) than weight-2 whys (27.8%). The candidate plan tended to preserve major architectural mechanisms and first-order product principles while compressing smaller exception rationales.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (64.3%) is much stronger than feature-level fidelity (42.6%). The plan transmits the product's broad posture, but the frozen reconstruction often cannot recover why a particular constraint exists.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers were most likely to survive. Secondary temptation/failure-mode layers and downstream consequences were frequently dropped, especially F10, F14, F23, F30, F31, F35, and F40.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync scored strongest because the plan was concrete about server ownership, append-only events, and no client vector writes. Social and relationship-protecting affordances were weakest: the plan captured several exclusions, but not the deeper comparison, attention-loop, and privacy-as-relationship rationales.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several apparent recoveries were denied because they were mechanism-only: F6, F9, F19, F21-F23, F26, F32-F35, and F37-F39. F33 is the clearest plan-only gap: the plan says the notebook is read-only, but the reconstruction does not recover the observer-record rationale.

What this failure shape suggests: the candidate planner is good at broad implementation coverage and hard technical invariants, but compresses affective intent into labels like naturalist, quiet, or felt-aliveness. Those labels are useful, but not enough for a fresh reconstructor to recover the specific anti-pattern each feature is guarding against.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It usefully separates feature capture from why recovery; this run would look much stronger under mechanism-only scoring than it should.
- Add or preserve more targeted affective exception whys. The clearest losses were around no welcome toast, no streak/calendar surfaces, social visits, no leaderboards, starter birds as arrivals, and read-only notebook intent.
- Consider adding a scorer-facing subdomain summary by PRD file. This run shows accounts/sync and performance surviving differently from social/accessibility/interaction whys, and that split is useful for benchmark diagnosis.
- Keep multi-layer whys for high-value features. They exposed that primary rules often survived while secondary temptation and downstream consequence layers disappeared.
- The >=3 cross-cutting bar was workable but subjective for S2, S4, and S8. Future rubrics could ask scorers to distinguish cross-cutting mechanism inheritance from cross-cutting rationale inheritance.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scorer used only the phase-two materials, assigned plan, assigned metadata/timing, and frozen reconstruction. The reconstruction was not modified.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one candidate plan and one reconstruction; no variance estimate is available from this slot alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive where a downstream builder would likely build the right surface from the plan, but flagged thin cases such as features 21, 55, 57, 63, 70, 83, 85, 90, 92, 97, 103, 104, and 114.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2, S4, and S8 were the subjective calls. S2 and S4 preserve mechanisms without the full rationale; S8 has a technical privacy boundary but not enough relationship-data inheritance for the full cross-cutting bar.
- **Confabulation cases.** No score-bearing reconstruction rationale was credited without PLAN grounding. Some broad summaries sounded stronger than the plan, but they were either grounded or scored conservatively.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Mechanism-only recoveries drove many denials. This is especially visible in social, notebook, performance, and account lifecycle features.
- **Operational timing.** The timing ledger contained phase 1 and phase 2A for run 001, but no phase 2B entry at scoring time; the score JSON includes only available bounded timing fields.
