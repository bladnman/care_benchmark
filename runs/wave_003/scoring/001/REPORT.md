## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys did better: 32.0 / 54.0 weighted points (59.3%) versus affective whys at 47.0 / 92.0 (51.1%). The reconstruction handled server-side tick, sync, presence, and telemetry boundaries more strongly than relationship-tone rationales such as F29, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 52.0 / 104.0 (50.0%), worse than weight-2 whys at 27.0 / 42.0 (64.3%). The high-weight rows often had downstream-consequence layers that the reconstruction compressed away.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was high at 82.1%, but feature-level fidelity was 47.5%. The planner preserved philosophy, but the reconstruction often stopped at implementation mechanics for individual features.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes survived most often. Secondary contributors survived when they were architectural (S7, F17) and leaked when affective (F30, F31). Downstream consequences were the most commonly dropped layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and telemetry did well when they were technical (S7, S8, F17, F36). Social and sharing rules were mostly captured but thinly rationalized (F37-F39). Accessibility was mixed: S9 and F25 were strong, while F40 kept cadence without the screen-reader queue rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The clearest rule-without-why cluster was F29-F40: many targeted headroom features were built or named but not reconstructed as rationale.

What this suggests: this plan was a strong implementation compression of the PRD, but the blind reconstruction could not always recover the product-intent explanation from those implementation rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom whys like F29-F40; they prevented saturation by distinguishing building the feature from preserving its reason.
- Keep multi-layer scoring, but consider explicitly reporting per-layer drop rates in the JSON-safe aggregate layer in a future schema. This run's most important diagnostic is downstream-layer loss.
- Clarify capture standards for named exclusions and exact constants. No welcome toast, no friend-visited notification, 30-day deletion grace, and no-panning were the hardest capture calls.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting bar. It exposed useful partials for S1/S2/S4 where a broad design principle survived but some specific consequences did not.
- Add more affective exceptions in future products. The technical system was easier to reconstruct than relationship-preserving product choices.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The prompt states fresh phase-2B context. The reconstruction shows no obvious gold-ID or rubric-vocabulary leakage.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run for candidate gpt-5.4; no variance signal is available here.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on product principle #3, bird naming, settle lighting, evening palette, account deletion grace, caption mood text, and tick-latency alarms. The score would drop modestly if those were treated more strictly.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S1/S2/S4 were the subjective cases; see the cross-cutting appendix above.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no material ungrounded reconstruction cases. The reconstruction was plan-derived and sometimes under-inferred, rather than inventing unsupported rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F4, F19, F29-F31, F33-F35, and F37-F40 are the main cases where a v1-style rule match might look good, but v06 denied why credit.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 13 included cases. Most are targeted headroom social/account/accessibility features where the implementation rule survived without the relationship rationale.
