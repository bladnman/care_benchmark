## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery is stronger (42.0/54.0) than affective recovery (68.0/98.0). The plan/reconstruction were especially strong on sync, drift, server ownership, and telemetry boundaries, while social and relationship-framing whys such as F23, F29, F33, F38, and F39 often collapsed to rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered much better (88.0/108.0) than weight-2 whys (22.0/44.0). The high-weight architecture and drift rationales were prominent in the plan; smaller exception rationales were easier to omit.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (85.7%) outpaced feature-level fidelity (69.4%). The plan transmitted broad philosophy well, but exact feature exception whys were often compressed away.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary operational causes usually survived. Secondary affective causes and downstream relationship consequences leaked most often, especially in S2, F14, F20, F30, and F35.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync/audio/accessibility recovered strongly. Social-option whys were the weakest: F22, F23, F38, and F39 were captured as product constraints but not as rationales.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The v06 gate denied credit for several plausible rows because they were only rules: F6, F11, F19, F22, F23, F26, F29, F31, F32, F33, F38, and F39.

The failure shape suggests a strong implementation plan whose compression strategy kept hard architecture and non-goals, but often dropped the more delicate relational reasons behind quiet social, starter/adoption, mood continuity, and observer-record choices.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound v06 operator. It usefully separated high planning quality from thinner rationale preservation.
- Add more targeted headroom whys around quiet social surfaces and onboarding/adoption; these were captured as rules but frequently missed as reasons.
- Preserve the multi-layer format for high-value affective whys. The layer table made it clear that models keep primary causes while dropping secondary/downstream consequences.
- Consider requiring planners to include a short "why this constraint exists" note for every non-goal and exception. This run would likely improve on F6, F11, F19, F23, F29, F33, F38, and F39.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar at three inherited decisions. It was discriminative here without making system scoring degenerate.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring pass treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not use prior run memory, subagents, peer slots, other waves, or `prd/`.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run in one wave; it gives no variance signal by itself.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on feature 25 (starter adoption), 53 (one screen/no panning), and 97 (actual aviary for visitors). Even if all three flipped, planning would remain above 94%.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The most subjective calls were S2 and S6. Both were preserved across the plan, but the reconstruction did not recover all layers as clean system-level principles.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no recovered whys that required an ungrounded reconstruction claim.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several rules were present but denied why credit because the frozen reconstruction either said `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN` or omitted the rationale.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing data existed for phase 1 and phase 2A only; the score JSON includes those bounded fields and does not fabricate phase 2B timing.
