## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional:** functional whys recovered **50/54** weighted points; affective whys recovered **81/98**. The functional architecture was unusually well encoded; the missing material is mostly relationship framing around quiet account/social surfaces.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2:** weight-3 whys recovered **98/108**, while weight-2 whys recovered **33/44**. The model preserved load-bearing architecture and explicit prohibitions better than smaller targeted rationales.
- **System vs feature level:** system-level recovery (**25/28**) was slightly stronger than feature-level recovery (**106/124**). The plan preserved philosophy, but the reconstruction sometimes flattened feature rationales into mechanisms.
- **Multi-layer pattern:** downstream consequence layers were most often compressed, especially F9, F14, F27, and F30. Primary implementation causes survived more reliably.
- **Subdomain pattern:** simulation, sync, privacy topology, audio, and accessibility scored highest. Starter onboarding, export, and visit-log rationale were weaker.
- **Evidence-bound effect:** F8, F11, F29, F34, and F38 would look superficially covered if rule survival counted; v06 correctly denied why credit.

The failure shape suggests a mechanism-first planner/reconstructor: excellent at converting product intent into enforceable architecture, less reliable at preserving the quiet emotional reason for account and social affordances.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom whys like F29, F34, and F38. They detected meaningful compression even in a very strong plan.
- Add a few more small account/social whys where the rule is ordinary but the relationship rationale matters. These are the easiest to flatten into security mechanics.
- Keep the v06 evidence-bound operator and `rule_without_why` diagnostic. It cleanly separated implementation capture from intent recovery.
- Consider adding a display distinction for system-level partials where the plan preserves the principle but B does not name it cleanly in `System-level intent`; S4 and S6 were the two subjective calls here.
