## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 34.0 / 54.0 (63.0%); affective whys recovered 55.0 / 98.0 (56.1%). Functional whys were helped by concrete architecture rules such as server canonical truth and telemetry separation.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 64.0 / 108.0 (59.3%), slightly below weight-2 whys at 56.8%. Multi-layer whys exposed compression of secondary/downstream rationale.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (67.9%) beat feature-level fidelity (56.5%). The reconstruction preserved the plan's philosophy better than feature-local non-obvious reasons.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes survived most often. Secondary contributors such as population-level drift corruption details, phase-canceling chorus artifacts, and harmless-toast temptation were the most frequent losses.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and privacy were strongest (S7, S8, F17, F18, F36). Social optional and quiet account operations were weakest (F23, F37, F38, F39, F34).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F6, F11, F23, F29, F33, F34, F37, F38, and F39 were mechanism-only under v06 and received no feature-level recovery credit.

The failure shape suggests a planner that can encode nearly all requested product work, while the downstream reconstruction tends to preserve operative constraints and slogans more reliably than the deeper affective rationale behind exclusions.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted feature-level whys for simple-looking social/account surfaces. This run captured visit invitations, logs, visitor snapshots, export, and notebook read-only behavior, but the reconstruction often lost why those are relationship-preserving rather than merely privacy or settings features.
- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated implementation capture from rationale recovery and prevented rule-only rows from inflating fidelity.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for calibration-sensitive features. F1-F4, F10-F14, F24-F25, and F40 gave useful partial-credit diagnostics rather than forcing brittle full/none calls.
- Consider adding a small required rationale field in phase-1 plan prompts for explicit exclusions. Non-goals were implemented thoroughly here, but their reasons often compressed to "do not build this."

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The frozen reconstruction reads as plan-derived and the validity audit found no gold-ID leakage or rubric vocabulary. Same-context risk appears low from the artifact, but this remains an artifact-level audit rather than proof of process isolation.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; no variance signal is available from slot 001 alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Capture was inclusive for glossary/domain terminology and settle opt-in because the plan encoded the implementation substance across architecture and UX rules. These calls did not create denominator exclusions because all F1-F40 anchors were captured.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The (b) bar was strict but generally easy because the plan repeated core principles across multiple sections. Partial system scores mostly reflect reconstruction layer loss, not absence from PLAN.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were found. Several rows had thinner evidence than the PLAN, so they were scored partial or none rather than treated as confabulated.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rule-only recoveries drove the largest denials: F6, F11, F23, F29, F33, F34, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing data existed for phase 1 and phase 2A only; phase 2B timing was not present in the slot ledger at write time.
