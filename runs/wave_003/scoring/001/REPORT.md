## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys scored slightly higher by weight (38.0/54.0) than affective whys (64.0/96.0). The planner was strongest where architecture made the rationale concrete, especially S7, F17, F18, F35, and F36.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys survived better (78.0/108.0) than weight-2 whys (24.0/42.0). Multi-layer items often preserved the primary mechanism while dropping a secondary affective rationale.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The system philosophy survived strongly (24.0/28.0), while feature-level fidelity was lower (78.0/122.0). The plan carried philosophy through architecture, but B often reconstructed feature rules without the PRD's deeper emotional reason.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** L1 primary causes were usually easiest to recover. L2 and L3 layers dropped when they involved subtle user-relationship consequences: S2 processed-vs-seen, F14 intention rotation, F30 unlock economy, and F40 screen-reader queue overload.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and accessibility were strongest. Social/optional and quiet product-experience exclusions were weaker: F22, F23, F29, F34, and F38 mostly survived as mechanisms.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** V06 denied several plausible v1-style recoveries because the reconstruction had only the rule: F5, F6, F8, F19, F22, F23, F29, F34, and F38.

What this suggests: the candidate plan is broad and buildable, and its strongest intent encoding happens where the implementation itself enforces the value. The main leak is not missing product scope; it is compression of non-obvious affective rationale into implementation rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separates high planning coverage from weaker why recovery, which mattered in this run.
- Add more targeted social/relationship whys like F38. The social feature area was easy for the planner to implement while compressing away why the surface stays quiet.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for high-weight whys. It exposed useful partial-credit shapes in F1, F2, F9, F14, F30, and F40.
- Consider adding a small number of single-layer affective exceptions around export and starter onboarding. F29 and F34 were good saturation breakers because the rules are obvious but the rationale is not.
- Keep the strict system-level cross-cutting bar, but require scorers to cite inherited decisions as done above; it prevented over-crediting one-line principle mentions.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify or backfill it. The prompt asserted fresh context; the validity audit below found no significant contamination signatures.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run from one candidate plan and one reconstruction. There is no variance signal here.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on #41, #43, #52, #57, #90, and #93. These calls raise planning quality but do not materially inflate fidelity because most are not F1-F40 anchors.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2 and S6 were the main subjective calls: both were cross-cutting in PLAN, but each lost a layer in reconstruction.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not find material ungrounded reconstruction claims; the reconstruction reads plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Nine included feature whys were mechanism-only recoveries and received no why credit.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing data existed for phase 1 and phase 2A only; the score JSON includes only those bounded fields and does not fabricate phase 2B timing.
