## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional architecture survived better: S7, F17, and F18 were full recoveries around server-side canonical truth. Affective exceptions leaked more often, especially F29-F35 and F38-F39, where the plan encoded the rule but not the relationship rationale.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys often kept their primary layer but lost secondary/downstream layers. F1 and F13 retained the presence conjunction but not population-wide drift corruption; S9/F24/F25 retained designed accessibility surfaces but not all downstream consequences.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (67.9%) was much higher than feature-level fidelity (45.6%). The plan conveyed product philosophy better than feature-specific why chains.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 was the strongest. Layer 2 was often lost when it required comparing against tempting shortcuts, and Layer 3 was often lost when it described the product-level consequence of a small rule break.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and simulation were strongest. Social and affective onboarding/export/notebook surfaces had high capture but weak why recovery.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The clearest evidence-bound denials were rule-without-why rows: F6, F8, F9, F22, F23, F29-F35, F38, and F39.

What this suggests: the candidate can produce a credible implementation architecture, but it compresses subtle product intent into operational rules. The downstream reconstructor was appropriately conservative and did not invent many missing whys.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Preserve the evidence-bound scoring operator; it exposed the difference between high capture and lower rationale transfer.
- Add or retain targeted headroom whys for affective exceptions, especially onboarding-as-arrival, no welcome surface, read-only observer notebook, visit log transparency, and actual-aviary sharing.
- Consider requiring plans to include a short "why this rule exists" sentence for every explicit exclusion. This run had many exclusions but fewer reasons.
- Clarify capture guidance for implicit API absences. I counted F33 read-only notebook inclusively because only generated/fetch flows exist, but this call did not affect fidelity because the why was absent.
- Keep multi-layer whys for high-risk functional surfaces like presence and sync. They separated mere implementation from calibration/failure-mode understanding.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the frozen reconstruction as valid phase-2A output and did not modify it.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one candidate run, so no variance estimate is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls included F33's read-only notebook anchor, social visit default-off, visitor actual snapshot, and top-bar contents. These mostly affected planning quality, not recovered why credit.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2, S5, S6, and S9 required the most judgment against the three-feature cross-cutting bar; evidence is listed above.
- **Confabulation cases.** No included why was marked as ungrounded reconstruction. Some reconstruction statements were compressed, but they remained plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several v1-style plausible recoveries were denied because the reconstruction supplied only the operational rule or explicitly said `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN`.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** 14 included whys were rule-without-why cases.
- **Operational compromise.** TIMING.json had phase 1 and phase 2A timing for this slot but no phase 2B timing at score-write time, so the JSON timing object omits unavailable phase 2B fields.
