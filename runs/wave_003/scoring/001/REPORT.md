## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 66.7%, ahead of affective whys at 73.5%. The planner and reconstructor preserved architecture/privacy mechanics well, especially S7/F17/F20/F36. Affective exceptions leaked more often: F11, F29, F33, and F34 became rules or lifecycle items without the relationship rationale.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 68.5%, below weight-2 whys at 77.3%. High-weight entries often retained the primary rule but dropped the secondary temptation/failure-mode layer. Examples: F1/F13 lost silent population-wide drift failure; F9/F10 lost the wrong-product/reframing consequence; F27 lost the foothold-to-different-product chain.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (78.6%) exceeded feature-level fidelity (69.4%). The plan carried product philosophy broadly, but reconstruction compressed several feature whys into mechanism summaries.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** The most frequently dropped layer was L3, the downstream consequence. L1 rules and L2 implementation constraints survived better than affective failure modes.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync/privacy and accessibility were strong. Social visit mechanics were mostly clear. The weakest areas were subtle affective exceptions: settle optionality, starter-birds-as-arrivals, notebook-as-observer-record, and export-as-relationship-copy.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied credit where PLAN or reconstruction had only mechanism text. F5, F11, F29, F33, and F34 are the clearest rule-without-why cases. No ungrounded reconstruction was found.

What this suggests: the candidate plan was an excellent implementation plan and a good carrier of system intent, but its compression style still flattened some non-obvious product-relationship rationales into operational guardrails.
