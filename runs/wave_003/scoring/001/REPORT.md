## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 53.6% by weight versus 51.2% for affective whys. The gap is small because functional architecture was strong but several functional privacy/export/deletion rationales were still mechanism-only.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered better (57.7%) than weight-2 whys (36.8%). High-weight items were more often repeated in plan structure, but downstream layers still leaked.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System fidelity (71.4%) outpaced feature fidelity (47.4%). The plan preserved broad architecture and accessibility principles better than local feature exceptions.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary layers survived most often. Secondary temptation/failure examples and downstream product-consequence layers were the common drops, especially F14, F27, F30, and F35.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Server-side sync, performance, reduced motion, and telemetry were strongest. Social surfaces, starter/adoption rationale, field-notebook editability, account export, and deletion rationale were weakest.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F6, F21-F23, F29, F33-F35, and F37-F39 were denied because the reconstruction preserved a rule but not the why. F31 was explicitly `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN`.

The failure shape suggests a high-coverage implementation planner whose compressed plan still lets a reconstructor recover architecture and local mechanics, but not many affective rationales that explain why tempting alternative surfaces must stay excluded.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted headroom additions. F29-F40 successfully exposed rule-without-why behavior in a strong plan.
- Add more affective exception probes in social/account/export surfaces, where the implementation rule is easy but the product-relationship rationale is fragile.

- Consider a clearer system-level distinction between named principle, inherited rule, and explicit rationale. S2/S3 were visible in rules but not cleanly in reconstructed philosophy.
- Keep evidence-bound scoring. It prevented plan-only or mechanism-only content from inflating feature-level recovery.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the supplied `RECONSTRUCTION.md` as frozen, did not modify it, and did not read `prd/`, peer slots, or other waves.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is a single run; no variance signal is available inside this score.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on notice-never-announce, settle gesture, subtle parallax, no avatars during visits, and visitor actual-aviary view. These calls raise planning quality but do not give why credit without reconstruction evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2, S3, S5, and S6 required judgment about whether repeated implementation rules met the cross-cutting bar; the appendix above records the evidence trail.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not mark any included why as ungrounded reconstruction; the reconstruction reads plan-derived and mostly conservative.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Mechanism-only rows were common and explicitly counted as `rule_without_why`, especially in social, account, and notebook features.
