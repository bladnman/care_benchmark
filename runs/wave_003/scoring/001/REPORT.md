## 3. Diagnostic patterns

**Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered **34 / 54** weighted points (63.0%); affective whys recovered **54 / 94** (57.4%). Functional architecture survived best where it was embodied in data flow: S7, F17, F18, F36. Affective whys often compressed to rules: F6, F19, F23, F29, F31, F34, F38, and F39.

**Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered **64 / 108** (59.3%); weight-2 whys recovered **24 / 40** (60.0%). High-weight whys did not clearly survive better. The common miss was the downstream consequence layer, not the primary rule.

**System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was **71.4%**, substantially above feature-level fidelity at **56.7%**. The plan preserved the philosophy broadly, but feature-specific rationales were frequently transformed into implementation instructions.

**Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 usually survived. Layer 2 survived when it was technical (open-tab inflation, phase cancellation, PII leakage), and often disappeared when it was product-political (well-meaning contributors adding toasts, leaderboard comparison pressure). Layer 3 was most often lost.

**Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and server authority were strongest. Audio was good on procedural mechanics but thinner on affective spine. Social and targeted account-lifecycle additions exposed the biggest rule-without-why gap.

**Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied likely v1-style credit where the plan or reconstruction had only the mechanism. Ten included whys were marked `rule_without_why`; there were no ungrounded reconstruction cases.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted F29-F40 additions. They produced useful separation between complete feature planning and actual why preservation.
- Add more consequence-layer probes. This run shows that downstream consequences are the first layer to disappear under implementation-heavy planning.
- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It prevented mechanism-only rows like F34, F35, and F39 from inflating intent fidelity.
- Consider reporting tiny product-surface misses as a separate planning subtype. Missing privacy-policy link, email-change flow, and top-bar fade are real misses but qualitatively different from missing server authority.
- Keep both affective and functional whys. Functional whys were easier to ground in architecture; affective whys exposed more compression.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The reconstruction was treated as frozen and was not modified. Validity audit verdict: PASS.

- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls were made for mood daily-ish reset, settle undo, top-bar contents, visits default-off, weather mood detail, and aggregate performance observability. These affect planning quality more than fidelity.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S1, S2, S6, and S9 were scored partial because the plan/reconstruction preserved the cross-cutting principle but dropped at least one multi-layer gold consequence.
- **Confabulation cases.** No ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted; weak rows were mechanism-only or honest non-recovery, not contamination.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F5, F6, F19, F23, F29, F31, F34, F35, F38, and F39 were counted as rule-without-why cases.
