## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** The split was close: functional recovered 30/54 weighted points; affective recovered 58/98. Functional losses were mainly deeper architectural consequences (F16, F17, F18, F20, F35), while affective losses were often product-relationship rationales (F23, F27, F31, F39).
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys did worse than weight-2 whys: 53.7% vs 68.2%. Multi-layer rows often kept the primary implementation rule and dropped a downstream consequence.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner/reconstructor preserved system philosophy much better than feature-specific whys: 78.6% vs 53.2%. S-level labels were explicit in the plan; feature exceptions often got collapsed into non-goal clusters.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** L1 was the strongest layer; L2 and L3 were frequently dropped. Examples: F1 lost silent population corruption; F2 lost the one-week/three-week instrument gap; F17 lost client-tick failure; F30 lost the economy-erosion consequence.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio and accessibility were comparatively strong (F4, F25, F40 full). Social and non-goal rationales were weaker (F22, F23, F27, F38, F39). Accounts/sync had strong rules but partial architectural whys.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The strict evidence gate denied likely v1-style credit for rows where the plan/reconstruction had the rule but not the why: F5, F8, F14, F23, F27, F31, F38, and F39.

The failure shape suggests a strong implementation planner that can preserve broad scope and many product rules, but tends to compress the reasons behind exceptions into umbrella slogans like no gamification, no social network, and server-owned canon.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted headroom additions. F31, F38, and F39 exposed misses that would have been hidden by only scoring the original canonical table.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for high-weight whys. The layer split produced useful discrimination: many rows were not simply right/wrong, but L1-only or L1+L2.
- Add a scorer checklist for downstream-consequence language. This run repeatedly dropped the consequence layer even when the primary cause survived.
- Maintain evidence-bound scoring. It prevented rule-only preservation from being mistaken for why recovery.
- Consider adding a small "exception rationale" cluster in future gold lists, because this run was weakest on why the product refuses tempting adjacent patterns.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The validity audit found no significant contamination signatures. Reconstruction was frozen and not modified.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run in wave_003 for candidate grok-build-0.1. It provides no variance signal by itself.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive for compressed clusters where the implementation substance was present, especially personality/mood definitions, starter-bird adoption, bird-chosen perches, and parallax.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S6 and S7 required the most judgment because the plan preserved them broadly, while the reconstruction did not always name every layer in the system section.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted. Thin rationale was usually grounded in PLAN slogans or mechanisms, then scored partial/none as appropriate.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rows such as F8, F14, F27, F31, F38, and F39 looked operationally correct but lacked exact why evidence.
- **Operational compromise.** TIMING.json contained phase1 and phase2a for run 001 only; phase2b timing was not supplied and was not fabricated.
