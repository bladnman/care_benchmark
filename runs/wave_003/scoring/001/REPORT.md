## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Recovery was nearly even: functional whys recovered 17.0 / 44.0 weighted and affective whys recovered 30.0 / 78.0. The plan preserved functional mechanisms around F2, F17, and F18, but missed privacy/account rationales such as F36 and F37. Affective rows often had broad atmosphere but lost exception logic, as in F8, F14, F23, and F31.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 rows scored higher because partial credit could capture one or two layers. Weight-2 rows were brittle under the v06 evidence gate: F6, F8, F21, F23, F26, F36, and F37 all had mechanisms but no recoverable why.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level and feature-level recovery were close (39.3% vs 38.3%). The planner preserved broad philosophy and architecture, but compressed many specific exception reasons out of the plan.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary mechanism layers were most likely to survive; secondary temptation/failure-mode layers and downstream product-collapse consequences were frequently absent.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Drift calibration and server-authoritative sync were strongest. Social, account lifecycle, privacy boundary, and detailed interaction nuance were weakest.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The strict gate denied many plausible v1-style recoveries because the reconstruction only restated rules: examples include F6 max-7, F8 hidden vector, F13 presence events, F21 read-only visits, F26 TTFB, F36 telemetry, and F37 sharing.

The failure shape suggests a competent implementation outline that is good at naming architectural primitives and weak at carrying affective/product rationale through to downstream builders.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep v06 evidence-bound scoring. This run demonstrates why: mechanism-only preservation looked superficially strong, but the evidence rows show why fidelity remained below 40%.
- Add more single-layer exception whys around account, privacy, and social features. These are where the plan named policy boundaries while dropping the deeper relationship rationale.
- Consider a slightly more mechanical system-level cross-cutting form: require scorers to list three inherited decisions before awarding the PLAN-preserved half. S3, S4, and S8 were judgment-heavy.
- Preserve multi-layer whys for calibration/architecture, but make downstream-consequence layers more explicit in the scoring notes; they were the most commonly lost layer.
- Keep the complete 120-feature denominator. It exposed that this plan was broad but shallow: 72 features captured, with many social/account/accessibility details absent.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring pass treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. The prompt supplied fresh-context constraints; the validity audit found no clear contamination signatures.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one sample from one candidate run. No variance conclusions should be drawn from this slot alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on several planning calls, including product aliveness, vector-vs-mood, mood state, user naming, no welcome-back toast, settle gesture, field notebook auto-entries, presence accounting, ambient weather, scene-load continuity, reduced-motion charm, call captions, 60fps hardware, and performance observability.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The cross-cutting bar was the most subjective area. S4 and S8 received partial credit from plan-side rules even though the reconstruction did not recover their full rationale.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not count any clear ungrounded-reconstruction cases. The reconstruction was sometimes broad, but its broad claims were generally traceable to the compact plan.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Many rows had PLAN and reconstruction evidence for the rule but not for the why; those stayed none for single-layer rows and partial at most for multi-layer rows.
