## 3. Diagnostic patterns

Functional whys recovered better than affective whys: 85.2% vs 80.6%. Architecture-heavy rationales like S7, F2, F7, F17, F35, and F36 were carried very cleanly because the plan encoded them as invariants, schema rules, CI checks, or operational boundaries.

The main leak is rule-without-why. The reconstruction preserved the mechanism for F8, F11, F19, F29, F33, and F38, but not the gold rationale behind the rule. These are mostly affective exceptions where the PRD's reason is about the user's relationship to the birds rather than about correctness.

Weight-3 whys recovered better than weight-2 whys (87.0% vs 70.5%). The high-weight mechanics were often protected by multiple plan surfaces, while several lower-weight headroom whys were implemented as quiet product rules.

System-level fidelity (96.4%) exceeded feature-level fidelity (79.0%). The plan carried the philosophy well; the reconstruction lost detail mostly when a local exception needed its own rationale.

Multi-layer partials usually retained the primary implementation cause and lost either the secondary explanatory layer or downstream consequence. Examples: F1, F12, F14, F16, F18, F20, and F30.
