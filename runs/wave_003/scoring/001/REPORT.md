## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 48.0% by weight, while affective whys recovered at 28.6%. Server continuity, presence accounting, and privacy boundaries survived better than relationship-shaping rationales like S2, F14, F27, F28, F29, and F31.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 41.7%, slightly below weight-2 whys at 21.1%. High-weight whys often preserved their primary rule but dropped secondary and downstream layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (64.3%) substantially exceeded feature-level fidelity (28.3%). The plan/reconstruction carried broad architecture and accessibility philosophy better than specific feature rationales.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers were the most likely to survive. Secondary calibration/failure-mode layers and downstream relationship consequences were usually lost, especially in F1, F3, F4, F13, F17, F18, F24, and F40.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, accessibility, and privacy performed best. Interactions, social optional surfaces, and aviary-load/layout details leaked most of their rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The largest effect was `rule_without_why`: 23 included whys preserved a mechanism or exclusion without the gold rationale. No ungrounded reconstruction was counted.

What this suggests: the candidate plan is a competent implementation outline, but it compresses product intent into architecture and non-goal lists. It is good at saying what to build and weaker at preserving why particular quiet/affective constraints exist.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It surfaced the central failure mode: high feature capture with low feature-level why recovery.
- Add or emphasize more targeted feature-level exceptions around quiet-affective product rules. This run captured no gamification, no Tamagotchi mechanics, read-only visits, and no public discovery, but not the relationship reasons behind them.
- Consider asking phase-1 planners to preserve rationale alongside implementation bullets. Without that nudge, strong implementation plans may still erase the intent being benchmarked.
- Preserve the system cross-cutting bar, but document rule-vs-rationale splits explicitly. S4 and S5 show that a plan can carry the rule and still drop the why.
- Keep functional and affective whys mixed. The gap between functional and affective recovery was one of the most diagnostic findings in this run.

---

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The reconstruction appears plan-derived and scoped: no gold IDs or rubric vocabulary were found, and the headings follow the plan's structure rather than the gold list.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only; no variance signal is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls were made for features 25, 29, 33, 43, 93, and 97. They raise planning quality but mostly do not increase fidelity because their whys were absent or partial.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 and S5 are the most subjective calls: the plan preserves the rules cross-cuttingly, but the reconstruction misses important rationale, so both were scored partial.
- **Confabulation cases.** None were counted. The reconstruction generally stayed grounded in PLAN text and often honestly marked whys as not recoverable.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Many apparent recoveries were denied because the reconstruction preserved only a mechanism: no streaks, no gamification, no Tamagotchi, visitor surfaces, account export, stable bird identity, and matter-of-fact errors.
- **Operational compromise.** `TIMING.json` had phase1 and phase2a timings for run 001 but no phase2b timing at scoring time, so the score JSON includes only available timing fields.
