## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional intent recovered better (70.4%) than affective intent (63.3%). The plan preserved the structural spine especially well: S6/S7/S8, F18, F21, F26, F36. Losses clustered where the why was relational or tonal rather than architectural: F4, F5, F10, F33, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 61.1%, below the weight-2 rate of 77.3%. The multi-layer rows often preserved the primary rule and one supporting layer, then dropped downstream consequences: F1, F2, F9, F13, F16, F17, F20, F24, F27, F30, F31, F35, and F40 are the recurring pattern.
- **System vs feature level.** System-level fidelity (78.6%) outperformed feature-level fidelity (62.9%). The reconstruction caught the plan's big product contracts - server ownership, privacy boundaries, accessibility at launch, and presence as real interaction - but feature-specific exceptions often collapsed into implementation summaries.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, privacy, and server simulation were the strongest areas. Audio and tonal/social exceptions were weaker: the plan says procedural calls and read-only/actual visits, but the reconstruction rarely recovered why recorded loops, editable notebooks, or show-off visit renderings would change the product.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** V06 denied credit where a v1 semantic glance might have been kinder. F4 and F5 are good examples: both mechanisms were plainly present, but the frozen reconstruction did not recover the gold rationale. F29 was scored none because B explicitly wrote `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN`.

Overall, the candidate plan carried an excellent implementation map. The reconstruction retained the architecture and launch contracts, but compressed several affective rationales into rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound split. This run shows why: implementation-complete plans can still lose the why, and rule-only rows would otherwise look artificially faithful.
- Add more targeted feature exceptions like F29-F40. The best discrimination came from cases where strong plans implemented the rule but did not preserve the deeper product reason, especially F33 and F39.
- Preserve multi-layer whys for high-leverage areas, but consider adding a per-layer subtotal to the report schema. The dominant loss was downstream consequence, not primary rule.
- For system-level scoring, keep the cross-cutting bar but clarify how much reconstruction-side rationale is needed when the PLAN itself strongly preserves the principle. S4 exposed that subjectivity.
- Consider separating "mechanism evidence" from "why evidence" in the next schema. The current ledger can represent rule-without-why, but the aggregate evidence counts need scorer judgment.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. The prompt represented this as a fresh-context scorer run with a separate phase-2A reconstruction.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one candidate run and one reconstruction/scoring pass. It gives no within-model variance estimate.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I counted #7 (glossary/domain terms) and #93 (no avatars during visits) as captured by semantic implication. I counted #87 (privacy-policy link) as missed because the specific account-settings surface is absent.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The hardest calls were S1-S4: the PLAN preserves them cross-cuttingly, but B often expressed the rule cluster without the full rationale. I awarded partial credit where only one side or only some layers met the evidence gate.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no material ungrounded reconstruction that I credited. No rows were downgraded for confabulated rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F4, F5, F10, F29, F33, and F39 were scored none despite captured features because the frozen reconstruction lacked the gold why.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Seven rows were marked rule-without-why: S4, F4, F5, F10, F29, F33, and F39.
