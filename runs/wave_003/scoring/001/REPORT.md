## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better (34.0/54.0) than affective whys (44.0/98.0). The plan and reconstruction were strongest where the why was architectural: server authority, low-pass drift, append-only events, synthetic IDs, and telemetry boundaries. Affective exceptions often collapsed to rules: no trait numbers, no catalog starter birds, no editable notebook, no social attention loop, and no show-off mode.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 rows recovered better in aggregate (64.0/108.0) than weight-2 rows (14.0/44.0), but this was driven by technical multi-layer rows such as F2 and F18. High-weight affective exceptions like F14, F30, and F31 still lost their deeper layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved system philosophy better than feature-specific rationale: system-level fidelity was 78.6%, feature-level fidelity was 45.2%. The system pass recovered the broad shape, while the feature pass frequently listed what to build without why it mattered.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 mechanisms survived most often. Layer 2 temptation/contrast arguments and Layer 3 downstream product-consequence arguments were the most common losses.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, drift, performance, and accessibility did well. Social, notebook ownership, bird identity, starter-adoption framing, and several notice-never-announce exceptions were much weaker.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Rule-only evidence denied credit for F8, F14, F21, F22, F23, F29, F30, F31, F33, F34, F37, F38, and F39. These would look better under a mechanism-only rubric but do not recover intent here.

The failure shape suggests a plan that is buildable and usually faithful at the systems layer, but compressed many product-relationship whys into prohibitions or implementation rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound operator. It cleanly exposed the difference between preserving a rule and preserving the reason for the rule.
- Add more targeted feature-level exceptions in areas where this run leaked: social sharing, visitor surfaces, editable/personal records, identity continuity, and reward-loop-adjacent unlocks.
- Keep multi-layer whys for high-risk affective exceptions, but make the reports ask explicitly which layer failed: mechanism, temptation/contrast, or downstream product consequence.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting bar. It prevented single principle mentions from scoring as full recovery on S2, S4, S5, and S6.
- Consider a diagnostic counter for rule-only recoveries by subdomain, since the strongest signal here was rule survival without rationale.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the frozen reconstruction as read-only and used only the allowed phase-two files plus slot 001 plan/metadata/timing entry. No peer slots, other waves, or `prd/` files were read.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; there is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on starter-bird onboarding, mood persistence, greeting stagger, settle opt-in, settle undo, color palette, and visitor actual-aviary behavior. These increased planning quality but also made more feature whys reachable.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2, S5, and S6 were partial because important affective/equivalence layers were missing even though operational inheritance was strong.
- **Confabulation cases.** I counted no ungrounded reconstruction cases. The reconstruction reads plan-derived, not contaminated, but it often compresses rationale away.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several rows had exact rule evidence and still scored none because neither PLAN nor reconstruction carried the gold rationale.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Eighteen included whys were marked rule-without-why, concentrated in social features, relationship-protection features, and no-gamification/no-announcement exceptions.
