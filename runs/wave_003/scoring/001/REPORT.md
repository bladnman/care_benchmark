## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered slightly better (32 / 54 = 59.3%) than affective whys (54 / 98 = 55.1%). Functional architecture such as S7, F17, F18, and F36 was explicit; affective social and observer-record whys leaked.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Higher-weight whys recovered better (66 / 108 = 61.1%) than weight-2 whys (20 / 44 = 45.5%), mostly because major architecture and accessibility whys had implementation-level hooks in the plan.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System principles were robust (85.7%); feature whys were compressed (50.0%). The plan carried the product philosophy, but the reconstructor often wrote rule summaries rather than why summaries.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** L1 implementation causes survived most often. L2 temptation/counterfactual layers and L3 relationship/product-collapse consequences were the common losses.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync, audio proceduralism, reduced motion, and telemetry did well. Social sharing, visit-log transparency, account export, starter birds, stable identity, and notebook-as-observer-record mostly became rule-without-why cases.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Fourteen rows were denied full recovery because the rule survived without the rationale. The largest denials were F29-F31 and F37-F39, where the plan captured mechanics but B did not recover the relational reason.

What this suggests: this candidate is strong at planning the build surface and preserving architectural invariants, but weaker at making exception rationales portable when those rationales are not repeated in the plan as explicit prose.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted headroom additions. They prevented a saturated score by measuring whether rules such as age-based offers, stable identity, visit logs, and show-off-mode refusal carried their deeper rationale.
- Add more exception whys around social/privacy/account surfaces. This run shows those are where strong planners most often preserve mechanics while dropping the reason.
- Consider requiring plans to carry a short "why" note beside non-goals and exception rules. This would distinguish intentional refusals from copied exclusions and improve reconstruction fidelity.
- Keep the v06 evidence-bound operator. It made the difference between "the feature exists" and "the intent survived" visible and auditable.
- Preserve the current cross-cutting system bar. The three-inheritance threshold was subjective but useful; it rewarded genuinely recurring plan constraints without over-crediting isolated slogans.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The validity audit found no significant contamination signs. The reconstruction mirrored the PLAN's headings and vocabulary, not the gold list.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; no variance signal is available from this slot alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive capture affected four noted rows: per-bird call signature, offer reaction variation, bird-chosen perch, and evening palette/call quieting. Even if all four were flipped to missed, all 40 feature why anchors would remain reachable except none of those are F anchors, so fidelity would not change.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The (b) calls used the evidence appendix above. S2 and S6 were partial because B missed important layers, not because the PLAN lacked cross-cutting support.
- **Confabulation cases.** None were counted. No ungrounded reconstruction received recovery credit.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several v1-style semantic recoveries were denied because the frozen reconstruction carried a mechanism without the gold rationale, especially F6, F11, F19, F22, F23, F26, F29-F31, F33, F34, and F37-F39.
