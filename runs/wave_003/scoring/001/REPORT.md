## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was stronger (55.6%) than affective recovery (44.0%). Server ownership, additive deltas, strict presence, and telemetry boundaries were reconstructable because the plan gave concrete mechanisms and failure modes. Affective exceptions such as S2, F9, F11, F23, F31, F37, F38, and F39 often survived only as rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 52.0%, slightly below weight-2 whys at 39.5%. Multi-layer whys often lost downstream consequence layers even when primary mechanics survived.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (60.7%) beat feature-level fidelity (45.5%). The reconstruction could infer broad architecture and accessibility principles, but many feature-specific whys were too compressed in the plan.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanism layers survived most often. Secondary calibration/failure-mode layers survived for drift, no-LWW, reduced motion, and narration cadence. Downstream affective consequence layers were the most commonly dropped.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and accessibility were strongest. Social optional features and targeted headroom additions were weakest: account export/deletion, per-invite sharing, visit log, and actual-aviary sharing were mostly mechanism-only.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The v06 evidence gate denied credit to rule-only rows: max-seven birds, return greeting, settle, synthetic account id, stable bird identity, account export/deletion, visit log, and no show-off visits. Those features counted for planning when captured, but not for why recovery.

The failure shape suggests qwen3-coder produced a useful implementation plan, but compressed the product's affective contract into engineering mechanisms. That is exactly where the reconstruction then became honest but thin.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom whys. F29-F40 exposed real separation between implementation coverage and rationale preservation; without them this run would look more saturated.
- Add more social/privacy/account-data exceptions. This run implemented export, deletion, visits, logs, and telemetry mechanically, while losing relationship-rights and control rationales.
- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It prevented rule-only statements from scoring as recovered intent and made the gap between 77.5% planning and 48.6% fidelity visible.
- Consider a stricter system-level rationale field. S4-like restraint rules can appear in many places without the depth-over-variety principle actually surviving.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for affective whys. It usefully distinguishes primary rule preservation from downstream product-shape consequences.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the frozen reconstruction as read-only and did not modify it. No PRD files, peer slots, or other waves were opened.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; no variance signal is available inside this scoring pass.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive for capture on top-bar settings, read-only notebook, no streak/days-visited, single horizontal/no-panning, bird-chosen perch, scene loads with motion, visits default-off, visitor non-interaction, no chat/avatar during visits, actual-aviary visits, and no leaderboards/discovery. These calls affect planning quality, not why recovery.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The cross-cutting bar was strict on rationale, not merely repeated rules. S2 and S4 therefore scored none despite adjacent implementation constraints.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not count any ungrounded reconstruction claims as recovered; the reconstruction mostly stayed close to PLAN wording.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several likely v1-style recoveries were denied because the reconstruction or plan carried only the operational rule, not the gold rationale.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 17 included rule-without-why cases, concentrated in bird cap, greeting/settle, synthetic IDs, identity, export/deletion, and social visit surfaces.
