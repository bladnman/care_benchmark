## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys did slightly better: **38/54 = 70.4%** versus affective whys at **66/98 = 67.3%**. The privacy/server/presence architecture survived strongly (S6-S8, F18, F20, F35-F36), while affective rationales around comparison, catalog choice, visitor authenticity, and export as relationship-copy leaked.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys were not much better than weight-2 whys: **74/108 = 68.5%** versus **30/44 = 68.2%**. The primary rules usually survived, but secondary and downstream layers were often compressed.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved philosophy better than per-feature rationale: system-level fidelity was **78.6%**, feature-level fidelity **66.1%**. The reconstruction recovered the large architectural posture but missed several narrow exception whys.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** L1 primary causes survived most often. L2 and L3 failed when the why depended on a specific future failure mode: Tamagotchi-vs-screensaver, well-meaning toast, impossible-to-retrofit IDs, client-tick collapse, and stable identity's retroactive relationship loss.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and privacy were strongest. Social was mixed: per-invite, read-only visits, visit logs, and notifications survived, but no-leaderboards and visitor-sees-actual-aviary lost their deeper relationship rationale. Accessibility was strong except F40, which is forced to none because the reconstruction marked the cadence why not recoverable.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Seven included feature whys were rule-only: F6, F11, F23, F29, F34, F39, and F40. Three rows were effectively plan-only/not reconstructed: S3, S4, and F40.

The failure shape suggests an excellent implementation plan with strong rule capture, but the encoding loses some "why this tempting alternative is the wrong product" explanations.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted exception whys where a feature is easy to implement correctly while losing the rationale: export as relationship-copy, visitor actualness/no show-off mode, and no-catalog onboarding are good examples from this run.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for high-weight whys, but add a clearer report convention for partial layer recovery when the PLAN has the layer and B recovers only the operational surface.
- Consider a separate diagnostic for "explicit NOT RECOVERABLE despite nearby evidence" because F40 shows a reconstruction can honestly mark non-recovery even while adjacent prose gestures toward the rationale.
- Preserve the current system-level cross-cutting bar. It was subjective for S3/S4, but useful: it separated plan-level preservation from reconstruction-level identification.
- For future product instances, include at least a few non-simulation, non-privacy functional whys; this instance's strongest recoveries cluster around server authority and telemetry boundaries.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scorer treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. No subagents or background agents were used in this scoring pass.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run with no variance signal. Wave-level aggregation is needed before drawing model-level conclusions.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Feature 62, 69, and 88 were counted as captured under the inclusive rubric. Counting all three as misses would move planning from 98.3% to 95.8%, but would not change the fidelity denominator because none anchor F1-F40.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3 and S4 were plan-preserved but not clearly identified by B, producing partial credit. See the cross-cutting appendix above.
- **Confabulation cases.** No ungrounded reconstruction cases were found. The reconstruction reads plan-derived and frequently refuses to fill gaps.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F6, F11, F23, F29, F34, F39, and F40 would likely look acceptable in a rule-only review but receive no why recovery under v06.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** The implementation mechanisms often survived without the reason the product needs them; this is most visible in social/comparison and relationship-copy features.
