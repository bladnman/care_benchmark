## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 40.0/54.0 (74.1%); affective whys recovered 79.0/98.0 (80.6%). Functional losses were mostly missing second/third layers (F1, F7, F16, F35), while affective losses were concentrated in rule-only ritual/copy rows (F5, F8-F11, F34).
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys recovered 86.0/108.0 (79.6%); weight-2 whys recovered 33.0/44.0 (75.0%). The stronger plan generally preserved high-weight architecture and non-goal rationale.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was high at 96.4%; feature-level fidelity was 74.2%. The planner encoded philosophy and architecture better than small feature exceptions.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Dropped layers were usually secondary calibration/rationale or downstream consequence: F1, F7, F13, F16, F31, F35, and F40 all kept the main rule but lost part of the reason it matters.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, privacy telemetry, no-gamification, procedural audio, and accessibility were strong. Greeting specificity, hidden trait values, settle optionality, seven-bird empirical cap, and export rationale leaked.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Seven rows were marked `rule_without_why`; these counted for capture but not fidelity because the frozen reconstruction did not carry the gold rationale.

The failure shape suggests that this candidate is an excellent implementation planner, but the reconstruction shows compression loss around affective exceptions that look like small UI/content decisions rather than architecture.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound operator. It prevented over-crediting feature rows where only the mechanism survived.
- Add or preserve more targeted feature-level exceptions around greeting, settle, hidden stats, and account/data rights; those were the best discriminators here.
- Consider asking reconstructors to group whys by plan sections plus a separate "exception rules" pass. That could expose rule-only losses without leaking gold IDs.
- Clarify examples for distributed affective system whys such as S3 and S4. Those calls were more subjective than the functional architecture rows.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for weight-3 rows; it usefully separated primary-rule recovery from missing calibration/downstream-consequence layers.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I scored from the assigned PLAN, candidate metadata, timing slice, frozen RECONSTRUCTION, rubric, and gold list only; I did not read PRD files, peer slots, or other waves.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; no variance estimate is available from this artifact alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive capture affected several rows: glossary, no welcome toast, settle details, click-anywhere undo, top-bar contents, visits default-off, and no chat/comments/avatars. These kept planning quality at 109/120 rather than a more conservative 102-107 range.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 was the hardest system-level call: PLAN preserves restraint across scope/rendering/data model, but RECONSTRUCTION did not identify the principle, so it was partial.
- **Confabulation cases.** I counted zero ungrounded reconstruction cases. The reconstruction generally stayed plan-derived and used `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN` rather than inventing missing whys.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F5, F6, F8, F9, F10, F11, and F34 were denied recovery because the frozen reconstruction preserved a rule or mechanism without the why.
- **Operational compromise.** TIMING.json supplied phase1 and phase2a timing for run 001 only; phase2b timing was not present and was not fabricated.
