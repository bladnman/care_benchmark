## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** The split was nearly even by rate: functional whys recovered 44.0 / 54.0 (81.5%), while affective whys recovered 80.0 / 98.0 (81.6%). Functional losses clustered around privacy/export nuance (F34) and partially recovered presence calibration layers (F1/F13/F20/F35). Affective losses clustered around social/account headroom whys where the plan preserved the rule but not the rationale (F22, F29, F38, F39).
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys survived better (92.0 / 108.0 (85.2%)) than weight-2 whys (32.0 / 44.0 (72.7%)). The higher-weight items tended to be repeated across architecture, risks, tests, and done criteria.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level intent was stronger: 92.9% vs 79.0%. The plan and reconstruction preserved the big product philosophy, but several small feature-level exception whys collapsed into implementation rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Lost layers were usually downstream-consequence layers: S2's one-toast cascade, F1/F13's silent failure framing, F4's audio-spine consequence, F9's generic-arrival-animation warning, and F40's same-rhythm-as-visual layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, audio architecture, and accessibility were strong. Social optional/account lifecycle headroom had the most rule-without-why cases, especially visits and export.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Six rows were denied because the reconstruction preserved the rule but not the gold rationale: F19, F22, F29, F34, F38, and F39. No recovery was denied for ungrounded reconstruction.

The failure shape suggests an excellent implementation plan that compresses some product-intent prose into architecture and guardrails. The strongest signal is not missing features, but missing reasons behind quiet social/account boundaries.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted social/account exception whys like F38 and F39; they exposed meaningful headroom after most core systems saturated.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for high-weight whys. The downstream layer was the most diagnostic and caught compressions that a simple yes/no why would miss.
- Consider adding a few explicit plan-side prompts that ask for why behind exclusions, not only the exclusion list. This would test whether models preserve rationale without making the gold list easier.
- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It prevented rule-only matches such as matter-of-fact sync errors and account export from receiving undeserved why credit.
- Clarify capture treatment for document/edge features such as glossary and empty-aviary states. These small rows can swing planning quality despite not bearing feature-level whys.
