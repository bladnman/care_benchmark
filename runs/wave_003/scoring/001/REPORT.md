## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better: 48.0/54.0 weighted (88.9%) vs affective 76.0/98.0 (77.6%). The functional architecture around server-side simulation, no LWW, privacy boundaries, and deletion was especially well carried. Affective social exceptions such as F22, F37, and F38 often survived only as mechanisms.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 92.0/108.0 (85.2%), better than weight-2 whys at 32.0/44.0 (72.7%). The plan's most load-bearing material tended to be repeated across architecture, risks, and tests, which helped reconstruction.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was high at 92.9%, but not perfect: S3 and S4 were preserved in the PLAN yet not named cleanly in B's system-intent section. Feature-level fidelity was 79.0%, where compression did more damage.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary layers usually survived. Secondary or downstream layers dropped when they were explanatory rather than operational: F1/F13 lost individual-signal analysis, F9 lost absence-length detail, F30 lost the reward-economy warning.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, privacy, accessibility, and audio were strong. Social and onboarding had the clearest rule-without-why losses: starter birds (F29), friend-visit notification/log rationale (F22/F38), and per-invite private-relationship rationale (F37).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied credit where B preserved a rule without the gold rationale. The most visible cases were F5, F11, F22, F29, F37, and F38. No ungrounded reconstruction cases were found.

The failure shape suggests a very strong plan whose implementation and test architecture carried much of the intent, but whose reconstruction compressed some affective rationale into operational safeguards.
