## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 33/54 weighted points (61.1%), while affective whys recovered at 44/94 (46.8%). The server/presence architecture carried intent better than relationship-tone exceptions.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered better (60/108 = 55.6%) than weight-2 whys (17/40 = 42.5%), mostly because the plan/reconstruction preserved load-bearing mechanisms even when later layers were lost.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System fidelity (60.7%) beat feature-level fidelity (50.0%). The reconstruction preserved product philosophies more readily than exact feature rationales.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary mechanisms were usually retained; downstream consequences were frequently dropped, especially F2, F4, F10, F15, F20, F27, and F40.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Presence/sync performed best (F1, F13, F17). Social/account affective whys leaked most (F23, F29, F33, F34, F35, F39).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Rule-only rows were common: F6, F8, F19, F22, F23, F29, F31, F32, F33, F34, F35, and F39 preserved mechanics without the gold rationale.

What this suggests: the plan is an excellent builder-facing implementation spec, but not a fully faithful intent carrier for non-obvious affective exceptions.
