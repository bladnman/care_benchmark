## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 50.0/54.0 (92.6%); affective whys recovered 89.0/98.0 (90.8%). The gap is small. Functional losses came from F16 and F35 missing one layer each; affective losses clustered in rule-without-why rows such as F19, F23, F29, and F38.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 104.0/108.0 (96.3%), while weight-2 whys recovered 35.0/44.0 (79.5%). The candidate and reconstruction preserved the big load-bearing invariants better than smaller exception rationales.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was 96.4%, feature-level fidelity 90.3%. The plan preserved philosophy strongly; the reconstruction missed restraint-over-richness as an explicit system-level principle and a few feature-specific whys.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Most layer losses were secondary/downstream omissions rather than primary-cause misses. F16 lost the retrofit consequence; F35 lost the relationship-regret grace-window layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, audio, accessibility, and privacy/sync were especially strong. Social/onboarding rationale leaked more often: F23, F29, and F38 survived as rules but not as why recoveries.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The v06 gate denied credit where the reconstruction kept the mechanism but not the rationale: F19 matter-of-fact sync errors, F23 no leaderboards/discovery, F29 starter birds not catalog choices, F38 visit log as transparency, and S4 restraint-over-richness.

The failure shape suggests a high-end planner that encoded nearly all product behavior structurally, plus a reconstructor that was excellent at central invariants but still compressed some targeted headroom rationales into operational rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted headroom additions. F29-F40 exposed real headroom even on a very strong plan; F29 and F38 would have looked fine under mechanism-only scoring but fail the why-recovery test.
- Add a small scoring prompt reminder that system-level intent includes scope/restraint principles. S4 was present throughout the PLAN but not named by the reconstruction as a system principle.
- Preserve the evidence-bound distinction between plan capture and reconstruction recovery. It prevented PLAN-only or rule-only cases from inflating fidelity.
- Keep the current cross-cutting bar, but require scorers to document when a principle is cross-cutting in PLAN yet not identified by B. S4 is the useful example here.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The phase prompt states this was a fresh-context phase-2B scoring run and the reconstruction was frozen. I did not modify `RECONSTRUCTION.md`.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run, so no variance estimate is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I marked five captures as borderline-inclusive: features 7, 69, 90, 104, and 116. Because all five were counted as captured, they do not create missed-feature exclusions; they only document semantic-equivalence calls.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 is the only case where PLAN preserved the principle cross-cuttingly but B did not identify it as a system-level why.
- **Confabulation cases.** No ungrounded reconstruction cases were scored. The reconstruction generally stayed close to the PLAN and used `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN` for many minor items.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Four feature-level rows and one system-level row were denied full recovery because evidence showed mechanism without why rather than recovered rationale.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** S4, F19, F23, F29, and F38 are the diagnostic mechanism-only cases.
