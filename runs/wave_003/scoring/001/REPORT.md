## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered somewhat better because server simulation, drift, no-LWW, and telemetry boundaries were stated concretely. Affective whys often had the rule but not the relationship-protecting rationale, especially F10, F11, F14, F23, F33, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 rows were rarely fully recovered unless the PLAN carried explicit calibration or risk text. Primary mechanics survived; downstream consequences were the common losses.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System philosophy survived better than feature-level rationale. S3, S8, and S9 were strong; S4 and S6 were plan-grounded but not identified by B.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 was usually easiest: precise presence, drift calibration, procedural audio, server tick. Layer 3 was most often dropped: product-collapse, staleness, economy, or retroactive-relationship consequences.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accessibility and audio were strongest. Social/notification and account lifecycle were weakest: many rules were present, but private-relationship or no-attention-loop reasons were missing.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The v06 gate denied plausible semantic credit for mechanism-only rows such as F16, F31, F34, and F35. F5 was the clearest ungrounded reconstruction.

The failure shape suggests a planner that covers surface area very well but often compresses why into engineering rules. The reconstruction mostly stayed faithful to that compression instead of inventing missing rationale.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom whys like F29-F40; they exposed rule-without-why gaps in identity, export, deletion, visit logs, and visitor display.
- Add more rows distinguishing social transparency from social attention; this run captured visits but missed friend-visit notification and visit-log rationale.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for high-weight whys; downstream-consequence loss was the most informative signal.
- Consider asking future reconstructions to label `rule` versus `why` explicitly to reduce ambiguity.
- Keep the cross-cutting appendix; S4 and S6 show a principle can be present in PLAN while absent from B.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I used only assigned phase-two files, PLAN, candidate metadata, run-001 timing, and frozen reconstruction. I did not read `prd/`, peer slots, other waves, or modify the reconstruction.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one scorer pass over one candidate run; no variance signal is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls were made for features 3, 4, 74, and 97. These modestly raise planning quality but do not grant why credit without evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 and S6 are the subjective cases: both are strongly present in PLAN but not named by B, so they receive partial system recovery.
- **Confabulation cases.** F5 was treated as ungrounded reconstruction: B supplied a plausible mood-readable rationale, but PLAN only provided behavior mapping.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F10, F11, F14, F16, F23, F31, F33, F34, F35, F38, and F39 are main mechanism-only losses.
- **Timing.** TIMING.json contained phase 1 and phase 2A only; no phase 2B timing was fabricated.
