## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys survived better: server-side continuity, presence qualification, no last-write-wins, and telemetry boundaries were repeatedly operationalized. Affective whys leaked more often when the plan had a rule but not the precise relational reason, especially F29-F34 and F38-F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys were not uniformly better. Core functional weight-3 rows such as F2, F17, F18, and F40 were strong, while affective weight-3 exceptions such as F30 and F31 fell to none because the reconstruction could not recover the deeper why.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was much higher than feature-level fidelity. The plan preserved philosophy as cross-cutting architecture, but feature-level rationale often collapsed to implementation rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** The most commonly lost layer was downstream consequence: silent drift corruption, a toast changing the product register, unlocks becoming an economy, or stable identity protecting remembered relationship.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync/audio/accessibility were strongest. Social/account-quality-of-life features were weakest because visit-log, visitor rendering, export, and deletion semantics were implemented more as endpoints than as relationship-preserving product decisions.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter operator denied plausible v1-style credit for rule-only rows: F22, F23, F29, F30, F31, F33, F34, F38, and F39.

The failure shape suggests a strong implementation planner whose compression preserved architecture and anti-gamification rules, but often dropped the subtle affective rationale that explains why those rules matter.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted F29-F40 headroom rows. They prevented saturation by exposing places where a planner can implement the feature while losing the product reason.
- Add more explicit guidance for system whys like S3 where the plan may preserve the principle in many surfaces, but the reconstructor may not summarize it in the system section.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for high-signal affective exceptions. The layer breakdown made it clear that downstream consequence was the fragile part.
- Retain the evidence-bound operator. It usefully separates feature capture from why recovery and makes honest `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN` outputs score correctly.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This phase-2B pass treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. The reconstruction itself reads plan-derived; see `VALIDITY_AUDIT.md`.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only, so there is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on #37, #43, #70, and #97. These calls affect planning quality but did not grant feature-level why credit unless reconstruction and plan evidence also supported the why.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The strict >=3 inheritance bar was easiest for S1, S5, S6, S7, S8, and S9; S3 was the main subjective call because PLAN had specific surfaces but B did not name specificity as the charm mechanism.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no clear ungrounded reconstruction cases. The reconstructor often said `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN`, which reduced scores but protected validity.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several operational rules survived without gold rationale; these are counted in `rule_without_why` rather than upgraded.
