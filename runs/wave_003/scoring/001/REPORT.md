## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 42.0% by weight; affective whys recovered 23.2%. Functional recovery was helped by explicit architecture for F2, F13, F16, F17, and F18. Affective recovery leaked badly when the rule was named but the relationship reason was not, especially F14, F27, F30, F31, F33, F37, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 36.0%, slightly better than weight-2 whys at 12.5%. The high-weight drift/sync/accessibility mechanics carried more rationale than many small exception rules.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (35.7%) beat feature-level fidelity (28.8%). The plan preserved broad architecture and accessibility posture better than specific exceptions such as no toast, no editable notebook, visit log transparency, or no-show-off visits.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary/mechanism layers survived most often. Secondary calibration layers survived for F2, F13, F16, F17, F24, F40. Downstream consequence layers were usually absent, except F2 and partial implementation-consequence cases like F18, F25, and F40.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and performance captured many rules. Interactions and aviary layout leaked many product-feel exceptions: absence-length greetings, welcome-toast refusal, settle opt-in, quiet loading field, no UI chrome, and top-bar behavior. Social captured invite/read-only mechanics but missed notification/log/show-off rationales.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The strict operator mattered. Several v1-style plausible calls stayed at none because either the reconstruction said NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN (F15, F30, F31) or the plan/reconstruction only had rules without whys (F5, F6, F14, F20, F21, F23, F26, F27, F32-F34, F36-F39).

What the failure shape suggests: the candidate produced a serviceable implementation skeleton, but its plan compressed product judgment into feature names. The downstream reconstruction could recover architecture and a few calibrated mechanics, but not the affective product contract that explains why many obvious features must be refused.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the F29-F40 targeted headroom set. This run shows those additions are useful: F30 and F31 were implemented as rules but explicitly unrecovered as whys, while F36-F39 exposed privacy/social rationale loss.
- Preserve the evidence-bound ledger. Without it, rules like no gamification, aggregate telemetry, reduced motion, and read-only visits would look stronger than they are. The ledger makes rule-without-why visible.
- Add more exception whys around social, privacy, and onboarding. This candidate captured opt-in visits and age-based additions but lost the non-obvious reasons those rules exist.
- Keep the system cross-cutting bar, but require scorers to document plan-only partials. S4 scored partial mainly because the plan constrained scope in several places despite weak reconstruction evidence.
- Consider a small diagnostic for "named feature only" capture. This plan often named a feature (Settle, Offer, Field Notebook) without enough product behavior; the current capture rubric handles this, but a named-only flag would make reports easier to compare.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The frozen reconstruction appears scoped to PLAN only: no gold IDs were present, headings followed plan structure, and no PRD/gold taxonomy leaked in.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only; there is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on several planning calls per rubric: 2, 9, 10, 15, 18, 24, 45, 76, 85, 90, 92, 93, 97, 98, and 115. These did not materially inflate fidelity because evidence-bound why recovery remained conservative.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The hardest calls were S4 and S8. S4 got plan-only partial credit from multiple restraint rules; S8 got partial only for a narrow telemetry/privacy statement and failed the 3-feature cross-cutting bar.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded-reconstruction cases were counted. Some reconstruction prose was inferential, but it stayed close enough to the plan to treat as plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The largest methodological effect was denial of mechanism-only recoveries. There were 36 rule-without-why cases and 1 plan-only-not-reconstructed cases in the included denominator.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing metadata existed for phase 1 and phase 2A only. The score JSON includes only those bounded fields and omits unavailable phase 2B duration fields.
