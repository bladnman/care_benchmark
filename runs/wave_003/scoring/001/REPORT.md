## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered **62.5%** (30.0/48.0); affective whys recovered **45.7%** (42.0/92.0). The difference is small, but affective feature exceptions such as F8, F22, F23, F29, F31, F32, F37, F38, F39, and F40 were often preserved as rules without the relationship-shaping why.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered **50.0%** (52.0/104.0) versus weight-2 whys at **55.6%** (20.0/36.0). High-weight items frequently got partial credit because the primary mechanism survived while the downstream consequence disappeared.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The system-level score (**64.3%**) is notably higher than the feature-level score (**48.2%**). The plan carried philosophy through broad architecture, but feature-local rationales were compressed into requirements.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers survived most often. Secondary layers survived when they were implementation-adjacent, such as F18 no-last-write-wins or F25 reduced motion. Downstream consequences were the most common casualty: S1, S2, S6, S7, S9, F2, F3, F4, F9, F10, F16, F17, F24, F27, and F30 all lost their final consequence layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync did relatively well on concrete architecture (F16-F18, F36) but missed account export/deletion entirely (F34-F35). Accessibility preserved the designed-surface concept (F24-F26) but failed to reconstruct the slow narration cadence why (F40). Social features preserved boundaries but lost privacy/relationship rationales (F22-F23, F37-F39).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several apparent successes were denied full credit because they were mechanism-only: F8 hidden trait numbers, F22 private visit log, F23 no leaderboards, F29 starter auto-selection, F31 stable IDs, F32 mood state, and F40 narration cadence. F12 is the cleanest `plan_only_not_reconstructed` case: the plan had sparse naturalist notebook material, but the reconstruction marked Field Notebook `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN`.

The failure shape suggests a strong implementation planner with a tendency to compress affective rationale into non-goal lists and architectural constraints. That yields high capture and decent system fidelity, but it weakens the handoff for why-sensitive product details.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It exposed meaningful gaps that a looser semantic scorer would have over-awarded, especially F8, F22, F23, F29, F31, F32, and F40.
- Add more targeted headroom around quiet-social and accessibility cadence. This run built most social rules, but the whys for notifications, sharing, actual-view, and visit logs nearly vanished.
- Preserve multi-layer whys for high-risk product-feel features, but consider explicitly separating `mechanism layer` from `rationale layer` in reports. Many partials here are operationally useful but still weak as intent recovery.
- Keep the strict system-level cross-cutting bar. It was workable here because the plan repeatedly expressed principles across architecture, interaction, accessibility, and non-goals.
- In future instances, include several whys where the correct rule is counterintuitive but easy to implement mechanically. The strongest diagnostic rows here were exactly that shape: F8, F11, F29, F31, F33-F35, and F40.

- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on several compressed features: #15, #18, #28, #29, #31, #35, #57, #59, #63, #65, #76, #82, #85, #92-#94, and #97. These calls moved planning quality upward but did not inflate fidelity without reconstruction evidence.

- **Confabulation cases.** I did not count any ungrounded reconstruction cases. Some reconstruction language is inferential, such as `scene-centered`, but it is well supported by the plan's structure.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Single-layer feature whys with only rule evidence scored none. Notable examples: F8, F22, F23, F29, F31, F32, F37, F38, F39, and F40.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Twelve included rows were marked rule-without-why; they are concentrated in hidden-stat, social, identity, starter-bird, mood-continuity, and narration-cadence decisions.
