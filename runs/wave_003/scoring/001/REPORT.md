## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was slightly stronger: 38/54 = 70.4%, versus affective 66/98 = 67.3%. The planner and reconstructor were best where a rationale was architectural, such as S7/F17/F18/F20, and leakier where the reason was affective register, such as F10, F19, F22, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys survived better (78/108 = 72.2%) than weight-2 whys (26/44 = 59.1%). The high-weight rationales often had multiple implementation hooks, which gave the reconstructor more to recover.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level recovery (71.4%) and feature-level recovery (67.7%) were close. The reconstruction preserved broad product identity well, but feature-level exceptions often degraded into rule-only rows.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary-cause layers survived most often. Secondary affective contrasts were the common losses: announced-at vs noticed in S2/F10, naturalist charm becoming evasive in F19, screen-reader queue overwhelm in F40, and stat-management depth in some privacy/social rows.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync/privacy was strongest. Social optional was the weakest: the rules for read-only visits, invite scoping, notifications, logs, and actual host snapshots were captured, but the social-affect reasons often did not reconstruct.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter v06 operator denied credit on nine rule-only cases. The main denials were F6, F10, F11, F19, F22, F29, F34, F38, and F39.

The failure shape suggests a high-capacity planner that preserved product mechanics and many architectural whys, while compressing some delicate affective rationale into broader "no engagement economy" and "naturalist observation" themes.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted headroom additions. F29, F34, F38, and F39 did useful work: their mechanisms were easy to plan, but their deeper whys did not survive.
- Add or preserve more social-affect feature whys. The social area exposed the largest rule-without-why gap despite complete feature capture.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for high-weight whys. It prevented over-crediting broad but incomplete recoveries such as F1, F4, F9, F12, F13, F14, F16, F35, and F40.
- Consider adding one or two explicit evidence examples for system-level partial credit in the rubric. S4 and S5 were real partials where the system idea survived, but only as a subset of the full principle.
- Do not loosen the evidence gate. The strict requirement separated feature capture from rationale carry-through cleanly in this run.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The run was scored from frozen `RECONSTRUCTION.md`; I did not modify it or consult PRD/peer slots. Same-context leakage cannot be independently proven from inside this slot, but the file itself does not show strong contamination signatures.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one candidate plan and one reconstruction; no variance signal is available from this slot alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on #1, #7, #44, and #97. These calls did not affect feature-level reachability except #44 and #97, whose why recovery still scored none.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The 3-feature bar was clear for all S1-S9 in the plan. The subjective calls were about reconstruction completeness, not plan preservation.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not score any recovery as confabulated/ungrounded; some reconstruction phrasing was inferential, but the scored full/partial rationales had plan support.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rule-only mechanisms were the biggest v06 effect, especially no-toast/no-notification/social-log/account-export cases.
- **Operational compromise.** `TIMING.json` contained phase1 and phase2a data for run 001 but no phase2b timing at scoring time, so the score JSON includes only the available bounded timing fields.
