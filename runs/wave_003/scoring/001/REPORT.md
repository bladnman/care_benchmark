## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better: 23.0/42.0 (54.8%) versus affective 22.0/72.0 (30.6%). Server authority, drift calibration, and telemetry boundaries were more recoverable than relationship-protecting affective refusals.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys did better (38.0/88.0) than weight-2 whys (7.0/26.0), mostly because the plan repeated drift/server/accessibility architecture.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (46.4%) exceeded feature-level fidelity (37.2%). The plan preserved some philosophies globally, but feature-specific whys often collapsed into implementation bullets.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanisms were usually easiest to recover. Secondary temptation/failure-mode layers and downstream relationship consequences were most often dropped, especially in S2, F4, F9, F14, F23, F37, and F39.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync was strongest (S7, F17, F18). Accessibility was mixed (S9, F24, F25, F40 partial). Social and anti-gamification features were often captured as rules but not as relationship rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** 23 included rows were marked `rule_without_why`. The operator denied likely v1-style credit for mechanism-only rows such as F4, F5, F9, F16, F21, F23, F26, F31, F32, F37, and F39.

What the failure shape suggests: this candidate can build a coherent implementation skeleton from the PRD, but compresses many product-protective reasons into generic categories like "observational relationship-building," "not gamification," or "privacy boundary."

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom whys like F29-F40. They exposed missing rationale even when related mechanisms were present.
- Continue separating capture from recovery. This run captured 70 features, but only recovered 45.0 weighted why points out of 114.0 reachable.
- Add explicit diagnostics for announcement surfaces and social/privacy relationship boundaries; these are easy for planners to summarize as non-goals while losing the why.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting >=3 bar, but require scorers to distinguish "cross-cutting rule" from "cross-cutting rationale" in notes.
- Keep multi-layer judging for high-weight whys. The downstream-consequence layer was the clearest discriminator in this run.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** No same-context issue is visible from the artifacts. The reconstruction follows the plan's structure, not the gold list.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run for this candidate; no variance estimate is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on several features: 2, 15, 20, 24, 29, 40, 48, 56, 61, 76, 85, 90, 93, 97, 98, 103, and 104. These raised planning quality but not fidelity unless reconstruction evidence carried the why.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S7 was clearly cross-cutting. S1, S3, S5, S6, and S9 had enough repeated mechanisms but missing layers. S2, S4, and S8 did not meet the full principle bar.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no clear ungrounded reconstruction claims that required a confabulation penalty; the main issue was under-recovery.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several plausible semantic recoveries were denied because evidence was operational only, especially audio, greeting, social sharing, and performance-affect rows.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** The dominant pattern was mechanism without rationale: 23 included whys were marked this way.
