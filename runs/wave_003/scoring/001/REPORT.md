## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys survived better (42.0/46.0 = 91.3%) than affective whys (76.0/106.0 = 71.7%). The candidate preserved architecture sharply: S6/S7/S8, F2/F17/F18/F20/F36 are strong. Affective exceptions such as F11, F19, F23, and F39 often became rules without the relationship-level why.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys scored 88.0/112.0 (78.6%); weight-2 whys scored 30.0/40.0 (75.0%). High-weight whys survived slightly better, mostly because engine/accessibility invariants were explicit. Several multi-layer rows lost only the secondary or downstream layer.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (85.7%) exceeded feature-level fidelity (75.8%). The plan's organizing philosophy was highly recoverable, but the reconstruction compressed feature-specific affective rationales into broad invariants.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes usually survived. Secondary affective layers were the most fragile: S2's processed-vs-seen distinction, F14/F27's intention-rotation and cheap-engagement temptation, F30's unlock-economy erosion, and F40's visual-rhythm layer were all partial losses.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, privacy, audio, and accessibility were strongest. Social and product-tone exceptions were weaker: visitor/show-off, leaderboards/comparison, and sync-error clarity were mechanisms more than whys.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 evidence gating denied credit where a v1-style read might have been generous. F11, F19, F23, and F39 were captured as build rules, but their gold rationales were not recovered enough to score.

What the failure shape suggests: the candidate is an excellent implementation planner and a strong carrier of architectural intent. The remaining loss is mostly not "forgot the feature"; it is compression of relationship-level reasons into implementable invariants.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted affective exception whys like F11/F23/F39. This run shows that frontier plans can preserve the rule while flattening the relationship rationale.
- Keep multi-layer whys, but consider labeling layer types in scorer tooling, not in under-test prompts. The layer split exposed exactly where compression happened: secondary affective cause vs downstream product consequence.
- Preserve the strict evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated excellent planning coverage from thinner reconstruction of intent.
- For v2, add a small validation helper that computes row-level aggregates from the scorer ledger before writing JSON. This would reduce manual arithmetic risk without exposing qualitative content in loose score files.
- Consider one or two more social/privacy boundary whys. Social optionality produced several rule-without-why cases and remains a useful discriminant.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the frozen reconstruction as a fresh phase-2A artifact and did not modify it. The validity audit found no meaningful contamination signal.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run at the supplied candidate settings; it has no variance signal by itself.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on feature 1, feature 55, and feature 69. They did not affect feature-level reachability because none carries a feature-level why.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3 and S4 were subjective: the plan preserved them across many decisions, but the reconstruction did not identify them as named system principles, so they scored partial.
- **Confabulation cases.** F19 contains one rationale paraphrase ("pretend to be the aviary") that was not directly grounded enough for credit; it is counted as ungrounded reconstruction.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F11, F19, F23, and F39 are the clearest mechanism-only rows. Several partial multi-layer rows would likely have been over-credited under a looser rubric.
