## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered **30.0 / 48.0** weighted points, versus affective whys at **40.0 / 92.0**. Concrete architecture rationales survived better than relationship-shaping rationales.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered **52.0 / 100.0**, modestly better than weight-2 whys at **18.0 / 40.0**. High-weight items usually kept their primary mechanism but lost secondary temptation and downstream failure layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (**64.3%**) beat feature-level fidelity (**46.4%**). The plan transmitted architecture and broad product posture better than small feature exceptions.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** L1 mechanisms often survived: strict presence, server tick, no-LWW, procedural calls, age-based unlocks. L2/L3 consequences were frequently absent: stale-machine leakage, stat-management collapse, social attention loops, and downstream erosion of product identity.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync was strongest (F16-F18, F36). Social and affective product surfaces were weaker: F21, F23, F38, and F39 mostly survived as rules. Accessibility was moderate: equivalent prose and cross-fades survived, but deeper first-class-launch and queue-overload rationales were compressed.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter operator denied credit where a v1-style read might have been generous: F8 hidden vectors, F14 no streaks, F23 no leaderboards, F29 starter birds, and F31 stable identity all had implementation hooks but not enough reconstructed why.

What this suggests: this candidate is a strong implementer of visible requirements and concrete architecture, but it compresses the product's affective intent into bans and mechanisms. CARE's feature-level why surface is doing useful work here.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Preserve targeted headroom whys like F29-F40. They prevented the run from saturating on feature capture and exposed losses around identity, sharing, export/deletion, telemetry, and accessibility cadence.
- Add more downstream-consequence checks. The most discriminating losses were not primary mechanisms; they were what breaks if the mechanism is relaxed.
- Keep separating capture from recovery. Many rows were legitimately captured but still deserved no fidelity credit because the rationale was gone.
- Add clearer guidance for partial feature capture. Borderlines such as mood persistence, scene-load behavior, visit log, and voice/tone can swing planning quality without affecting why recovery.
- Consider a small subscore for named exceptions. Many losses involved exception logic: no visible stats, no welcome toast, matter-of-fact errors, no show-off visit mode.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The frozen reconstruction reads as plan-derived, and the validity audit verdict is PASS. No same-context contamination signs were found.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only, so there is no within-model variance estimate.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on partially specified features, including S-style product principles, mood persistence, scene loads, visit log, and top-bar/snapshot implications. This raises planning quality but does not award why recovery without evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The most subjective calls were S2, S4, S5, and S9, where several inherited rules existed but not all gold layers survived.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted. The reconstruction mostly stayed close to plan language.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The largest diagnostic bucket was rule-without-why, not plan-only-not-reconstructed.
- **Operational compromise.** I did not read PRD files, peer slots, or other waves; run label was left blank because the allowlist did not include wave metadata.
