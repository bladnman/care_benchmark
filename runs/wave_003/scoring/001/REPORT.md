## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better (42.0/54.0, 77.8%) than affective whys (64.0/98.0, 65.3%). Privacy, server authorship, presence accounting, and telemetry boundaries were especially durable. Affective exceptions such as F10, F23, F29, and F39 often collapsed to rule-only exclusions.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 76.0/108.0 (70.4%); weight-2 whys recovered 30.0/44.0 (68.2%). The high-weight items did not saturate because downstream consequence layers were often dropped even when primary mechanics survived.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved philosophy strongly and the reconstruction noticed it: system-level fidelity is 92.9%. Feature-level fidelity is lower at 64.5%, mostly because specific affective rationales were compressed away.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary-cause layers usually survived; secondary technical contributors often survived in functional whys; downstream consequence layers were the most common losses (for example F1, F2, F9, F30, F31).
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, privacy, accessibility, and canonical simulation were strongest. Social/product-affect features were more uneven: visits were mostly recovered, but public comparison and show-off-mode rationales were not.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied credit for mechanism-only rows including F4, F8, F10, F11, F23, F29, F34, and F39. No recovery was denied for ungrounded reconstruction.

The failure shape suggests that a strong plan can carry a large amount of product intent, but reconstructor compression preferentially drops the more literary relationship rationale behind small exclusions.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted feature-level affective exceptions. F29, F34, and F39 were useful headroom items because the mechanism was easy to preserve while the reason was not.
- For multi-layer whys, require per-layer evidence in the report as v06 does. The layer view showed that downstream consequences are the main compression loss.
- Consider adding a scorer calibration note for broad system phrases. S2 shows the difference between generic anti-engagement language and the exact noticed-vs-announced affective register.
- Keep the complete 120-feature denominator. Even a very complete plan missed four concrete feature rows, which would be invisible if scoring only the 40 why-bearing features.
- Preserve the strict split between capture and why recovery. This run demonstrates why: planning quality is excellent while fidelity is materially lower.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring turn used only the allowed phase-two materials, assigned plan and metadata, the slot-specific timing slice, and the frozen reconstruction. The reconstruction was not modified.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one sampled candidate plan and one frozen reconstruction; there is no within-run variance estimate.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on feature 7 (glossary-like concepts), 57 (evening palette), and 90 (visits default off). The missed rows were 41, 62, 87, and 88.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The (b) bar is documented in §2.2. Only S2 lost partial credit, because the reconstruction's broad anti-engagement phrase did not fully recover the noticed-vs-announced affective layers.
- **Confabulation cases.** None. Recovered claims were grounded in the plan; missing rationales were usually marked honestly or left as mechanism-only.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several v1-style plausible recoveries were denied because the exact rationale was absent from reconstruction evidence.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** 11 included feature whys preserved operational rules without the full gold rationale. These counted for capture but not full fidelity.
