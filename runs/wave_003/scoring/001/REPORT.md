## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better: 32/54 weighted points (59.3%) versus affective whys at 35/92 (38.0%). Server authority, drift calibration, and LWW prevention were concrete enough to survive; relationship reframings such as F29, F34, F37, F38, and F39 usually collapsed to mechanics.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 56/108 (51.9%), while weight-2 whys recovered 11/38 (28.9%). High-weight architecture whys survived because the plan repeated them in multiple sections.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (67.9%) was much higher than feature-level fidelity (40.7%). The planner preserved philosophy as broad constraints, while the reconstruction lost many per-feature exception rationales.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary causes were most likely to survive. Secondary comparisons and downstream consequences, especially "this becomes a different product" consequences, were most often dropped.
- **Subdomains.** Simulation/account sync was strongest (F2, F17, F18). Social and relationship-copy surfaces were weakest (F21-F23, F37-F39, F34). Accessibility was mixed: concrete surfaces survived, but equal-quality/charm rationale was partial.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Rule-only rows drove the gap: F6, F10, F19, F21-F23, F26, F29, F31-F35, and F37-F39 had build rules but not the gold why.

The failure shape suggests a planner that is good at implementable coverage and common architectural rationales, but weaker at preserving non-obvious affective product intent.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence ledger. It cleanly separated feature capture from why recovery and prevented rule-only credit from inflating fidelity.
- Add or retain targeted headroom whys around private relationship surfaces, because F29, F34, F37, F38, and F39 were all captured as mechanics but not as intent.
- Preserve multi-layer whys for architecture and affective exceptions. The downstream-consequence layer was the most diagnostic missing layer.
- Consider sharper guidance for borderline capture where a plan implies a rule through architecture, such as account hard deletion, visit default-off, or actual-aviary visitor rendering.
- Keep the system cross-cutting bar at three inherited decisions, but require reports to show the inheritance trail as this one does.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The reconstruction appears plan-derived. The validity audit found no gold ID leakage or rubric vocabulary.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run with no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Borderlines include features 2, 15, 18, 24, 30, 37, 52, 56, 57, 59, 63, 76, 81, 84, 85, 90, 92, 97, 103, and 104. These calls move planning quality by several points but do not alter the main fidelity finding.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The (b) calls were most subjective for S1, S3, S4, S8, and S9; the appendix lists the evidence used.
- **Confabulation cases.** No ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted. The reconstruction stayed close to PLAN wording and used honest NOT RECOVERABLE markers.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The largest effect was denying feature-level credit for rule-only rows, especially social, account, notebook, and no-toast/no-streak variants.
- **Operational timing.** TIMING.json contained phase 1 and phase 2A data for run 001 only; phase 2B timing was unavailable and omitted.
