## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 34.0/54.0 (63.0%); affective whys recovered at 60.0/98.0 (61.2%). The small aggregate gap hides a sharper qualitative pattern: functional architecture whys like S7/F17 survived well, while affective social and comparison whys like F23 and F37-F39 collapsed into rules or disappeared.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3-tier whys recovered at 66.0/108.0 (61.1%), a little below weight-2 whys at 28.0/44.0 (63.6%). High-weight whys often became partial because one or two layers survived while downstream consequence language was dropped.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner encoded system philosophy strongly enough for 78.6% system fidelity. Feature-level fidelity was lower at 58.1%, mainly because reconstruction often named the mechanism without the why.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary-cause layers survived most often. Secondary contributor and downstream consequence layers were the first to fall away, especially for F9, F10, F14, F16, F18, F20, F27, F30, and F31.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio, server simulation, privacy, first-frame performance, and accessibility did well. Social sharing, no-comparison surfaces, starter/adoption framing, and deletion/export nuance were less faithful.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several apparent v1-style recoveries were denied or capped: F8, F19, F23, F29, F30, F35, F37, F38, and F39 had rules or nearby mechanics but not the required rationale.

The failure shape suggests that the PLAN is excellent at carrying build constraints and architectural intent, but reconstruction compresses relational/affective reasons into enforcement mechanisms.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep v06 evidence-bound scoring. This run would look nearly perfect on feature capture alone, but the evidence ledger exposed a real intent-transfer gap.
- Add or retain targeted social/relational headroom whys. F37-F39 were valuable because they tested why private sharing must not become social discovery or a show-off surface.
- Consider making more easy-to-build affective exceptions multi-layer. F29 and F35 were single/three-layer probes where the rule is easy, but the relationship rationale is what matters.
- Tighten guidance for rule-without-why classification. S2, F10, F14, and F27 show that no-announcement/no-gamification clusters can be enforced while the emotional reason is only partially carried.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting bar. It usefully separated S7/S8/S9, where architecture and launch gates preserved the why, from S3/S4, where the PLAN carried the principle but the reconstruction did not name it.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The supplied workflow says the reconstruction was frozen before scoring. I did not modify it and did not read `prd/`, peer slots, or other waves.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only; there is no variance signal inside run 001.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on features 7, 44, 57, and 90. All four had enough PLAN grounding to build the intended behavior, so the planning score was not inflated by pure guesses.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The strict three-feature bar was applied using the appendix in section 2.2. S3 and S4 are partial because the PLAN preserved them but the reconstruction did not identify them as system principles.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear scored confabulation penalty was applied. The validity audit found the reconstruction broadly plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rule-only rows were kept in the denominator and scored none when the gold rationale was missing, including F8, F11, F19, F23, F29, F30, F35, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Operational compromise.** `TIMING.json` contained phase1 and phase2A timing only for run 001; phase2B timing was omitted from the score file rather than fabricated.
