## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was 36.8%; affective recovery was 38.9%. Functional sync/presence scaffolding survived, but privacy and telemetry rationale disappeared.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 42.9%; weight-2 whys recovered 23.1%. High-weight whys usually retained a primary rule and lost secondary/failure-mode layers.
- **System vs feature.** The plan preserved broad philosophies better than feature-level exceptions. S2, S7, and S9 were visible; F16, F22, F23, F31, F32, and F37 were rule-only or unrecovered.
- **Multi-layer pattern.** L1 survived most often. L2 calibration/temptation/data-boundary reasoning and L3 downstream consequences were common losses.
- **Subdomain pattern.** Accessibility and sync retained the strongest skeleton. Aviary layout, greetings, social nuance, privacy, telemetry, and account lifecycle were sparse.
- **Evidence-bound effect.** V06 denied likely v1-style credit where the plan named a mechanism but neither PLAN nor reconstruction carried the gold why.

What the failure shape suggests: this planner can retain a product skeleton and several absolute exclusions, but compresses affective and data-governance rationale aggressively. The reconstruction was mostly honest about non-recovery rather than filling gaps.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted headroom additions. They exposed meaningful differences between feature presence and why preservation, especially F29-F40.
- Preserve multi-layer whys. This run often recovered only the primary cause, so the layer split was diagnostic.
- Add or sharpen privacy/account lifecycle whys in future instances. S8 and F20/F34-F36 were almost entirely absent even though the sync architecture existed.
- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It prevented mechanism-only rows from inflating intent fidelity.
- Clarify broad-plan capture examples. Several inclusive calls are reasonable but subjective; future rubrics could show more examples for one-line compressed plans.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** Scoring treated `RECONSTRUCTION.md` as frozen and read-only. No edits were made to it.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run with no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls included: #21 Idle micro-motion (preen, scan, head-tilt, shuffle); #24 Bird naming (user-assigned at adoption; renameable); #28 Personality vector persistence (server-side, never resets); #29 Mood persistence across sessions; #37 No "Welcome back!" toast or banner; #38 Listen-in interaction (focus a bird; its call rises in the mix); #50 No streak counter, no "days visited" display; #53 Single horizontal scene (one screen, no panning); #63 No UI chrome inside the aviary view (icons live in a thin top bar); #76 Client pulls state snapshot on visibility; #79 Last-write-wins is forbidden for personality state; #89 Visit invitations (email-based, opt-in per invite); #90 Visits default OFF for new accounts; #92 Visitor cannot trigger greetings, listen-in, or offers; #94 No "your friend visited!" notification by default; #103 Reduced-motion mode preserves charm (not a stripped fallback); #104 Captioning toggle for procedural calls (text describes mood); #110 60fps idle motion target on 5-year-old laptop. These pushed planning quality upward but did not grant why recovery without evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 and S5 are the most subjective cases because rules survived while rationales were thin.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted. The dominant issue was missing rationale, not invented rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Mechanism-only recoveries were denied for F6, F8, F16, F22, F23, F26, F31, F32, and F37.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** 13 included whys were marked rule-without-why.
