## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 36.0/54.0 (66.7%), while affective whys recovered at 54.0/98.0 (55.1%). Functional architecture around server tick, sync, and telemetry survived better than social/relationship exception rationale.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 66.0/108.0 (61.1%); weight-2 whys at 24.0/44.0 (54.5%). Higher-weight items survived slightly better, but several targeted high-weight exceptions still failed hard: F30, F31, and F35.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved philosophy more effectively than per-feature why: system-level 26.0/28.0 (92.9%) versus feature-level 64.0/124.0 (51.6%).
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanism layers were often recovered; downstream consequence layers leaked first. Examples: F1 lost silent user-report fallout, F3 lost the return-after-absence trust consequence, F18 lost invisible drift deletion, and F27 lost the foothold/cascade consequence.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Server/sync, accessibility, and audio were strongest. Social sharing, visit logging, account lifecycle, and onboarding/growth rules were weakest because their rules were present but deeper affective/privacy rationales were not reconstructed.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Apparent v1-style recoveries were denied for rule-only rows such as F8, F10, F11, F29, F30, F35, F37, F38, and F39. The reconstruction frequently behaved correctly by writing `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN` instead of inventing rationale.

The failure shape suggests that the candidate plan is an excellent implementation scaffold but a lossy carrier for subtle per-feature product intent, especially where the rationale is social, affective, or exception-based rather than architectural.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom whys like F29-F40. They separated implementation completeness from rationale fidelity cleanly.
- Add more exception whys around social/privacy boundaries: per-invite sharing, visit logs, visitor actualness, and notification defaults all became rule-without-why cases.
- Retain multi-layer scoring for architecture and affective exceptions. It revealed that primary mechanisms were easier to carry than downstream consequences.
- Consider making `notice never announce` a more strongly probed system area. This run preserved the slogan and rules but dropped the processed-vs-seen affective register.
- Keep the system cross-cutting bar at three inherited decisions, but require scorers to document it; it was useful and mostly stable here.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** Scoring treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. Same-context isolation is accepted from the phase-2 runner setup.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one plan/reconstruction pair; no within-slot variance is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive capture was applied to presence signal detail, settle opt-in/no-penalty, settle undo, evening/night behavior, and top-bar/chrome boundaries. These raise planning quality slightly but do not grant recovery credit without reconstruction evidence.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2 was the main fine call: the PLAN inherited the no-announcement principle in several places, but the reconstruction mostly preserved the rule cluster rather than the affective reason.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction was counted. The reconstruction tended to mark uncertain features as `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN`.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rows with only rule evidence were held at none for feature-why recovery even when the implementation feature was captured.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Sixteen included feature whys were rule-only. This is the main diagnostic behind the low feature-level fidelity.
- **Timing.** TIMING.json contained phase 1 and phase 2A for run 001, but no phase 2B timing at score-write time; the JSON includes available bounded timing only.
