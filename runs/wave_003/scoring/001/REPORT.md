## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Planning vs. encoding:** The planner captured every feature. The lost points are why-recovery losses from the reconstruction, not feature omissions.
- **Affective vs functional:** Affective whys recovered better by weight (78.0 / 98 = 79.6%) than functional whys (36.0 / 54 = 66.7%). Functional misses concentrated in precise secondary layers: F1/F13 signal failure cases, F16 retrofit stakes, F18 silent lost drift, and F20 relationship-as-data-product.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2:** Weight-3 whys recovered better (84.0 / 108 = 77.8%) than weight-2 whys (30.0 / 44 = 68.2%). The highly repeated system architecture survived; smaller affective exceptions like F29 and F37 were easier to flatten into rules.
- **System vs feature:** System fidelity was 78.6%, slightly above feature fidelity at 74.2%. B recovered S1, S5, S7, S8, and S9 strongly, but did not elevate S3 charm-from-specificity or S4 restraint-over-richness as system principles.
- **Multi-layer pattern:** Primary causes usually survived. Secondary examples and downstream failure modes were most often dropped, especially when the plan encoded them as risk mitigation rather than in the local feature paragraph.
- **Subdomains:** Accessibility, audio, privacy boundaries, and server-canonical sync were strongest. Social/private-sharing exceptions and starter/adoption framing were weakest.
- **Evidence-bound effects:** Six rows were rule-without-why misses: F5, F6, F11, F23, F29, and F37. Two system rows, S3 and S4, were plan-preserved but not reconstructed as system-level principles.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound v06 operator. It separated feature capture from why transfer and made mechanism-only rows visible.
- Keep targeted headroom additions like F29 and F37. They are good probes for whether a plan protects non-obvious refusals against later relaxation.
- Consider requiring a compact plan-side "why ledger" for very long implementation plans. This run's plan carried many whys, but phase 2A compressed several secondary/downstream layers.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for high-weight whys. The downstream consequence layer was the best discriminator here.
- Revisit whether system-level S3/S4 should have additional feature-level probes in future instances; they were preserved in the plan but not recovered as named system intent.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity:** The reconstruction appears plan-derived. The validity audit found no gold ID leakage and no held-out taxonomy structure.
- **Single-run limitation:** This is a single run with no variance estimate.
- **Borderline capture calls:** None affected the planning score; all 120 features were captured under the semantic-equivalence standard.
- **System-level cross-cutting:** S3 and S4 were the subjective cases. Both were cross-cutting in PLAN but not identified by B as system principles, so they scored partial.
- **Confabulation:** No material ungrounded reconstruction was counted.
- **Evidence-bound denials:** V06 denied credit where only a build rule survived: F5, F6, F11, F23, F29, and F37.
- **Operational compromise:** TIMING.json had phase 1 and phase 2A timing for run 001, but no phase 2B timing at scoring time, so phase 2B timing was omitted from run_001.json.
