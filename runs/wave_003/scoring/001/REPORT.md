## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered strongly (48.0/54.0 = 88.9%). Affective whys were weaker (67.0/98.0 = 68.4%), especially rule-only refusals around social/gamified surfaces and onboarding.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered better (90.0/108.0 = 83.3%) than weight-2 whys (25.0/44.0 = 56.8%). The more architectural multi-layer whys were often easier to recover because the plan repeated them in implementation, tests, and risks.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (89.3%) outpaced feature-level fidelity (72.6%). The planner preserved the product philosophy, but feature-level rationale often became implementation rule text.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary implementation causes often survived; secondary and downstream affective consequences were the common loss. Examples: F3 kept monotonic drift but dropped the no-punishment/Tamagotchi relation; F30 kept not-visit-count rewards but dropped economy-collapse; F31 kept stable ID but dropped retroactive relationship loss.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, privacy, and accessibility were strongest. Social optionality, onboarding, and several quiet-product refusals were more rule-bound than rationale-bound.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Ten included whys were marked `rule_without_why`. There were no denominator exclusions among F1-F40 and no clear ungrounded reconstruction cases.

What this suggests: the plan is excellent at building the product and preserving many cross-cutting constraints, but the encoding still loses relationship-shaped affective reasons where the rule looks self-explanatory.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They found real differences inside an otherwise high-capture plan.
- Add or preserve more feature-level exception whys for social, onboarding, and anti-gamification surfaces, because those are where operational refusals most easily lose their reason.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for high-risk affective mechanisms. It distinguishes "rule kept" from "consequence understood" without needing more features.
- Keep the strict system cross-cutting bar. It correctly rewarded the plan's broad architecture while still surfacing partial compression in S2 and S3.
- Consider asking phase-1 plans to include a compact rationale trail for explicit non-goals and exceptions; this would reduce rule-without-why losses without bloating the build plan.
