## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was slightly stronger by weight (44/54 = 81.5%) than affective recovery (78/98 = 79.6%). The functional architecture around server ownership, additive drift, UUID privacy, deletion, and telemetry mostly survived. Affective losses clustered where the plan/reconstruction named a refusal but not the deeper relationship reason, especially F8, F23, and F29.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-2 whys scored 38/44 = 86.4%, while weight-3 whys scored 84/108 = 77.8%. The high-weight items often recovered the primary mechanism but dropped secondary or downstream consequence layers, which is exactly what the multi-layer rubric exposed.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (85.7%) beat feature-level fidelity (79.0%). The plan carried strong product philosophy and architecture, while several feature exceptions compressed into implementation rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers usually survived. The most common losses were L2 temptation/comparison/calibration details and L3 consequence language. Examples: S2 lost the processed-versus-seen rationale, F10 lost the well-meaning-toast temptation, and F18 lost the invisible lost-drift example.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, privacy, deletion, and telemetry were strong. Audio and accessibility were good but sometimes lost specific secondary warnings. Social sharing recovered invitation and visit-log boundaries, but no-leaderboards lost the public-comparison why.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Apparent v1-style recoveries were denied when only the rule survived. F8, F23, and F29 were the clearest rule-without-why rows.

What the failure shape suggests: this was an excellent implementation plan that preserved the product surface and much of the architecture. Its weakest encoding pattern was not missing features; it was compressing some affective exception rationales into negative acceptance tests.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound operator. It usefully separated a near-complete plan from imperfect rationale transfer.
- Add more targeted feature-level exception whys for common product-instinct reversals. F8, F23, and F29 were high-signal because they caught rules that survived without relationship rationale.
- Consider asking phase-1 planners to include a rationale sentence for every hard non-goal or irreversible refusal. This would reduce mechanism-only encoding without forcing full PRD prose into the plan.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for weight-3 whys. This run shows that primary causes are easy to carry, while secondary temptations and downstream failure consequences are the real headroom.
- Keep system-level cross-cutting, but add more consequence-oriented system whys like S2. S2 exposed the difference between "no toasts" and the deeper "noticed, not processed" affective register.
