## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys scored slightly higher by weight (38.0/54.0, 70.4%) than affective whys (66.0/98.0, 67.3%). The reconstruction was strongest when the PLAN encoded service boundaries directly, such as S7/F17/F18 and S8/F36. Affective headroom whys such as F29, F33, F34, and F39 often survived only as mechanisms.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** The two tiers were nearly tied: weight-3 whys recovered 74.0/108.0 (68.5%) and weight-2 whys recovered 30.0/44.0 (68.2%). High-weight whys did not strongly outperform because many multi-layer rows lost the secondary or downstream consequence while preserving the primary rule.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was much higher (85.7%) than feature-level fidelity (64.5%). The plan carried a coherent philosophy; the reconstruction retained that philosophy but compressed several feature-level exceptions into "what to build" rather than "why this exception matters."
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes survived most often. Secondary and downstream layers leaked first: F1/F13 lost the silent population-wide corruption consequence, F4 lost the chorus/recorded-loop artifact rationale, and F27 lost the future-foothold argument.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and telemetry recovered well where the rationale was architectural. Social and notebook whys were weaker: visits were captured as read-only and invite-only, but the relationship/private-sharing rationales were often reduced to authorization mechanics.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Seven rows were marked `rule_without_why`: F8, F19, F23, F29, F33, F34, and F39. These would look much better under a looser rule-preservation rubric, but v06 correctly withholds why recovery when the affective rationale is absent.

What the failure shape suggests: run 001 is not a planning-coverage failure. It is a compression failure at the rationale layer: the PLAN is unusually complete, but the reconstruction turns several relational/affective whys into bounded implementation rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted headroom rows for places where strong implementation plans commonly write the rule but not the relationship rationale: export, notebook editability, show-off mode, and hidden personality values were especially diagnostic here.
- Keep multi-layer whys. They exposed compression that a single full/none row would hide, especially for presence accounting, procedural audio, anti-gamification, and age-based offers.
- Consider splitting some broad system-level affective principles into narrower cross-cutting checks. S1 and S2 were recoverable, but the three-layer scoring still depended on judgment about whether scattered anti-spinner/anti-toast evidence was enough.
- Preserve the strict evidence-bound operator. The key signal in this run is not missing feature coverage; it is the gap between precise implementation rules and the deeper affective/relationship rationale.
- For future instances, include a few non-gold feature misses that are small but concrete (like support matrix or privacy-policy link) to keep planning quality from saturating when the main product architecture is extremely well covered.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the frozen reconstruction as read-only and scored only against the allowed PLAN, gold list, rubric, schema, templates, and the assigned timing/metadata. The same-context safeguard appears to hold from this scorer context.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one sample for the candidate/evaluator pair, so it gives no variance estimate.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive for visit social exclusions (#93) and captions (#104), because the plan's read-only visit model and caption/accessibility scope would guide a builder correctly. The four misses were #7, #62, #87, and #116; none affects feature-level why reachability.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The strict three-feature bar was met for all S1-S9. The most subjective calls were S1 and S2, where the PLAN is cross-cutting but the reconstruction compresses the downstream affective consequence.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no clear ungrounded reconstruction claims. The reconstruction generally stayed plan-derived and often marked honest non-recovery.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** v06 denied recovery for several mechanism-only rows even though the feature rule was accurately reconstructed, especially F8, F19, F23, F29, F33, F34, and F39.
- **Operational timing.** `TIMING.json` had phase1 and phase2a data for run 001, but no phase2b timing at scoring time; the score JSON includes only available bounded timing fields.
