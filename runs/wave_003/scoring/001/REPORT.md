## 3. Diagnostic patterns

The plan was broad and implementation-oriented: 82/120 features were captured, with strong coverage in architecture, sync, performance, accessibility, and explicit non-goals. The missing areas were mostly small but product-defining interaction and presentation details: greeting variation, settle semantics, notebook rarity/read-only behavior, top-bar behavior, account export/deletion, and several social/privacy subtleties.

Functional whys survived better than affective whys. Functional recovery was 25.0/48.0 weighted points (52.1%), while affective recovery was 28.0/84.0 (33.3%). The reconstruction recovered server authority, additive events, drift calibration, and aggregate telemetry more reliably than the relationship-shaping reasons behind no streaks, no leaderboards, no welcome toast, and read-only visits.

Feature-level rationales leaked before system-level principles. System-level fidelity was 53.6%, but feature-level fidelity was 36.5%. The plan often named the rule and the reconstruction repeated the rule, but the why was absent: F6, F14, F15, F16, F21, F23, F31, F32, F37, and F38 are the clearest mechanism-only rows.

Multi-layer whys usually lost secondary and downstream layers. F1 recovered the precise conjunction but not shortcut failure cases; F17 recovered server canonicality but not the divergent-client collapse; F30 recovered 'not usage metrics' but not growth-as-relationship-deepening or unlock-economy erosion. This is the dominant failure shape.

Evidence-bound scoring denied several plausible v1-style recoveries. The reconstruction used coherent language, but exact plan grounding often showed only a rule, not a rationale. I found no clear ungrounded-reconstruction cases; the issue was compression, not fabrication.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted F29-F40 headroom additions. They exposed meaningful gaps in stable identity, account export/deletion, visit-log transparency, and visitor actualness.
- Add or preserve affective exception whys. This run did much better on functional architecture than on reasons that protect the user relationship from familiar product patterns.
- Retain evidence-bound scoring. It separated genuine recovery from mechanism-only survival in a way the headline planning score could not.
- Clarify system-level partials where B misses the named principle but the PLAN carries cross-cutting rules. S2/S3/S4 are useful examples for a v2 scoring note.
- Consider additional social/privacy whys. The plan captured opt-in/read-only mechanics, but repeatedly missed why these surfaces must not become attention or publishing loops.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- Fresh-context fidelity appears to hold: the reconstruction contains no gold IDs or rubric vocabulary, and it mirrors the plan structure rather than the gold list.
- This is a single run, so there is no variance signal.
- Borderline capture calls were handled inclusively for broad no-notification coverage, weather, mood, and social-visit notification coverage. These calls lifted planning quality but generally did not inflate fidelity, because the whys were not recovered.
- System-level cross-cutting was the most subjective judgment. I required at least three visible inherited decisions for full PLAN preservation, but awarded partials where the plan preserved rule families without the full rationale.
- Evidence-bound denials were common: 18 included whys were marked `rule_without_why`. I found 0 plan-only-not-reconstructed cases and 0 ungrounded-reconstruction cases under this ledger.
