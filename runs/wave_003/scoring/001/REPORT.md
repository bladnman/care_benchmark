- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered much better: 63.0% versus 37.0% for affective whys. The reconstruction was strongest when the plan gave architectural causality, especially S7, F17, F18, and F36. It leaked affective product-rationale rows such as F8, F14, F23, F29, F30, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 50.0%, above weight-2 whys at 38.1%. High-weight technical whys survived; high-weight affective exceptions like F10, F14, F27, F30, F31, and F35 did not.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The system layer is much stronger (71.4%) than the feature layer (40.7%). The plan made enough cross-cutting architecture and voice/privacy/accessibility commitments for B to infer system intent, but many feature rows were reconstructed as what-to-build rather than why-it-matters.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary cause layers were most likely to survive. Secondary examples and downstream consequences often dropped: F1 lost the silent/no-test failure, F3 lost the returned-bird relationship consequence, F7 lost the deleting-the-bird rationale, F20 lost relationship-as-private-data, and F40 lost screen-reader queue overwhelm.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and simulation were the strongest areas because the plan encoded causality in implementation language. Social and onboarding were weakest: visit sharing, visit logs, actual-aviary viewing, and starter-bird presentation mostly survived as rules only. Accessibility was mixed: S9 and F25 were strong, while F24/F40 lost implementation-specific cautions.

Rows such as F5, F8, F14, F21, F22, F23, F27, F29, F30, F31, F33, F34, F35, F37, F38, and F39 preserve rules without why.

The failure shape suggests that the candidate writes a broad, buildable plan, but the encoding step compresses rationale aggressively unless the rationale is already attached to an architectural hazard.
