## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Both were strong, but affective recovery was slightly higher by weight (86.0/98.0 = 87.8%) than functional recovery (46.0/54.0 = 85.2%). Functional leakage concentrated in F1/F2/F16 plus F6; affective leakage concentrated in F4/F5/F9/F10/F14/F27.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-2 whys recovered better (40.0/44.0 = 90.9%) than weight-3 whys (92.0/108.0 = 85.2%). The added layer structure did useful work: the reconstruction often captured primary mechanisms while dropping secondary temptations or downstream collapse modes.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level intent was essentially intact (28.0/28.0). Feature-level fidelity was lower (104.0/124.0) because several specific whys were compressed into broader implementation rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** The most common dropped layer was downstream consequence: F1 missed silent population drift failure, F2 missed Tamagotchi/screensaver collapse, F4 missed audio as affective spine, F9 missed generic arrival-theater risk, and F10 missed the full no-variant welcome surface.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/privacy/sync and accessibility were especially strong. Audio and presence were functionally captured but lost some affective calibration rationale. Social recovered surprisingly well despite being optional.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter operator changed two likely v1-style calls: F5 and F6 were captured as mechanisms but denied why recovery because B did not cite the mood-legibility or recognizability-ceiling rationale. There were no ungrounded reconstruction cases.

The failure shape suggests a plan that carries implementation very well and system philosophy very well, but compresses several poetic or product-positioning consequences into enforceable rules.
