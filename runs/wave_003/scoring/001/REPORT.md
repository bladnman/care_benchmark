## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- Functional whys survived better than affective whys: 38.0/54.0 recovered functional weight versus 51.0/98.0 affective weight. The reconstruction was strongest where PLAN had architectural invariants like server-only ticks, UUID/PII separation, and append-only events.
- Weight-3 whys survived better than weight-2 whys: 66.0/108.0 versus 23.0/44.0. Multi-layer whys usually recovered the primary mechanism but often lost secondary temptation/failure-mode layers.
- System-level fidelity was high at 89.3%, while feature-level fidelity was 51.6%. The plan preserved the product philosophy more reliably than the reconstruction recovered the feature-specific emotional reasons.
- The most common multi-layer leak was Layer 2 or Layer 3. Examples: F1 lost the per-signal miss cases and silent failure; F4 lost chorus phase-cancel rationale; F27 lost the future slippery-slope rationale.
- Strong subdomains: server-side simulation/sync (S7, F17, F18) and accessibility narration (S9, F24). Weaker subdomains: social nuance and relationship-preserving exceptions (F23, F38, F39), identity affect (F31, F32), and observer-record notebook/export/deletion whys (F33-F35).
- Evidence-bound scoring mattered most for mechanism-only rows: F8, F10, F11, F23, F29, F31-F35, F38, and F39 were captured as rules but not as whys.

What the failure shape suggests: the PLAN is implementation-rich and broadly product-aware, but the reconstruction compresses many relationship-level reasons into engineering invariants. It is much better at recovering "what mechanism prevents breakage" than "what relationship would be changed if this exception were relaxed."

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more headroom around affective exceptions where a plan can easily implement the rule without the reason: stable identity, show-off mode, visit-log transparency, editable notebooks, and export/deletion as relationship rights.
- Keep multi-layer whys, but label secondary and downstream layers in scorer-facing gold more consistently; this run shows primary mechanisms survive while temptation/failure consequences disappear.
- Consider a binding-tolerant system-level scoring note for future rubrics. This reconstruction put S5-style voice rationale under per-feature sections, creating a judgment call about whether system-level recovery must appear in the system section.
- Add validator support for score JSON and HTML REPORT_DATA. The manual schema is clear, but a mechanical check would reduce variance in evidence_audit and timing-field handling.
- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It usefully distinguishes captured implementation from recovered intent, especially for F8/F23/F31-F35.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- Fresh-context fidelity: This phase-2B scorer treated the phase-2A reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. The audit found no material contamination signatures.
- Single-run limitation: This is one candidate run with no variance signal by itself.
- Borderline capture calls: #36, #37, #41, #64, and #104 were counted captured under RUBRIC section 4.7's inclusive capture rule. Counting them as misses would lower planning quality modestly but would not alter feature-why reachability for F1-F40 except F10 if #37 were treated differently.
- System-level cross-cutting: S4 was the main subjective call. PLAN preserves restraint across multiple decisions, but the reconstruction does not elevate it as a system principle, so it received partial credit.
- Confabulation cases: No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were found. The reconstruction often said "NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN" rather than inventing missing whys.
- Evidence-bound denials: Thirteen rows were rule-without-why cases, mostly relationship-protection exceptions and quiet social/account whys.
