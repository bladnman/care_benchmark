## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 40.0/54.0 (74.1%), while affective whys recovered at 60.0/98.0 (61.2%). The strongest functional cluster was server-side simulation and sync (S7, F17, F18). Affective losses clustered around relationship framing rather than rules: F29, F33, F37, and F39 preserve the mechanism but lose the relationship rationale.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 76.0/108.0 (70.4%), above weight-2 at 24.0/44.0 (54.5%). High-weight implementation whys were often spelled out by risk tables and tests; lower-weight affective exceptions were easier to compress away.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System fidelity (71.4%) slightly exceeded feature fidelity (64.5%). B reconstructed system themes well for server continuity, privacy, accessibility, and voice, but split S1-S4 into narrower product rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary implementation layers usually survived. Secondary affective explanations and downstream product-shift consequences were often dropped, especially in S2, F9, F10, F14, F30, F31, and F40.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Sync/accounts/privacy were strongest; accessibility was mostly strong but lost some cadence/continuity details. Social and notebook whys were weakest because the PLAN stated quiet rules more often than why those rules protect the relationship.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Twelve rows are `rule_without_why`; v06 denied credit where a looser scorer might have rewarded a preserved mechanism, especially F6, F11, F19, F23, F29, F33, F37, and F39.

The failure shape suggests a plan can be excellent as an engineering blueprint while still compressing away the precise affective negative space that makes Pocket Aviary distinct.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add targeted feature whys for rule-heavy affective exceptions, especially social sharing, notebook editability, starter onboarding, and show-off/public surfaces.
- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separates feature capture from why recovery and made rule-only survivals visible.
- Consider asking phase-1 planners to name rationale for each explicit non-goal, not only the non-goal itself. This would test whether relationship-protecting refusals survive as reasons, not just exclusions.
- Keep multi-layer whys for high-risk affective features. The layer breakdown showed that downstream consequence layers are the first to leak.
- Revisit system-level S1-S4 wording in future instances to reduce overlap: in this run, B preserved the behaviors but split the broader principles into narrower themes.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The same-context safeguard appears to hold for scoring. I did not spawn subagents and treated the reconstruction as frozen.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; no variance estimate is available inside this slot.
- **Borderline capture calls.** #7 and #69 were captured under the inclusive convention; #58 was not captured because the PLAN omitted the nightjar-like active bird detail.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The 3-feature bar was met for every system why in the PLAN, but S1-S4 B-identification required judgment because B used narrower labels.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear scored confabulation was found; several weak rows are rule-only rather than invented.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The main denials are mechanism-only rows where exact rationale evidence was absent or too generic.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing had phase 1 and phase 2A only; phase 2B timing was not present in `TIMING.json` at score-write time.
