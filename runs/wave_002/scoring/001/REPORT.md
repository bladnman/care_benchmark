## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 38/54 (70.4%); affective whys recovered 71/98 (72.4%). The split is close. Losses were concentrated in targeted affective exceptions such as F29, F33, and F39, plus multi-layer functional details like F1, F16, and F17.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 76/108 (70.4%), below the weight-2 rate of 75.0%. High-weight rows were demanding because many recovered the primary cause but dropped secondary/downstream layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level intent survived much better (89.3%) than feature-level rationale (67.7%). The plan's big philosophies were compact but visible; targeted per-feature whys exposed compression.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers usually survived. Secondary contributors and downstream consequences were the common losses, especially where the gold why explained future pressure, silent failure, or how a harmless variant would mutate the product.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Server canonicity, accessibility, privacy boundaries, and scene loading were strong. Starter/adoption framing, notebook-as-observer-record, show-off-mode refusal, and no-gamification future-pressure layers were weaker.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F8, F11, F29, F33, and F39 were mechanism-only: the reconstruction preserved the rule but not the gold rationale. No ungrounded reconstruction cases were found.

What this suggests: the candidate plan is an excellent implementation plan, but the plan-to-reconstruction channel still compresses some non-obvious product-relationship rationales into implementation rules.
