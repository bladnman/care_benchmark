## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 63.0% by weight; affective whys recovered at 55.1%. Functional architecture was especially strong around S7/F17/F18, while affective exceptions such as F10, F22, F23, F33, F38, and F39 often survived only as rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 64/108; weight-2 recovered 24/44. Multi-layer whys often recovered the primary rule but dropped the downstream consequence.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (78.6%) substantially exceeded feature-level fidelity (53.2%). The plan carried broad principles well; the reconstruction compressed many feature-level exceptions.
- **Layer pattern.** The most common lost layer was L2 or L3: examples include S1 staleness/leaving, F1 silent cross-account corruption, F9 notice-never-announce consequence, F18 silent lost drift, and F40 screen-reader queue overload.
- **Subdomain pattern.** Simulation/sync, accessibility rendering, and procedural audio were comparatively faithful. Social/account quality-of-life whys were weaker: export, visit log, show-off mode, friend-visit notification, and soft/hard deletion were mostly mechanism-only.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Thirteen included whys were rule_without_why cases. Most would look superficially preserved in a looser review because the feature appears in the plan, but v06 denies why credit without reconstruction evidence.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated 120/120 feature capture from only 57.9% intent fidelity.
- Add more targeted headroom around account/social quality-of-life surfaces. The run implemented export, deletion, visit log, and sharing, but their relationship/privacy/control rationales often disappeared.
- Preserve multi-layer whys, but add scorer examples for half-recovered downstream consequences. This run repeatedly recovered L1 while losing L2/L3.
- Consider adding a small rubric note for mechanism-only explicit non-goals. F10/F22/F23/F33/F38/F39 show how a plan can say no to a surface without carrying why.
- Keep system-level cross-cutting strict. It exposed that broad principles survived better than feature-level exceptions, which is useful diagnostic signal.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The frozen reconstruction is plan-structured, contains no gold IDs, and appears derived from PLAN rather than the gold list. Same-context safeguard is not independently provable from inside this slot, but the artifact passes the local contamination audit.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run for one candidate/temperature setting. There is no variance signal from this artifact alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on features 7, 52, 55, 64, and 93. Since all were counted captured, planning quality is 100.0%; a stricter capture read would lower planning modestly but would also exclude any affected feature whys from the fidelity denominator.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The three-feature bar was met for all S1-S9 in PLAN. Partial system scores come from missing reconstruction layers, not absent plan inheritance.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no clear ungrounded reconstruction cases. Partial scores were mostly omissions rather than invented rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F8, F10, F11, F19, F22, F23, F29, F30, F33, F34, F35, F38, and F39 were counted as rule_without_why, not recovered whys.
