## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys survived better: 25.0 / 54.0 weighted points (46.3%) versus affective whys at 30.0 / 92.0 (32.6%). The architecture-side material around S7, F16, F17, F18, and F36 had concrete plan grounding. Relationship-shaping whys around starter birds, social quietness, read-only notebook authorship, and no show-off visits often collapsed to rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 44.0 / 108.0 (40.7%), better than weight-2 whys at 11.0 / 38.0 (28.9%). The high-weight rows usually carried enough mechanism for partial credit, but rarely all three layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was 53.6%, while feature-level fidelity was 33.9%. The plan broadly preserved philosophies like server-side simulation, naturalist voice, and accessibility, but many feature-level exceptions lost their rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 rules were the most likely to survive. Layer 2 temptation/calibration explanations and Layer 3 downstream product-collapse consequences were frequently absent, especially in F2, F4, F15, F17, F25, F30, F31, F35, and F40.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, performance, and simulation had strong capture and moderate why recovery. Social and field-notebook whys had weaker fidelity because the plan named quiet/read-only mechanisms but not the relationship-protection reasons.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The strict v06 gate denied many plausible v1-style credits. F6, F8, F10, F11, F21, F23, F26, F29, F30, F31, F33, F34, F35, F37, F38, and F39 are rule-without-why cases.

The failure shape suggests a planner that can reproduce a broad implementation checklist, especially technical architecture, but compresses away the product's affective theory of why the checklist matters.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It separated capture from fidelity cleanly and prevented rule-only rows from scoring as recovered intent.
- Preserve and possibly expand targeted affective headroom whys. F29-F40 were useful: this run implemented many of those rules while losing their rationale.
- Add a few more non-obvious functional why rows around performance and observability. F26 showed that a metric can be captured without the felt-experience threshold that made the metric important.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar, but require explicit inherited-decision lists in every report. It made S4 and S5 more consistent to judge.
- Continue using multi-layer whys for high-value rows. Partial recovery exposed the exact layer most often lost: downstream consequence.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring pass used only the allowed phase-two files, assigned plan, run-001 metadata/timing, frozen reconstruction, and scoring output slot. No PRD, peer slot, or other wave was read.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run from one candidate configuration, so it provides no within-model variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on per-bird call signature, call timing by vocal-frequency, settle opt-in, evening palette shift, weather/mood, parallax subtlety, email magic-link no-passwords, no avatars, visitor actualness, and reduced-motion charm. These calls raised planning quality, but not fidelity unless why evidence existed.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The strict >=3 inherited-decision bar was applied. S4 and S5 are the most subjective: the plan has relevant surfaces, but the reconstruction did not recover the full rationale.
- **Confabulation cases.** None were scored as confabulation. Where B wrote `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN`, the row was scored as none.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several semantically plausible rules were denied recovery because neither the reconstruction nor the plan carried the gold why. The dominant class was rule-without-why.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 21 included cases, concentrated in social, account lifecycle, no-announcement/no-gamification details, and starter/identity features.
- **Timing metadata.** `TIMING.json` contained phase 1 and phase 2A timings for run 001 only. The score JSON includes those bounded fields and omits unavailable phase 2B / complete-run duration fields.
