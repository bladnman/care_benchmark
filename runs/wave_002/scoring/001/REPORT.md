## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better: 36/54 weighted points (66.7%) versus 54/94 affective points (57.4%). The plan and reconstruction were strongest where architecture itself carried the why: S7/F17/F18, F20, and F36. Affective exceptions often became rules without their relationship logic: F10, F14, F27, F33, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 62/104 (59.6%), slightly below weight-2 at 28/44 (63.6%). The higher-weight rows frequently kept their primary rule but dropped secondary or downstream consequences, especially F1, F3, F7, F24, F25, F30, F31, and F40.
- **System vs feature.** System-level fidelity was much stronger (22/28, 78.6%) than feature-level fidelity (68/120, 56.7%). The plan preserved philosophy better as architecture than as feature-local rationale.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and privacy were strong. Audio was also strong because the plan repeatedly tied procedural generation to avoiding dead loops. Interaction/social affective details leaked: return greeting was not planned, and visit/share mechanics often kept the mechanism without the interpersonal rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Fourteen included whys were marked rule-without-why. Two system rows, S3 and S4, received partial credit because the plan preserved the concern cross-cuttingly even though the reconstruction did not explicitly name the deeper rationale.

What the failure shape suggests: this candidate is a strong systems planner but a weaker rationale carrier. It preserves the architecture that makes the product safe and coherent, yet compresses many delicate affective rules into generic refusals.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted affective exceptions in v2, especially for interaction and social features. This run shows that rules like no toast, no streak, and no show-off mode are easy to carry while the relationship reason evaporates.
- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated strong architectural recovery from rule-only survival and prevented broad non-goal language from scoring as recovered intent.
- Consider adding a small reporting affordance for system-level plan-only partials. S3 and S4 were preserved cross-cuttingly but weakly reconstructed; that is useful signal and currently hides inside prose.
- Preserve the 3-feature cross-cutting bar for system whys. It was workable here and helped avoid over-crediting one-off principle mentions.
- For future Pocket-Aviary-like instances, include more feature whys around greeting/arrival and UI chrome; those gaps changed planning quality and exposed where broad plans skip delicate first-session behavior.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The prompt states this is fresh context, and no subagents or background agents were used. The frozen reconstruction was not modified.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run at the supplied candidate settings; there is no within-model variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Borderline captured calls include the glossary, per-bird call signature, evening palette shift, color palette, and no chat/avatar during visits. These did not change the main finding: the plan is broad but misses fine interaction/layout details.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3 and S4 were the hardest system calls: the plan carries naturalist/specific and restrained design choices, while the reconstruction does not explain the deeper rationale.
- **Confabulation.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction was counted. The reconstruction mostly uses plan vocabulary and often honestly says NOT RECOVERABLE.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rule-only denials are the major caveat: F5, F6, F10, F11, F14, F27, F33, F34, F35, F37, and F39 all had mechanisms without recovered whys.
- **Timing.** TIMING.json provided phase 1 and phase 2A data for run 001 only; no phase 2B duration was fabricated.
