## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 70.4% weighted fidelity, while affective whys recovered at 53.1%. The plan encoded functional architecture explicitly: strict presence, server tick, no LWW, PII-safe IDs, telemetry partitioning, and screen-reader queue throttling. Affective exceptions such as no streaks, no leaderboards, no catalog-like starter selection, and no show-off visit mode mostly survived as rules without their relationship-shaping rationale.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered slightly better (61.1%) than weight-2 whys (54.5%), but mostly because functional weight-3 whys were concrete. Affective weight-3 whys frequently lost layer 2 or layer 3.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved philosophy better than individual feature whys: S-level recovery was 78.6%, F-level recovery was 54.8%. The reconstruction cleanly identified high-level principles, then often converted feature-specific whys into build mechanics.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes often survived; downstream consequences were the common leak. S1, S2, S9, F4, F9, F10, F15, F16, F30, and F35 all lost consequential layers that explain why a small shortcut changes the product.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync were strongest (F2, F17, F18 full; S6/S7 full). Social and relationship-boundary features were weakest: F22, F23, F37, and F39 retained rules but not the attention-loop, comparison, privacy, or show-off rationales. Accessibility was mixed: narration cadence (F40) was strong, while first-class accessibility and reduced-motion charm lost deeper parity consequences.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The v06 evidence gate mattered. F5, F8, F11, F14, F22, F23, F26, F29, F34, F37, and F39 would look superficially covered in a rule checklist, but were scored none for why recovery because the reconstruction and plan lacked rationale evidence.

What this suggests: the candidate is a strong implementation planner and a decent principle preserver, but not a consistently reliable carrier of affective intent at the feature-exception level.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the targeted headroom additions. All 40 feature-why anchors were captured, yet the targeted whys produced substantial fidelity spread.
- Add a phase-1 artifact field that separates "rule/mechanism" from "rationale/consequence" for non-goals and exceptions. This directly targets the failure mode in F14, F22, F23, F29, F37, and F39.
- Preserve multi-layer whys and consider explicitly asking planners for downstream consequences. The most common leak was not primary-cause loss; it was the consequence that explains why a shortcut changes the product.
- Keep the current system-level cross-cutting bar. It was strict enough to expose partial S1/S2/S9 recovery while still allowing S6/S7/S8 to score cleanly when the architecture carried them.
- For future instances, include more affective exceptions that are easy to implement but hard to justify. This run shows those are the best discriminators once planning capture saturates.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The prompt states fresh context, and the validity audit found no gold IDs, rubric vocabulary, or heading mirrors. I did not inspect PRD files, peer slots, or other waves.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only; there is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive capture affected #31, #55, #67, #76, #90, and #103. Even changing all six to missed would leave planning high, but would alter the exact planning score.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The three-feature bar was most subjective for S1, S2, and S9. They were cross-cutting in the plan, but multi-layer recovery remained partial because downstream rationale was not reconstructed.
- **Confabulation cases.** None were counted. The reconstruction stayed close to the plan, sometimes too close to its rule/mechanism phrasing.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Eleven rule-only cases were denied fidelity credit despite feature capture: F5, F8, F11, F14, F22, F23, F26, F29, F34, F37, and F39.
- **Timing.** TIMING.json had run 001 phase1 and phase2a entries only; score JSON includes only those bounded timing fields and does not fabricate phase2b timing.
