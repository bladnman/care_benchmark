## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered better (73.0/98.0) than functional whys (32.0/54.0). The plan/reconstruction preserved product refusals and accessibility tone well, but precision-functional rationales such as F1/F13 presence-accounting and F6 the empirical cap rationale fell out.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys did better (78.0/108.0) than weight-2 whys (27.0/44.0). The load-bearing rules were often named, but social sharing exceptions (F37-F39) were thin despite being simpler single-layer whys.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System fidelity was strong at 82.1%, while feature fidelity fell to 66.1%. The planner encoded philosophy cross-cuttingly, but the reconstructor frequently compressed feature exceptions into mechanisms.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanics survived most often. Secondary affective contributors were the common drop: S2's processed-vs-seen layer, F9's absence/boldness/procedural variation, F20's private-relationship-as-not-data-product layer, and F35's privacy layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio, accessibility, server-side simulation, privacy boundary, and no-gamification were strong. Presence precision, bird-count recognizability, account export, and social-visit rationale were weaker.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied credit for plausible mechanism-only rows: F1/F13 had presence rules but not the drift-corruption why; F22/F23/F37-F39 had social constraints without the relationship/attention-loop rationale; F34 explicitly said the reason was not recoverable.

The failure shape suggests an excellent implementation plan with high surface coverage, but a reconstruction that sometimes summarized the plan's rules instead of carrying the exact why behind exceptions.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted social and privacy headroom whys like F37-F39. This run shows social mechanics are easy to encode while the private-relationship rationale is easy to compress away.
- Keep multi-layer whys for high-impact exceptions. They exposed meaningful partials in S2, S6, F9, F10, F14, F20, F30, and F35 that a single full/none call would flatten.
- Consider a distinct diagnostic for mechanism-only reconstruction. `rule_without_why` was highly informative here, especially for F1/F13 presence accounting and F22/F23 social refusals.
- Tighten guidance on system-level partials when the PLAN clearly preserves a principle but B misses it. S4 is a useful example: the implementation is restrained, but the reconstruction does not recover the product-level why.
- Preserve the evidence-bound requirement. It prevented over-crediting rows where the plan was excellent but B said `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN` or reconstructed only the rule.
