## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered slightly better (67.3%) than functional whys (63.0%). Functional misses concentrated in downstream architecture rationales: F16 retrofit cost, F17 divergent client simulations, and F35 deletion rationale.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 66.7%, modestly above weight-2 at 63.6%. The high-weight entries carried more explicit implementation hooks, but often lost layer 3.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System philosophy survived strongly (92.9%), while feature-level rationale was much weaker (59.7%). The plan encoded broad principles better than the reconstruction could recover every local exception.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes survived most often; downstream consequences leaked. Partial rows repeatedly recovered the rule and immediate reason while dropping the product-collapse consequence.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio, load/performance, reduced motion, and no-LWW sync were strong. Social/account lifecycle rationale was weaker: F22, F23, F34, F35, F37, and F38 were rule-only or not recoverable.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Ten included feature whys were marked `rule_without_why`; v06 denied credit where a v1-style read might have rewarded named mechanisms.

Overall, the candidate plan is excellent at feature coverage and system principles. The measurable weakness is intent compression at the feature-exception layer.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It exposed the difference between comprehensive planning and rationale transfer rather than letting feature coverage hide why loss.
- Add more targeted headroom rows like F29-F40. This run's discrimination came from local exceptions whose implementation was easy to include while their deeper reason vanished.
- Add a downstream-consequence diagnostic for multi-layer whys. The recurring loss pattern was layer 3, not primary implementation mechanics.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting bar, but require scorers to call out when a system row is partial despite broad plan inheritance. S6 is the example here.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring turn used only the allowlisted phase_two files, slot 001 PLAN/metadata/timing, and frozen RECONSTRUCTION. The PRD, peer slots, and other waves were not read.

- **Borderline capture calls.** Borderlines were leaned inclusive: distributed glossary (#7), settle opt-in (#44), background-tab pause (#51), visits default-off (#90), and no avatars during visits (#93). These did not materially affect the headline because the plan was saturated.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The main subjective call was S6: presence precision was clearly preserved, but settle/tab-close equivalence was not fully reconstructed as a layer.
- **Confabulation cases.** No ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rows such as F8, F11, F14, F22, F23, F29, F34, F35, F37, and F38 preserved rules without enough gold-rationale evidence.
