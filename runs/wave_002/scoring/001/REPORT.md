## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 66.7%, while affective whys recovered at 49.0%. Server-side simulation, no LWW, privacy boundaries, and presence precision were the strongest areas. Affective relationship-protection whys such as F14, F22, F23, F29, F31, F33, and F35 often collapsed to operational bans.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 55.6%, only slightly above weight-2's 54.5%. High-weight architecture whys did well, but high-weight affective exceptions often kept only their first layer.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (71.4%) was materially higher than feature-level fidelity (51.6%). The plan gave the reconstructor enough to recover broad architecture and product posture, but not enough to recover many local, product-defining exceptions.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers were most likely to survive. Secondary temptation/risk layers and downstream product-corruption consequences were often missing, especially in F9, F10, F14, F27, and F30.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and simulation were strongest. Social/account settings carried most rules, but their whys were thin. Accessibility was mixed: screen-reader narration and cadence were strong, reduced-motion rationale was only partial.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The evidence gate denied credit for mechanism-only cases such as F5, F8, F11, F19, F22, F23, F29, F31, F33, F34, and F35. S3 and S4 were plan-only system principles: present in the plan but not reconstructed as named cross-cutting intent.
- **Anomaly.** The plan says personality vectors are hidden, but its snapshot sample exposes trait scalars. That did not change capture for F8, but it prevented any confidence in the hidden-vector why.

The failure shape suggests a planner that is excellent at building a complete implementation plan, good at carrying technical rationales, and weaker at encoding why affective constraints must remain constraints under future pressure.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound ledger. It surfaced the difference between broad feature capture and actual intent recovery.
- Add more targeted exception whys around social/account settings. This run built the surfaces but often missed the reason those surfaces must not become growth loops or data products.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for high-risk affective features. The strongest diagnostic came from seeing L1 survive while L2/L3 disappeared.
- Consider adding an explicit contradiction marker in future rubrics. F8 shows a plan can both state a rule and violate it in an API example.
- Keep the strict system-level cross-cutting appendix. It made S3 and S4 visible as plan-carried but reconstruction-dropped principles instead of silently awarding or denying them.
