## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 30/54 weighted credit (55.6%); affective whys recovered at 48/96 (50.0%). Functional architecture was strong where server ownership and telemetry boundaries were explicit, but functional relationship-rationales such as F34 and F35 still dropped out.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-2 whys recovered better (28/42, 66.7%) than weight-3 whys (50/108, 46.3%). The high-weight rows usually preserved the primary mechanism but lost secondary failure modes.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner preserved philosophy better than specific whys: system-level fidelity is 71.4%, feature-level fidelity is 47.5%. The reconstruction could restate plan principles, but feature intent often collapsed into implementation.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers survived most often. Secondary contributors and downstream consequences were the common leaks: examples include F1, F2, F3, F4, F7, F13, F16, F17, F18, F25, F30, and F40.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio, loading, server tick, and accessibility narration were comparatively faithful (F15, F24, F26, F36). Identity, export/deletion, social comparison, and onboarding whys were weaker (F29, F31-F35, F39).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F8, F14, F19, F23, F27, F29, F31, F32, F33, F34, F35, and F39 were mechanism-only or NOT RECOVERABLE cases. They counted for capture where the feature existed, but not for why recovery.

The failure shape suggests a strong engineering plan that retained surface coverage and architectural constraints, but compressed many product-intent rationales into rules. The benchmark is doing useful work here: the plan looks highly complete at the feature layer while only about half of reachable weighted intent survived through reconstruction.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated broad implementation coverage from actual why recovery in this run.
- Add or preserve targeted headroom whys around identity, export, deletion, social privacy, and onboarding. These were the clearest places where the plan named a rule but the reconstruction lost the reason.
- Consider requiring reconstructors to label mechanism, rationale, and failure mode in their own words. That could improve auditability without leaking gold IDs or the gold list structure.
- Keep the current system-level cross-cutting bar, but continue documenting the feature/architecture inheritance trail. In this run the bar was usable, though layer recovery remained subjective for S1, S2, S6, and S9.
- Do not loosen single-layer feature scoring. Several tempting semantic matches were correctly denied because the reconstruction preserved only the operational rule.

---

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The same-context safeguard appears to hold. The validity audit found no gold IDs, rubric vocabulary, or 1:1 gold-list mapping in the frozen reconstruction.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only. It provides no variance signal for candidate model behavior.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive capture calls included settle definition, bird naming/renameability, vector numerical exposure, greeting by boldness, offer reaction variation, field notebook read-only, click-anywhere undo, bird-chosen perch, evening palette, and per-device session revocation. These raised planning quality but did not grant why recovery.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The cross-cutting judgment was strongest for S3-S5, S7, and S8. S1, S2, S6, and S9 were partial because the reconstruction did not recover all layers.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted. The reconstruction mostly stayed close to plan language.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Many v1-style apparent recoveries were denied because reconstruction evidence carried only the mechanism. This particularly affected F8, F14, F19, F23, F27, F29, F31-F35, and F39.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing data had phase 1 and phase 2A only; phase 2B timing was not present in the ledger at write time.
