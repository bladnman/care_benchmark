## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered better overall: 77.0 / 98.0 versus 38.0 / 54.0 for functional whys. The candidate plan repeatedly named affective guardrails, while functional partials often dropped calibration/failure consequences in F1, F2, F16, and F18.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 rows recovered at 80.0 / 108.0, lower than weight-2 rows at 35.0 / 44.0. The common loss was the downstream consequence layer.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was very high: only S4 was partial because B did not identify restraint-over-richness as its own principle. Feature-level fidelity was lower because many multi-layer feature whys were compressed to mechanism plus immediate rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** L1 was usually recovered. L2 often survived when it was operational. L3 was most often lost: silent drift corruption (F1/F13), Tamagotchi-vs-screensaver collapse (F2), invisible deleted drift (F18), and economy erosion (F30).
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accessibility, telemetry/privacy, and first-frame behavior were strongest. Small product-shape exceptions were weakest: F29 starter birds as arrivals, F34 export as relationship copy, and F39 no show-off mode.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Four captured features preserved the rule but not the why: F5, F29, F34, and F39. No ungrounded reconstruction was found.

What this suggests: the plan is an excellent implementation carrier, but the reconstruction's plan-shaped summary loses some deeper rationale when the plan expresses a feature as architecture or release gates rather than as explicit product psychology.
