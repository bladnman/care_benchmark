## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was stronger (59.3%) than affective recovery (60.4%). The plan and reconstruction were especially good at server ownership, additive sync, privacy boundaries, and telemetry; relationship-protecting affective whys such as F23, F31, F33, F37, and F39 often collapsed into rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 61.1%, slightly better than weight-2 whys at 57.1%. Multi-layer rows often preserved primary mechanism but lost downstream consequence.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (78.6%) outpaced feature-level fidelity (55.7%). The plan encoded philosophy structurally, but the reconstruction often compressed feature-level reasons into implementation summaries.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** The most commonly dropped layer was downstream consequence: silent drift corruption (F1/F13), generic arrival animation (F9), client-simulation collapse (F17), and accessibility-monitoring rhythm (F40).
- **Subdomain patterns.** Sync, telemetry, privacy, and performance were strong. Social and notebook rules were implemented well but often lost the relationship rationale. Accessibility system-level intent was strong, while narration/reduced-motion feature whys were partial.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F6, F8, F23, F26, F31, F33, F34, F37, and F39 were rule-without-why cases. F34 was especially clean: B wrote `NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN`, so the score is none even though the feature itself was captured.

What this suggests: this candidate is an excellent implementation planner with strong architectural preservation, but the plan compresses many affective rationales into mechanisms. A downstream agent can rebuild the system shape, yet not always explain why a specific relationship-protecting exception exists.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound operator. This run shows why: rule survival and why survival diverged sharply.
- Preserve targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They exposed losses in stable identity, account export, read-only notebook, per-invite sharing, and visitor actual-aviary rationale that broad system whys would have hidden.
- Tighten guidance for single-layer affective exceptions. If the mechanism is present but the relationship rationale is absent, the rubric should continue to score none.
- Keep small edge features in the planning denominator. The seven missed features were narrow, but they gave useful resolution between an almost-complete plan and a truly complete plan.
- Consider adding a report field for layer-level plan-only gaps. Several partial rows had plan grounding for missing layers that B did not reconstruct, and that diagnostic is useful when comparing plan compression strategies.
