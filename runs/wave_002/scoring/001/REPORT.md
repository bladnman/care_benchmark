## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was 36/54; affective recovery was 64/98. Functional losses were mostly missing failure-mode specifics, as in F16 and F18. Affective losses were mostly compressed relationship reasons, as in F8, F23, F31, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 recovery was 72/108, slightly above weight-2 recovery (28/44). High-weight whys often preserved at least the primary layer but dropped downstream consequences.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The candidate preserved philosophy better than per-feature why. System-level fidelity was 92.9%; feature-level fidelity was 59.7%.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** The most commonly dropped layer was the downstream consequence: F1, F10, F13, F16, F18, F24, F30, F35, and F40 all lost or thinned the consequence layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync and accessibility were strongest. Social and relationship-copy features were weakest: visit-log transparency, actual-aviary sharing, leaderboards, and export all became rules without their deeper reasons.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Ten cases were rule-without-why. The strict evidence gate denied credit where a v1 looser semantic read might have awarded it for mechanisms alone.

What this suggests: the plan is a very strong implementation artifact, but it often encodes the product as operational constraints rather than as reasons future builders can defend.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Preserve the F29-F40 targeted headroom additions. They caught meaningful losses in a strong run, especially F31, F34, F38, and F39.
- Add more exception whys that distinguish "we do not build this" from "why this tempting thing would change the relationship." F14, F23, and F27 show that the rule alone can survive while the product reason does not.
- Keep multi-layer feature whys. This run often recovered L1 but missed L3, which is useful signal.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar, but make layer notes mandatory for S-level partials. S2 was cross-cuttingly present as a rule but weak on the processed-vs-seen rationale.
- Continue evidence-bound scoring. It separated impressive architecture from durable intent preservation.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The prompt states this was fresh context; the frozen reconstruction was read-only and was not modified.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run, so no variance signal is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on settle definition, single-scene/no-panning, evening palette, visits-default-off, and actual-aviary visitor view. These do not materially change the main finding because all F1-F40 anchors remained reachable.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The hardest S-level judgment was S2: the rule was everywhere, but its affective processed-vs-seen layer was not.
- **Confabulation cases.** None were counted as ungrounded reconstruction; the reconstruction was generally conservative and frequently marked non-recovery.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F5, F6, F8, F14, F19, F23, F31, F34, F38, and F39 were denied because the rule survived without the why.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing data existed only through phase 2A for this slot; score JSON includes only bounded available phase timing and does not fabricate phase 2B duration.
