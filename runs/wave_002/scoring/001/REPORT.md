## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered slightly better (77/98) than functional whys (40/54), but the difference is not the main story. The plan was strong on both; losses came from compression of secondary/downstream layers rather than inability to handle one type.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-2 whys recovered better (84.1%) than weight-3 whys (74.1%). Multi-layer whys exposed missing failure-mode rationale, especially F1/F13 presence corruption, F18 silent drift loss, F24 narration-as-wrong-feature, F30 unlock economy, and F31 retroactive identity collapse.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The planner encoded philosophy very strongly. System-level fidelity was 96.4%; feature-level fidelity dropped to 72.6% because the reconstruction often preserved mechanisms while losing why layers.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Audio, accessibility, privacy, and simulation were broadly well preserved. Thin spots were targeted headroom rows around account deletion, visit-log transparency, visitor actual-aviary rationale, and some social/share downstream consequences.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The strict evidence gate denied credit where the reconstruction said what to build but not why it mattered: F35 deletion, F38 visit log, and F39 show-off mode are the cleanest examples.

What this suggests: this plan is excellent as an engineering blueprint and carries most system intent. The remaining loss is not breadth; it is the deeper rationale behind a few features that look operationally obvious once translated into architecture.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They separated "implemented the rule" from "preserved the relationship/privacy rationale" in a way the original canonical set likely would not.
- Add or refine gold whys around boring account/privacy lifecycle features. Account deletion and visit logs are easy to implement mechanically while losing the product why.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for weight-3 whys. The most useful signal here was not full misses; it was partial recovery with L2/L3 consequences compressed away.
- Consider adding a more lexically distinctive restraint-over-richness system why. S4 was clearly in the plan, but the reconstruction did not name it as a system principle; future scorer consistency may benefit from a clearer reconstruction hook.
- Continue reporting rule-without-why counts. They are a useful diagnostic for plans that are strong implementation documents but imperfect intent carriers.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The reconstruction was treated as frozen and was not modified. The audit found no evidence of gold/rubric contamination.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one sampled plan/reconstruction pair. No variance estimate is available from this score alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Feature 7 was counted captured because the plan operationalized the glossary concepts despite lacking a separate glossary section. Feature 52 was counted missed because "5s undo" does not specify click-anywhere undo. These calls do not materially change the result.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 is the most subjective call: the plan preserves restraint in more than three decisions, but B did not identify it in the system-level intent section, so it is partial.
- **Confabulation cases.** No ungrounded reconstruction cases were found. The reconstruction reads plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several plausible v1-style recoveries were denied because exact rationale evidence was absent or incomplete, especially F35, F38, and F39.
- **Operational timing.** The timing ledger supplied phase 1 and phase 2A timings for run 001. It did not contain phase 2B timing, so no phase 2B timing was fabricated.
