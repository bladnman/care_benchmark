## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional recovery was stronger: 40.0 / 54.0 weighted points (74.1%). Affective recovery was 62.0 / 98.0 (63.3%). The PLAN and reconstruction carried architecture, privacy, and simulation invariants more faithfully than relationship-framing nuances.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 74.0 / 108.0 (68.5%), slightly better than weight-2 whys at 28.0 / 44.0 (63.6%). High-weight mechanics like S6, S7, F13, F17, F18, F20, F25, and F31 survived especially well.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (78.6%) exceeded feature-level fidelity (64.5%). The plan carried broad principles, but several feature exceptions were reconstructed as rules without their deeper why.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary implementation causes were usually recovered. Secondary affective contributors and downstream product-shape consequences were more often dropped, especially F2, F4, F9, F10, F12, F14, F16, F24, F27, F30, and F40.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Sync, privacy, presence, accessibility, and first-frame performance were strong. Social sharing and relationship-specific exclusions were weaker: F23, F37, and F39 were rule-only. Onboarding/adoption nuance also leaked at F29.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter v06 operator denied credit where implementation rules survived without rationale: F6, F8, F19, F23, F29, F34, F35, F37, and F39. S3 and S4 were plan-only system principles: preserved cross-cuttingly but not named by the reconstruction.

What the failure shape suggests: this candidate is excellent at producing an executable plan and preserving hard technical constraints, but it compresses some affective product intent into prohibitions. A downstream builder would likely build the right surfaces, but might not always know why the surface must stay that way when future pressure arrives.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted feature-level headroom rows. The F29-F40 additions exposed real separation between "rule captured" and "why recovered."
- Add or emphasize rationale prompts for affective exclusions: numeric trait hiding, no catalogs, no public comparison, per-invite sharing, account export, and no show-off visit mode.
- Preserve the evidence-bound ledger. Without it, this run would look nearly perfect because the PLAN captures all features; the ledger shows where the why was only implicit or absent.
- Consider reporting plan-only system-principle recoveries separately. S3 and S4 were visibly present in PLAN but not identified in B, which is different from a pure miss.
- Keep the cross-cutting bar at three inherited decisions for now. It discriminated well without excluding obvious system principles.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scorer treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. No memory or peer slots were used.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run with no variance estimate.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Feature 7 (glossary) and feature 64 (top bar contents) were inclusive captures. Marking either missed would not change the core finding.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3 and S4 are the subjective cases: the PLAN preserves them in multiple features, but the reconstruction did not name the principle directly.
- **Confabulation cases.** No scored why was marked as ungrounded reconstruction; suspect-looking phrasing in B was traceable to PLAN language.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Nine feature-level rows were rule-without-why denials. These are mechanism survivals, not denominator exclusions.
- **Timing.** `TIMING.json` exposed phase 1 and phase 2A timing only for run 001. Phase 2B timing was omitted rather than fabricated.
