## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better: 32/48 weighted points (66.7%) vs affective whys at 46/98 (46.9%). The plan's engineering-heavy specificity helped F2, F17, F18, F36, and F40; relationship-shaping rationales such as F29, F31, F32, F33, and F39 leaked.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 56/104 (53.8%); weight-2 whys recovered 22/42 (52.4%). Higher-weight functional items did well, but high-weight affective exceptions such as F9 and F31 still failed outright.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was 16/28 (57.1%), while reachable feature-level fidelity was 62/118 (52.5%). B preserved broad philosophy somewhat better than detailed feature rationales.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers usually survived when the plan named the mechanism. Secondary and downstream layers leaked most often: e.g. F10 kept no-toast/ambient return but lost processed-vs-seen and different-product consequences; F15 kept first-frame motion but lost spinner-as-machine rationale.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync/accessibility performed strongest. Social and onboarding were mostly mechanism-only: visits were read-only and revocable, but actual-aviary/no-show-off and per-invite-private-relationship rationales were thinner.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The strict evidence gate denied likely v1-style credit for F5, F6, F19, F23, F29, F31-F33, and F39 because the reconstruction or plan carried the rule without the gold why.

What this suggests: the candidate plan is a strong build plan with high surface coverage, but it compresses affective rationale into implementation choices. The reconstructor often noticed the implementation rule and sometimes the top-level philosophy, but not the deeper exception logic that protects the product from adjacent-product drift.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They exposed meaningful differences between "implemented the rule" and "preserved the reason."
- Add more feature-level exceptions for affective product boundaries: onboarding choice, social exposure, editable personal records, and state continuity. These are exactly where plausible implementation language can hide lost intent.
- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It made the report auditable and prevented broad plan quality from inflating why recovery.
- Consider slightly more guidance for scorers on implicit capture vs explicit why, especially for API-only omissions like read-only notebook and same-snapshot visitor rendering.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar at three inherited decisions. It was subjective but workable, and it kept philosophy boilerplate from becoming automatic full credit.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The reconstruction appears plan-derived. It follows the plan's section headings, uses plan vocabulary, and contains no gold IDs or rubric terms.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run in one wave; no variance estimate is available from this artifact alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls affected features 7, 44, 47, 51, and 97. Marking all five as not captured would lower planning quality from 90.8% to 86.7%, but would only change feature-why reachability for F11, F33, and F39.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The strict ≥3 inherited-decisions bar was applied using the appendix in §2.2.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction was counted. Several broad reconstructions were denied credit as too thin rather than treated as confabulation.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The largest denial class was rule-without-why: 11 included whys.
- **Operational compromise.** TIMING.json contained phase 1 and phase 2A timing for run 001 only; no phase-2B timing was available at write time, so it is omitted.
