## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional architecture was strongest: S7/S8 and F17/F20/F26/F36 all recovered cleanly. Affective losses concentrated where the rule survived but the relationship rationale did not, especially F5, F31, F33, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys performed better overall (86.0/108.0) than weight-2 whys (30.0/44.0). The plan tended to make load-bearing systems explicit enough for reconstruction, while quieter single-layer exceptions were easier to compress into rules.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was very high at 92.9%, but S3 and S4 became partial because the reconstruction did not elevate specificity and restraint as named cross-cutting principles. Feature-level fidelity was lower at 72.6% because v06 denied credit for several rule-only recoveries.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary operational layers usually survived. Secondary or downstream affective layers were more often dropped: F1/F13 lost the silent population-wide failure layer, F16 lost the retrofit consequence, and F18 lost the invisible cross-device overwrite example.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, privacy, performance, accessibility, and core simulation were strong. Social and identity relationship whys were mixed: F21-F23 and F37-F38 recovered, while F39 and F31 lost the deeper relationship framing.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Seven included whys were marked rule_without_why. The most important were F6, F19, F31, F33, and F39, where a builder would likely implement the rule but miss why the rule matters.

The failure shape suggests an excellent implementation plan whose reconstruction carries architecture and refusal principles better than subtle relationship semantics.
