## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered **40.0 / 54.0** weighted points; affective whys recovered **62.0 / 98.0**. The planner and reconstructor were strongest on architecture, privacy, sync, and presence precision; the bigger leaks were affective exceptions such as F23, F29, F30, F34, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered **72.0 / 108.0**; weight-2 whys recovered **30.0 / 44.0**. High-weight whys did not dominate the loss, but many multi-layer rows dropped one secondary/consequence layer.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was **85.7%**, much stronger than feature-level **62.9%**. The system philosophy survived as review gates and architecture, while individual feature whys often became implementation rules.
- **Multi-layer pattern.** Primary causes were usually found. Secondary affective/consequence layers were most often compressed away: S2, F4, F9, F10, F12, F14, F16, F18, F20, F24, F25, F31, and F40 all lost at least one layer.
- **Subdomain pattern.** Presence/simulation/sync did well (F1, F2, F13, F17 full). Social and account-adjacent affective whys were weaker: no-leaderboards (F23), starter arrivals (F29), age-based offers (F30), export as relationship-copy (F34), and visitor actualness (F39).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter evidence operator denied credit for mechanism-only recoveries in eight cases: F5, F11, F23, F29, F30, F34, F35, and F39. No ungrounded reconstruction cases were found.

The failure shape suggests a strong planner that encoded most constraints directly, plus a reconstructor that accurately extracted the plan but often summarized feature-level affective rationale into mechanism labels.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound format. It exposed the difference between excellent capture (115/120) and lower feature-level why recovery (78/124).
- Add more feature-level exception whys around account/social surfaces. This run shows rules like export, deletion, visit actualness, and no leaderboards are easy to implement while losing their relational rationale.
- Consider making some currently single-layer affective whys multi-layer. Rows like F23 and F39 had partial semantic preservation, but single-layer scoring forces all-or-none; multi-layer structure would record the gradient more faithfully.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar. It distinguished S5/S6/S7/S8/S9 clean recoveries from S3/S4 cases where the plan preserved decisions but B did not reconstruct the principle.
- Continue requiring exact reconstruction evidence. It prevented PLAN-only rationale from silently improving the reconstruction score.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I used only the allowed phase-two files, assigned PLAN, metadata, assigned timing entry, and frozen reconstruction. I did not read `prd/`, peer slots, or other waves.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one candidate run and one reconstruction. There is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I counted bird-chosen perch (#55) and weather-affects-mood (#60) inclusively. This does not change the feature-why denominator because all F1-F40 anchors were captured anyway.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3 and S4 were the most subjective: the plan preserves the design stance across several features, but the reconstruction does not state each principle cleanly in its system section.
- **Confabulation cases.** None found. The reconstruction stayed grounded in PLAN language.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F5, F11, F23, F29, F30, F34, F35, and F39 were mechanism-only or explicitly unrecoverable, so they received no feature-level why credit.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Eight included whys were marked rule-without-why; this was the main reason feature-level fidelity lagged planning quality.
