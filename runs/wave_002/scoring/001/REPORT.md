## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional architecture survived better because the plan encoded it as service boundaries and data rules. Affective exceptions often survived only as prohibitions: F10, F14, F22, F23, F27, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys mostly landed partial: the primary mechanism survived, while failure-mode and downstream-consequence layers were dropped. F2, F4, F9, F15, F17, F18, F20, F24, F30, and F31 are representative.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was higher (60.7%) than feature-level fidelity (40.0%). The reconstruction captured broad architecture and philosophy better than the specific feature exceptions.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 was usually easiest. Layer 2 survived when tied to a concrete implementation constraint, such as sync or WebAudio. Layer 3, the downstream consequence, was most often missing.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, privacy, accessibility, and audio had the strongest recoveries. Social and anti-gamification surfaces were often rule-only. Layout captured many rules but missed specific evening/night/color details.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter v06 operator denied credit where the reconstruction named a feature but not its reason: F5, F8, F11, F19, F26, F32, F33, F34, F37, F38, and F39.

The failure shape suggests a planner that can cover a large implementation surface and preserve architectural constraints, but compresses affective rationale into product-tone shorthand. The reconstruction then faithfully carries the shorthand, not the lost why.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom whys like F29-F40. They exposed meaningful rule-without-why cases after the plan captured almost every feature.
- Add a separate mechanism-survival diagnostic, since many rows were captured operationally but scored none on rationale. This would make high planning quality with low fidelity easier to interpret.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for weight-3 whys. It was the clearest discriminator: primary layers survived, downstream consequence layers did not.
- For system-level whys, add more examples that distinguish naming a principle from preserving all three layers. S1, S2, S6, and S7 were partial largely because downstream consequences were compressed away.
- Keep the affective/functional mix. Functional architecture had higher fidelity, while affective exception whys supplied the useful ceiling pressure.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The artifacts support fresh-context isolation: no gold IDs, rubric terms, or 1:1 gold-list heading/order appeared in the frozen reconstruction.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only; there is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive for bird naming, settle opt-in, account export, and visits default-off. These increased planning quality and made more whys reachable.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The three-feature bar was satisfied for every S why, but S1/S2/S6/S7 still scored partial because not every layer appeared in the reconstruction.
- **Confabulation cases.** No significant ungrounded reconstruction was found; the reconstruction mostly stayed plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** v06 denied several plausible semantic recoveries when exact rationale evidence was missing, especially rule-only rows in social, gamification, tone, and export/log surfaces.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 17 included rule-without-why cases, the dominant diagnostic pattern in this run.
