## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered better overall (76.0/98.0, 77.6%) than functional whys (30.0/54.0, 55.6%). The functional misses were usually downstream failure stories: F1, F2, F16, F17 and F18 kept implementation precision but dropped why the alternative corrupts the product.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 recovery (72.0/108.0, 66.7%) lagged weight-2 recovery (34.0/44.0, 77.3%) because multi-layer whys often preserved the primary rule but lost secondary/downstream layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** Feature-level fidelity (71.0%) beat system-level fidelity (64.3%). The reconstruction was strong at carrying concrete feature rationale, but less strong at elevating cross-cutting philosophies, especially S4 and S6.
- **Multi-layer pattern.** Primary implementation causes usually survived. The most commonly dropped layer was the downstream consequence: S1, S7, F2, F9, F16, F17, F18 and F27 all lost some of the failure-mode story.
- **Subdomain pattern.** Accessibility and privacy were strongest (S8, S9, F20, F24, F25, F36-F40). Audio/simulation had excellent mechanisms but thinner whys (F4, F16-F18). Adoption and account-lifecycle headroom exposed sharper losses (F29 none, F35 partial).
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Four rows were rule-without-why cases: F6, F19, F26 and F29. F6 and F26 were plan-only-not-reconstructed: the plan carried more rationale than B recovered.

The failure shape suggests that this plan carried an unusually complete build surface, but the reconstruction compressed several affective and downstream arguments into implementation rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They were useful even under a very complete plan: F29 and F35 surfaced rationale loss that ordinary feature capture would hide.
- Preserve the evidence-bound ledger. It prevented feature saturation from becoming fidelity saturation and made rule-without-why cases visible.
- Split system-level scoring into two reported subcomponents in v2: PLAN cross-cutting preservation and reconstruction identification. S6 shows that a plan can strongly preserve a principle while B fails to name it as a principle.
- Add scorer guidance for system-level multi-layer rows where the plan has all layers but the reconstruction lacks them. The current partial/full interaction is workable but subjective.
- Consider adding more downstream-consequence prompts in gold construction. This run repeatedly kept the first layer and lost the consequence layer.

- **Borderline capture calls.** I marked all 120 features captured. Feature 7 (glossary) was distributed across domain records and definitions rather than a literal glossary heading; by the rubric's inclusive capture standard, it was buildable enough to count.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The strict three-feature bar was easy to satisfy in the plan for all S1-S9. The subjective part was B identification, especially S4-S6.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no clear ungrounded reconstruction claims. Losses were compression or omission, not invented rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F19 and F29 are clean examples where the rule survived but the gold rationale did not. F6 and F26 had plan-side rationale but were not recovered by B.
