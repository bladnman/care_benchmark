## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys survived much better (48/54, 88.9%) than affective whys (65/98, 66.3%). The plan and reconstruction were especially strong on presence, server-side simulation, no-LWW, and telemetry boundaries. Losses concentrated in affective exceptions where the rule was obvious but the relationship rationale was subtle: F29, F33, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys scored 84/108 (77.8%) versus 29/44 (65.9%) for weight-2. High-weight whys survived better overall because the plan repeatedly named the load-bearing architecture; still, many multi-layer whys lost L3 downstream consequences.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (82.1%) exceeded feature-level fidelity (72.6%). The planner preserved philosophy well, and the reconstructor found it, but targeted feature-specific reasons were more likely to collapse into mechanism-only notes.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes usually survived; downstream consequence layers were the common casualty. Examples: S1, S2, F9, F10, F14, F27, F30, and F31.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync and privacy were strongest. Social optional features were weakest: F22, F38, and F39 became quiet/read-only rules without the attention-loop, transparency, or show-off rationale. Accessibility was strong, with S9, F24, F25, and F40 all recovered fully.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied likely v1-style credit for F19, F22, F33, F38, and F39 because reconstruction evidence carried the mechanism but not the gold why. Seven rows were marked `rule_without_why`.

The failure shape suggests the plan was excellent as an implementation artifact, but dense implementation prose still compresses relationship-shaping rationale unless the rationale is repeatedly tied to concrete mechanisms.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the F29-F40 targeted headroom additions. They did useful discriminating work by exposing rule-only preservation.
- Add more exception whys around quiet social surfaces, account-history surfaces, and record authorship. This run shows those areas are easy to implement correctly while dropping the deeper reason.
- Keep multi-layer scoring, especially downstream-consequence layers. Most partials came from missing L3, which is useful signal about whether a plan carries intent or just design rules.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar at three inherited decisions, but continue requiring a written appendix. S4 shows why: the plan preserved restraint, but the reconstruction did not name it as a system principle.
- Keep the evidence-bound denial rule. Without it, mechanism-only rows would be over-credited and the benchmark would saturate on strong implementation plans.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scorer read only the phase_two package, the assigned PLAN, candidate metadata, run 001 timing, and the frozen reconstruction. The reconstruction was not modified.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This score has no variance signal; it is one planner output, one blind reconstruction, and one scoring pass.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Feature 7 and feature 53 were captured inclusively. Marking both missed would change planning from 100.0% to 98.3% but would not alter feature-level why denominators, because neither is one of F1-F40.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 was the most judgment-sensitive call. The PLAN preserves restraint across caps, chrome, density, and palette, while the reconstruction recovers only pieces; partial credit reflects that split.
- **Confabulation cases.** No recovery credit was awarded to ungrounded reconstruction; the audit found no clear contamination or confabulation signatures.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rows with rule-only evidence were scored none for why recovery even when the feature was captured.
- **Operational compromise.** TIMING.json had phase1 and phase2a for run 001 but no phase2b entry at scoring time, so the score JSON includes only available bounded timing fields.
