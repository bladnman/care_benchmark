## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better: 28.0 / 54.0 (51.9%) vs affective 34.0 / 96.0 (35.4%). Server authority, presence precision, and telemetry boundaries survived more cleanly than relationship-shaping refusals.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys were only slightly better: 46.0 / 108.0 (42.6%) vs weight-2 at 16.0 / 42.0 (38.1%). The extra layers mostly exposed omissions rather than boosting recovery.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The plan preserved broad philosophy better than feature-specific rationale: system fidelity was 64.3%, feature fidelity 36.1%.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanism layers survived most often. Secondary temptation layers and downstream product-collapse consequences were usually dropped, especially F10, F14, F27, F30, F31, and F35.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and privacy were comparatively strong. Social and non-goal whys were often rule-only. Accessibility preserved surface mechanics and cadence but not always the full first-class-surface rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several apparent recoveries became `rule_without_why`: F5, F8, F22, F23, F26, F29, F33, F34, and F39 are representative.

The failure shape suggests an implementation planner that can enumerate a broad product surface but tends to flatten the reason behind delicate product constraints into a requirements checklist.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated captured mechanisms from recovered intent, especially for non-goals and social features.
- Add or retain targeted feature whys around tempting product extensions. This run shows no-streak/no-leaderboard/no-catalog/no-show-off rules can be implemented without preserving why they matter.
- Keep multi-layer weight-3 whys; they surfaced the consistent loss of secondary contributor and downstream consequence layers.
- Consider adding more functional whys around account lifecycle and error/sync surfaces. The planner missed the sync-conflict/account-error feature while capturing much of the backend sync model.
- For system-level whys like S3/S4, future rubrics could require a named plan rationale for full cross-cutting preservation, not only repeated constraints.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The reconstruction reads plan-derived: it uses the plan headings and vocabulary, with no gold IDs or scorer vocabulary found by grep.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only; it provides no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls affected planning quality for features 18, 25, 37, 44, 52, 64, and 70. Without them, planning would be lower by about 5.8 percentage points.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3 and S4 were the hardest calls because the plan implemented nearby rules without strongly naming the philosophy.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear scoring-affecting confabulation was found. The main issue was missing rationale, not invented rationale.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rule-only preservation was common and accounts for most none recoveries among captured features.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing data existed for phase 1 and phase 2A only; phase 2B timing was not present in `TIMING.json` during scoring.
