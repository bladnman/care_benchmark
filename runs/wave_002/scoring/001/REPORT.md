## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Both are strong. Functional recovery is slightly lower because F16 only partially recovered the layered synthetic-UUID rationale and F35 was explicitly not recoverable. Affective leakage clustered around exception surfaces: F8, F23, F29, plus partial F14 and F27.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 recovery was high at 98/108, but the dropped layers were informative: F14 and F27 lost the intention-rotation layer, and F16 lost the retrofit/non-negotiable downstream layer.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System philosophy survived almost perfectly. Feature-level fidelity dropped where the plan preserved the rule as an architectural invariant but not the full gold rationale.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes were almost always recovered. Secondary temptation/contributor layers were most likely to compress away.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, audio, accessibility, and performance were especially faithful. Social/comparison and account lifecycle whys had the most leakage.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F8 is the clearest operator effect: the reconstruction supplied a plausible rationale, but I could not ground that rationale in the PLAN beyond the operational rule, so it receives no credit.

The failure shape suggests a very strong planner whose high-level design philosophy was preserved, but whose concise rule declarations sometimes left phase 2A without enough plan-grounded rationale for smaller exception whys.
