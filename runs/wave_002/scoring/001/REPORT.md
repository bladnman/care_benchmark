## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional and affective recovery were close: functional whys recovered 42/54, affective whys 75/98. The largest affective leaks were rule-only cases: F5, F8, F23, plus partials such as F10, F14, F27, and F30.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-2 whys recovered 35/44; weight-3 whys recovered 82/108. High-weight whys were not harder overall, but multi-layer consequence clauses were commonly compressed.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The PLAN and reconstruction preserved philosophy strongly: server authority, privacy, accessibility, aliveness, and honest presence all survived. Feature-level whys leaked first, especially where the gold why explains a refusal of familiar product patterns rather than an implementation rule.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** The most frequently dropped layer was downstream consequence: F1/F13 silent drift corruption, F2 Tamagotchi-vs-screensaver calibration, F16 retrofit risk, F17 client-tick merge failure, F27 gamification foothold, F30 unlock economy, and F40 same-rhythm narration.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, privacy, and accessibility were strong. Bird affect details were mixed: stable identity and return greeting survived, while mood-shaped motion, numeric-trait hiding, and bird-count cap rationale lost why detail. Social features were mostly strong except the public-comparison rationale for no leaderboards.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Four rows became explicit rule-without-why denials: F5, F6, F8, F23. Several v1-style plausible recoveries stayed partial because one or more multi-layer clauses lacked exact reconstruction evidence.

The failure shape suggests a very capable plan that encodes what to build almost completely, while compressing some of the product-theory reasons behind refusals and calibration boundaries.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. This run would look nearly saturated on implementation coverage alone; the evidence gate exposed meaningful differences in rationale carry-through.
- Add more targeted affective exception whys. The strongest missed signal came from familiar product instincts the plan refused: stats, comparison surfaces, catalog choice, notification loops, and reward framing.
- Keep multi-layer whys for high-value calibration rules, but make the downstream-consequence layer easy for scorers to identify consistently. Most partials came from missing consequence clauses rather than missing mechanisms.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting bar. It distinguished S2/S4 compression from the stronger S6/S7/S8/S9 recoveries without punishing the plan for different wording.
- Consider a separate diagnostic for "rule captured, rationale absent" across all whys, since those cases were the cleanest actionable failure here.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scoring pass used the frozen reconstruction plus assigned plan, rubric, gold list, constants, schema, and templates. No PRD, peer slots, or other waves were read.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run for one candidate/evaluator setup, so there is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** No capture calls were treated as borderline; the PLAN addressed all 120 features with enough substance to count.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The most subjective calls were S2 and S4: both had strong plan inheritance but thinner reconstruction of the deeper affective rationale.
- **Confabulation cases.** None were found. The reconstruction reads as grounded in PLAN wording and structure.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F5, F6, F8, and F23 were denied because the frozen reconstruction preserved the rule/mechanism without the gold rationale.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 4 such cases, all included in the denominator because their features were captured.
- **Timing.** TIMING.json contained phase 1 and phase 2A records for run 001 only. Phase 2B timing was unavailable and was not fabricated.
