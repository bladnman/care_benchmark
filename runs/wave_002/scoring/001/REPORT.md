## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better (42/54 = 77.8%) than affective whys (64/98 = 65.3%). The plan was very strong on enforceable architecture: server-owned state, privacy topology, synthetic IDs, tick scheduling, no-LWW, and telemetry boundaries. Affective exceptions around social sharing and user relationship framing leaked more often: F22, F23, F29, F33, F37, F38, and F39 all preserved mechanisms but not the deeper reason.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys survived better (84/108 = 77.8%) than weight-2 whys (22/44 = 50.0%). The planner made load-bearing multi-layer material prominent enough for the reconstructor to preserve many of it. Lower-weight exception rationales were easier to compress into rules, especially account/social/notebook exceptions.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was perfect under the rubric: the reconstruction identified all nine principles and the plan preserved each across at least three implementation decisions. Feature-level fidelity was lower at 62.9%, because the reconstruction often turned a specific product why into an implementation rule.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanism layers survived most often. Secondary affective layers such as "why this temptation is dangerous" and downstream consequences such as "this becomes a different product" were the most commonly compressed away.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, sync, privacy infrastructure, performance, and reduced-motion accessibility were strong. Social-optionality and relationship-copy surfaces were weak: F22/F23/F37/F38/F39 mostly became privacy or feature-scope rules instead of preserving the intended relationship frame.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied credit where a v1 semantic scorer might be tempted to award it. For example, F19 had matter-of-fact error copy, but the reconstruction did not recover the reason that naturalist phrasing in an error context reads as evasive. F33 had append-only/read-only notebook mechanics, but not the observer-record-not-journal why.

The failure shape suggests the plan was excellent as a build document and strong on cross-cutting intent, but some local affective whys were encoded as guardrails rather than as rationales.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more feature-level exception whys in social and account surfaces. This run shows that mechanisms like per-invite sharing, visit logs, and no show-off mode can be planned while their relationship rationale disappears.
- Keep the v06 evidence-bound operator. It separated true rationale recovery from rule preservation in a useful way, especially for F4, F11, F19, F22, F23, F33, and F37-F39.
- Consider tagging "mechanism-only" failures separately in aggregate dashboards. The 12 rule-without-why cases here are diagnostically different from missing features.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar at three inherited decisions. The bar was easy to apply here and highlighted that the candidate carried philosophy well even when some local whys leaked.
- For future Pocket-Aviary-like instances, add headroom around quiet data-rights and social-transparency whys. Frontier plans may implement these rules from privacy/security instincts while missing the product relationship reason.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The run was scored from the frozen reconstruction and assigned plan only. I did not modify the reconstruction or read PRD material, peer slots, or other waves.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one candidate run and one reconstruction. It gives no sampling variance estimate.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I counted feature 7 as captured because the plan operationally defined domain terms across sections despite no formal glossary. I did not count feature 87 (privacy policy link) or feature 116 (last-two-majors browser support matrix). These calls change planning quality by only 1.7 points.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The (b) judgment was easiest for S2, S6, S7, S8, and S9 because the plan had explicit enforcement mechanisms. S3 and S5 required reading voice, prose, notebook, narration, captions, and error copy together.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not find awarded recovery that was ungrounded in the plan. Several rows were denied or partial because the reconstruction was mechanism-only, not because it confabulated.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Twelve feature whys had rule_without_why = yes. Ten multi-layer rows received partial credit because the reconstruction recovered some layers but omitted other plan-grounded layers.
- **Operational compromise.** TIMING.json contained phase1 and phase2a data only for run 001. The score JSON includes those bounded timings and omits unavailable phase2b fields.
