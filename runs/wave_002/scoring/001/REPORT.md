## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better: 48.0/54.0 weighted (88.9%) versus affective 72.0/98.0 weighted (73.5%). The plan encoded architecture and privacy/sync mechanics very explicitly; more poetic relationship rationales like F8, F10, F11, and F39 leaked.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys did slightly better (88.0/108.0, 81.5%) than weight-2 whys (32.0/44.0, 72.7%). Load-bearing implementation areas such as drift, server-side simulation, and accessibility carried enough structure to survive, while compact exception rationales were easier to drop.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was higher (85.7%) than feature-level fidelity (77.4%). The reconstruction preserved the architecture/philosophy spine but sometimes converted feature whys into rules: F5, F8, F10, F11, F22, F35, and F39.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary implementation causes survived most often. Secondary affective contributors and downstream relationship consequences were the common losses: S2 lost the seen-vs-processed and one-toast-collapses-surface layers; F9 lost the notice-never-announce consequence; F20 lost the private-relationship-to-data-product layer.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Sync, drift, privacy architecture, performance, audio, and accessibility were strong. Social and interaction exceptions were weaker, especially visit notifications and the visitor actual-aviary/no-show-off rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied credit where the reconstruction had an operational rule but not the why. This changed several likely v1-style successes into none or partial recoveries, especially F8, F10, F11, F14, and F35.

The failure shape suggests that the plan is excellent as an engineering artifact, but the reconstruction preferentially preserved executable architecture over relationship-preserving rationale.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted affective exception whys in the interaction and social areas. This run shows headroom precisely where a rule is easy to state but the product relationship rationale is easy to compress away.
- Keep the v06 evidence-bound operator. Without it, F8/F10/F11/F35 would look deceptively healthy because the rule survived, even though the why did not.
- Consider adding layer prompts or scorer guidance that separately names "rule," "user relationship rationale," and "downstream product consequence." The most common leakage was not missing features; it was collapsing rationale into implementation.
- Keep system-level cross-cutting at the >=3 inheritance bar, but require report rows to say whether B identified the principle in the system section or only elsewhere. S6 was recoverable overall, but its clearest evidence appeared in per-feature text.
- Preserve targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They separated strong plans from strong rationale transmission: F30/F31/F40 survived, while F35/F39 did not.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scoring prompt stated fresh context and the frozen reconstruction showed no gold IDs or rubric vocabulary. Same-context contamination is not evident.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one candidate plan and one reconstruction. There is no variance signal for this model/harness/effort configuration.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I counted all 120 features as captured. The broadest call is the domain-glossary row, which the plan covers through definitions and implementation contracts rather than a literal glossary section; changing that single call would barely affect the qualitative finding.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3 and S4 were the most subjective system-level calls: the plan clearly preserves them, but the frozen reconstruction does not identify the principles cleanly in its system-level section.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not find positive recovery that depended on ungrounded reconstruction claims. Ungrounded reconstruction count is 0.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F5, F8, F10, F11, F22, F35, and F39 are the key denials where feature capture or rule survival was not enough for why recovery.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Eight included whys were marked rule_without_why, concentrated in affective exceptions and social/account lifecycle details.
