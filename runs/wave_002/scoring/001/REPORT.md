## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys survived better (47.5%) than affective whys (31.8%). The plan preserved server authority, presence conjunction, and sync rules, but compressed relationship rationales such as S2, F14, F23, F27, and F31.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight/multi-layer whys mostly became partial recoveries (38.6%): primary mechanics were visible, but secondary contributor and downstream consequence layers usually dropped. Weight-2 whys landed at 33.3%, mostly because many were rule-without-why cases.
- **System vs feature.** System-level fidelity (50.0%) exceeded feature-level fidelity (33.3%). The reconstruction could infer broad architecture and accessibility intent, while fine-grained exceptions like no welcome toast, read-only notebook, and per-invite sharing were simply absent.
- **Subdomain pattern.** Accounts/sync and accessibility performed best; social_optional and interactions performed worst. The social area was reduced to a generic no-social-network rule, losing visit behavior, visit log, notification, and privacy-control details.
- **Evidence-bound effect.** Many plausible v1-style recoveries were denied or reduced because the frozen reconstruction only restated a rule. The clearest examples are F4, F14, F23, F26, F27, F30, F31, and F36.

The failure shape suggests a compressed implementation checklist: enough to sketch a build, but not enough to transmit the product's affective contract.

---

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the evidence-bound operator. This run shows why: rule-level mentions were frequent, but rationale-level recovery was much thinner.
- Add more targeted social/interaction whys in future instances. Generic exclusions masked the absence of no-toast, visit-read-only, visit-log, no-notification, and public-comparison rationale.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for high-weight whys. The partials here are informative: the planner often captured L1 mechanics and dropped L2/L3 calibration or consequence.
- Consider requiring plans to distinguish feature mechanics from rationale explicitly. That would make it harder for terse plans to appear stronger than they are.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar strict, but require scorers to document inherited decisions. S7 was genuinely cross-cutting; S2/S5/S8 showed why generic principle mentions are not enough.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This phase-2B scoring used the frozen reconstruction and did not modify it. The reconstruction itself appears plan-derived; see VALIDITY_AUDIT.md.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one candidate run in wave_002; it gives no variance signal by itself.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Several sparse bullets were counted inclusively: features 9, 27, 45, 50, 66, 76, 86, 98, 103, 112, and 114. These raise planning quality but do not create why recovery without reconstruction evidence.
- **System cross-cutting.** S1, S3, S7, and S9 had enough inherited decisions to consider cross-cutting; S2, S4, S5, S6, and S8 were partial or thin under the three-feature bar.
- **Confabulation.** No material ungrounded reconstruction rationale was found. The reconstructor more often wrote NOT RECOVERABLE FROM PLAN than invented a why.
- **Evidence denials.** Rule-without-why cases were the main driver of lost fidelity, especially F4, F14, F23, F26, F27, F30, F31, and F36.
