## 3. Diagnostic patterns

The plan captured the full product surface, including the quiet exclusions and the targeted headroom additions F29-F40. The encoding leak appears after capture: many reconstruction rows preserve mechanisms but flatten the rationale into engineering summaries.

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered 36.0 / 54.0 (66.7%); affective whys recovered 63.0 / 98.0 (64.3%). Affective misses clustered around F5, F8, F11, F38, and F39, where the rule appeared but the relationship-shaping reason did not.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 66.0 / 108.0 (61.1%), below weight-2's 75.0%. The extra layers exposed lost downstream consequences.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was stronger (75.0%) than feature-level fidelity (62.9%). The plan encoded cross-cutting principles well; the reconstruction often retained the architecture while omitting local whys.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 was usually easiest: the mechanism or primary cause survived. Layer 3 was most frequently dropped: examples include F9's generic-arrival-animation consequence, F16's retrofit warning, F30's economy erosion, and F31's retroactive relationship loss.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/privacy/sync were strongest (S7, S8, F17, F19, F21-F23, F34, F36-F37). Affective interaction and social-subtlety rows were weaker: settle optionality, visit-log purpose, actual-aviary/no-show-off rationale, and mood-as-readable-motion all leaked.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter operator mattered. F5, F8, F11, F38, and F39 would look superficially present under a mechanism-only read, but there was no frozen reconstruction evidence for the gold rationale.

What this suggests: the candidate is very strong at exhaustive implementation planning. The loss is not missing scope; it is rationale compression, especially when the rationale is a product-relationship consequence rather than a technical invariant.
