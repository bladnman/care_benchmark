## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better (27.0/44.0) than affective whys (45.0/88.0). The plan was strongest where architecture encoded the reason directly: F2, F17, S7. It leaked more on relationship-shaping product refusals such as F23, F29, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 58.7%, above weight-2 whys at 45.0%. The high-weight engineering whys carried more explicit calibration and failure-mode text, while many lower-weight affective exceptions survived only as rules.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (57.1%) was only slightly above feature-level fidelity (53.8%). The system pass preserved broad architecture and accessibility commitments, but S2 and S8 show that named philosophy can be lost even when related rules remain.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary layers were most likely to survive; secondary temptation/failure-mode layers and downstream product-consequence layers often disappeared. Examples: F1 kept the conjunction rule but lost population-drift corruption; F14 kept no streaks but lost adjacent-disguise boundaries.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync performed best. Social, ambient UI/loading, and subtle relationship-copy/privacy surfaces leaked. Accessibility preserved concrete controls and cadence but usually compressed the charm/parity rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several apparent recoveries were denied because they were only mechanisms: F5 mood motion, F6 cap 7, F19 matter-of-fact sync copy, F26 TTFB, F38 visit log, and F39 visitor view.

The failure shape suggests the planner can execute a competent implementation plan while still flattening the “why this rule exists” layer, especially for affective product boundaries that are easy to treat as ordinary scope choices.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted feature-level affective exceptions like F29, F38, and F39; they exposed meaningful headroom even in a broad plan.
- Keep multi-layer whys for calibration and refusal surfaces. The L2/L3 drop-off was the clearest signal of compression, not noise.
- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It prevented mechanism-only rows such as F26 and F23 from receiving credit just because the implementation rule was present.
- Clarify capture guidance for “weak cluster capture.” F30 was captured inclusively from expansion/no-gamification language, but the age-based unlock rule was not really developed; future rubrics could mark such anchors borderline by default.
- Keep the system-level cross-cutting bar but require scorers to name the inherited decisions, as done above. S2 and S8 show why restatement alone is insufficient.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring treated the frozen reconstruction as the only phase-2A artifact and did not modify it. The prompt stated fresh context; I did not use subagents or prior-run memory.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one candidate run at one configuration, so there is no variance signal inside this score.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on feature capture for #2, #3, #32, #61, and #97. Together they affect planning quality by about 4.2 points and feature reachability for F8/F39.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S2, S4, and S8 were the most subjective; related mechanisms existed, but the named cross-cutting rationale was incomplete.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no strong ungrounded reconstruction cases; the reconstruction mainly paraphrases the plan and sometimes over-compresses.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Rule-without-why denials are the main scoring effect: 14 included whys had mechanism evidence but insufficient rationale recovery.
- **Operational compromise.** TIMING.json provided phase 1 and phase 2A timing only; the score JSON includes those bounded fields and omits fabricated phase-2B timing.
