## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys survived better (55.6%) than affective whys (42.7%). The planner and reconstructor were strongest where the why was architectural: S7, S8, F17, F21, and F36. Affective whys often collapsed into rules: F8 hid trait numbers but lost the stat-management rationale; F33 kept the notebook read-only but lost the observer-record rationale.

- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 53.7%, much better than weight-2 at 31.0%. The high-weight rows had more repeated plan support, so at least one or two layers often survived. Weight-2 exceptions were brittle: F22, F23, F26, F29, F34, F37, and F39 mostly became "what to build" without "why it matters."

- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (60.7%) exceeded feature-level fidelity (44.3%). The plan preserved broad principles strongly, but the reconstruction did not carry many feature-local exceptions. S2 was recognizable, yet F10/F14/F22/F38 split unevenly depending on how explicit the local rationale was.

- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary-cause layers survived most often. Secondary layers were the first to disappear: individual-signal failure cases in F1/F13, the common-toast temptation in F10, the relationship-as-data-product layer in F20, and the sensory-equity layer in S9/F24 all leaked.

- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync and simulation were strongest, especially server-canonical state and telemetry boundaries. Social and product-affect exceptions were weakest: no leaderboards, no notifications, per-invite sharing, and visitor actual-aviary rules were captured as boundaries but not as relationship-protecting decisions.

- **Evidence-bound effects.** The evidence gate denied several plausible v1-style recoveries. F6 had the cap rationale in PLAN through the seven-bird chorus test, but the reconstruction used a performance/deepening rationale. F26 had the 500ms budget but not the "already running" affective-perf bridge. F34 and F35 had complete account mechanics but not relationship/retention rationales.

What the failure shape suggests: this candidate is an excellent build planner and a moderate intent encoder. It preserves scope and architecture, but compresses product philosophy into testable prohibitions, which is exactly where CARE's second phase exposes loss.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted headroom whys around "obvious but wrong extension" cases. F22/F23/F37/F39 show that social/privacy product boundaries are easy to implement and easy to de-rationalize.
- Keep multi-layer whys for high-value affective rules. The split on F10/F14/F27 was useful: the rule survived, the temptation and downstream product-shift layers often did not.
- Consider a separate diagnostic for "rule preserved but wrong rationale substituted." F6 is a clean example: the plan grounded recognizability, while the reconstruction substituted performance and pacing.
- Preserve the v06 evidence-bound operator. It prevented over-credit on F8, F11, F26, F33, and F35, where a less strict scorer could be tempted to award semantic credit for a mechanism alone.
- Revisit system-level partial scoring language. S3/S4 can be cross-cutting in the plan while absent from B's system summary; the current rubric handles this, but the evidence fields are awkward because recovery credit can be plan-only at the system level.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated phase 2A as frozen and did not modify `RECONSTRUCTION.md`. I did not read `prd/`, peer slots, or other waves.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run. No variance signal is available from this slot alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on bird naming (#24), adoption flow (#25), single horizontal scene (#53), and mood persistence (#29). These affect planning quality more than fidelity; the main intent denominator remains almost fully reachable.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The cross-cutting bar was easiest for S7/S8 and hardest for S3/S4, where PLAN decisions inherited the principles but B's system wording did not name them cleanly.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not count any clear ungrounded-reconstruction cases. F6 was a wrong-rationale substitution, but the substituted rationale was itself grounded in PLAN, so I counted it as rule-without-why/plan-only rather than contamination.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The most important denials were F6, F8, F11, F26, F33, F34, F35, F37, and F39.
