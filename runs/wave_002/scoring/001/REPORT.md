## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered slightly better (81.5%) than affective whys (78.6%). The losses cluster in affective exception rationales: F8, F23, F29, F30, F31, and F34.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 79.6%; weight-2 whys recovered at 79.5%. The high-weight structural rows were strong, but several high-weight exception rows only recovered one or two layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (89.3%) exceeds feature-level fidelity (77.4%). The PLAN preserves big principles across implementation, but B loses some local why detail when a feature is common enough to look self-explanatory.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary implementation layers survived best. Secondary affective explanations and downstream product-consequence layers dropped more often: S2 L2, F2 L3, F4 L3, F7 L2, F14 L1-L2, F16 L3, F20 L2, F27 L2-L3, F30 L1/L3, F31 L2-L3, and F40 L1.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Server simulation, sync, deletion, reduced motion, narration-as-prose, and no-LWW recovered strongly. Public/social comparison, export-as-relationship-right, starter-bird onboarding, and numeric trait secrecy were weaker.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** A looser scorer might have given credit to mechanism-heavy rows, but v06 denied or limited them where B recovered the rule without the why: F8, F14, F23, F27, F29, F30, F31, and F34.

The failure shape suggests the candidate plan is excellent at preserving executable architecture and product constraints. The reconstruction is strongest when the plan states the causal rationale in operational language, and weakest when the reason is an affective relationship-frame behind a familiar product refusal.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom additions like F29-F40. They found compression in an otherwise near-complete plan, especially starter-birds-not-catalog, export-as-relationship-copy, and visitor/show-off/social boundaries.
- Ask phase-1 planners to label exception rationales separately from invariant rules. Rows such as F8, F23, and F34 show that a plan can be highly buildable while still leaving the relationship-protecting reason under-specified.
- Preserve the multi-layer scheme for high-weight whys. It differentiated rows where the primary mechanism survived but the downstream failure mode did not, especially F2, F4, F16, F20, F27, F30, F31, and F40.
- For system-level scoring, consider a clearer instruction that B's system-level section must identify product restraint as its own principle. S4 was preserved in the PLAN but not named as a system-level reconstruction item.
- Keep the evidence-bound reports. The stricter fields made rule-only recovery visible without penalizing feature capture.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** Based on the artifact audit, fresh-context separation appears intact: the reconstruction has no gold S/F IDs and follows PLAN section structure. The scorer cannot independently inspect the spawning context, so this is artifact-based.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run in wave_002; no variance signal is available from this individual score.
- **Borderline capture calls.** All 120 features were counted captured. The only mild borderline was feature 7, the glossary row, counted because the implementation plan defines the relevant terms across architecture, engine, voice, accessibility, and visits sections.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 is the main subjective call: the PLAN clearly preserves restraint across bird count, layout, chrome, palette/audio recognizability, and interaction choices, but B did not surface it as a system-level principle.
- **Confabulation cases.** None were scored as recovery. Some B phrasing is high-level, but every credited rationale had matching PLAN grounding.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F8, F23, F29, and F34 were denied despite captured features because the reconstruction did not carry the gold why. Several multi-layer rows received partial instead of full for missing layers.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Eight included rows were marked rule-without-why: F8, F14, F23, F27, F29, F30, F31, and F34.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing metadata was available for phase 1 and phase 2A only in `runs/wave_002/TIMING.json`; no phase-2B timing fields were fabricated.
