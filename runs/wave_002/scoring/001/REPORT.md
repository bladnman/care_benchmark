## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better: 32/54 weighted points (59.3%) versus affective whys at 41/96 (42.7%). Server-side simulation, sync, privacy, and performance had concrete implementation anchors; affective whys often became rules without relationship rationale.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys did better than weight-2 whys, 56/108 (51.9%) versus 17/42 (40.5%), mostly because the plan gave multi-layer architecture for drift, tick ownership, accessibility, and age-based bird arrivals.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was much stronger at 67.9% than feature-level fidelity at 44.3%. The planner preserved broad philosophy and architecture, but feature-specific rationales such as F29, F33, F38, and F39 leaked.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary causes were most often recovered. Secondary contributors and downstream consequences were the common losses, especially in F10, F14, F27, and F30.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accessibility and server/sync were strongest. Social and account lifecycle were weakest at the why level: visits, visit logs, actual-aviary sharing, export, and deletion mostly survived as mechanisms.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The v06 gate denied several plausible v1-style credits where the rule was present but the why was not: F6, F8, F11, F21, F23, F29, F31, F33, F34, F35, F38, and F39.

What this suggests: the candidate is good at translating PRD scope into executable engineering structures, and weaker at preserving the product's non-obvious affective rationale unless the plan itself already names the rationale bluntly.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep targeted headroom whys like F29-F40. They found real failures after the obvious architecture was captured.
- Add a few more account/support surface whys. This run missed the sync-conflict matter-of-fact tone entirely, which is a useful product-boundary test.
- Keep feature-level exception whys. The strict refusals around gamification and social sharing are where rule-without-why shows up most clearly.
- Consider asking phase-2A reconstructors to retain fewer broad feature bullets and more explicit rationale layers, while still not revealing gold IDs. That would make evidence-bound scoring less dependent on scattered prose.
- Preserve the cross-cutting system bar, but keep requiring concrete inheritance lists; it prevented over-crediting principles that only appeared as slogans.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** Accepted from the harness prompt. This scorer did not alter the frozen reconstruction and did not read `prd/` or peer slots.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one scored sample; no variance conclusion is possible from this file alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls included glossary/domain terms, mood persistence, bird-chosen perch, evening shift, background-tab pause, visits default-off, and species pool. They did not materially change the finding that feature-level why recovery lagged system-level recovery.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The most subjective calls were S3-S6, where the plan preserved many operational pieces but B did not always name the principle in system terms.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were counted. The dominant failure mode was honest non-recovery or rule-without-why.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several rows had strong PLAN mechanisms but no frozen reconstruction rationale; these remained zero under v06.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** 17 included rows were marked rule-without-why, concentrated in social/account lifecycle and affective exception features.
