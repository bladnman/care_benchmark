## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys were much stronger: 48.0/54.0 vs affective 67.0/98.0. Server-canonical state, IDs, no LWW, privacy, and drift calibration stayed crisp; social/notice/charm rationales compressed more.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 86.0/108.0, outperforming weight-2 whys at 29.0/44.0. The larger load-bearing mechanisms were easier for the reconstruction to recover than small exception rationales.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was 75.0%; feature-level fidelity was 75.8%. The plan preserved philosophy well, but B partially compressed S1/S2/S4/S9.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary rules usually survived; secondary affective explanations and downstream social/announcement consequences were most often dropped.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, simulation, privacy, audio, and accessibility performed strongly. Social sharing and quiet UI-detail whys were weaker, especially F22, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Eight rows were rule-without-why: F5, F11, F15, F22, F23, F37, F38, F39. These would likely be over-credited by a looser semantic scorer.

The failure shape suggests a strong planner whose plan captured the product in detail, plus a reconstruction that could recover explicit architectural rationales more reliably than subtle affective/social intent.
