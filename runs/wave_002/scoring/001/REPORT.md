## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys scored slightly higher (44.0/54.0) than affective whys (75.0/98.0). The reconstruction was especially strong on server-owned state, privacy boundaries, deletion, and aggregate telemetry; relationship-tone rationales like F23 and F33 leaked more.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 79.6%, modestly above weight-2 whys at 75.0%. Multi-layer scoring exposed compression rather than outright misses: many rows kept the primary cause while losing secondary/downstream rationale.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (89.3%) was higher than feature-level fidelity (75.8%). The plan carried product philosophy well; targeted headroom whys show where exact reasons compressed into implementation rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary layers usually survived. Secondary temptation/failure-mode layers were the most common losses, especially F1, F2, F10, F12, F13, F14, F16, F27, F30, and F35.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accessibility, simulation, sync, privacy, and visit-read-only behavior were strong. Social comparison/no-notification rationales and UI relationship-rationales around hidden vectors and read-only notebooks were weaker.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** v06 denied credit for plausible v1-style recoveries where B preserved only a rule: F8, F22, F23, F29, and F33. No positive recovery relied on ungrounded reconstruction.

The failure shape suggests that the candidate plan is excellent for build completeness, while the reconstruction compresses non-obvious affective reasons into rule lists.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the reconstruction as frozen and did not modify it. The validity audit found no strong contamination signatures.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run only; no variance estimate is available.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on features 57, 62, 64, 93, and 117. The planning score would remain high even if one or two of these were marked missed.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S1, S2, and S4 were the most subjective. S4 received only partial because the plan preserved restraint but B did not identify that system principle directly.
- **Confabulation cases.** I counted no ungrounded reconstruction cases; B's positive claims were plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F8, F22, F23, F29, and F33 are the clearest cases where the rule survived but the why did not.
- **Operational compromise.** `TIMING.json` contained phase 1 and phase 2A only for run 001 at scoring time. The score JSON omits phase 2B timing fields rather than fabricating them.
