## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better (56.0%) than affective whys (45.6%). The plan is strongest where architecture itself carries the reason: S7, F18, F36, F24, and F25. Affective social and relationship rationales often collapsed into implementation rules: F21, F22, F23, F37, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 60.0%, much better than weight-2 whys at 22.5%. The heavier whys were often repeated across plan sections, while weight-2 exceptions were easy to preserve as rules without their rationale.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (67.9%) beat feature-level fidelity (44.6%). The planner carried philosophy better than individual exception whys, especially around privacy and accessibility. Feature-level losses are concentrated in layout/interaction details and social-optionality rationales.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary implementation-cause layers survived most often. Secondary contributor and downstream consequence layers were the usual losses: S1 lost the staleness consequence, F7 lost the bird-deletion consequence, F16 lost PII leakage/retrofit layers, and F20 lost relationship-as-data-product framing.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accessibility and sync were strongest; social optionality was weakest for why recovery despite full feature capture. Interactions and aviary layout drove most planning misses.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Seventeen reachable whys were rule-without-why cases. No ungrounded reconstruction was found, which suggests the reconstructor was conservative rather than confabulatory.

The failure shape suggests this candidate is an excellent build-plan compressor but not always an intent-preservation compressor: it protects core architecture and accessibility philosophy, while many small affective reasons become plain product constraints.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted social and micro-interaction whys. This run captured visit mechanics, notification defaults, leaderboards, offers, and settle in many places, but often lost why those rules exist.
- Keep the evidence-bound operator. It cleanly separated rule preservation from why preservation, especially for F21-F23 and F37-F39.
- Add more worked examples for system-level partials. S3-S5 were semantically adjacent to plan principles but not exact; examples would reduce scorer variance.
- Preserve multi-layer scoring for high-stakes architecture. It identified exactly which layers compressed away in drift, server tick, vector persistence, and privacy.
- Consider a small set of layout/interaction headroom features in v2. The current plan missed perch-zone specifics, panning, top-bar fade, palette, cropping, offer kinds, settle undo, and no welcome toast.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The frozen reconstruction appears plan-derived and uncontaminated. It follows the plan section order, uses plan vocabulary, and contains no gold IDs or scorer-side vocabulary.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run. There is no variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Borderlines were judged inclusively when the implementation substance was enough to build the right thing, but not when key concrete behavior was absent. The largest borderline choices were feature 8 (presence definition), feature 40/41 (offer details), feature 44 (settle opt-in), feature 54 (three perch zones), and feature 81 (sync-conflict tone).
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The most subjective calls were S3-S5 partials and S4 especially, where technical restraint survived more clearly than the product-specific restraint-over-richness rationale.
- **Confabulation cases.** None were counted. The reconstruction was conservative and used NOT RECOVERABLE frequently.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Most denials were rule-only cases, not plan-only-not-reconstructed or ungrounded-reconstruction cases.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** There were 17, concentrated in social optionality, exports/deletion, bird identity, age-gated unlocks, and smaller interaction/layout rules.
