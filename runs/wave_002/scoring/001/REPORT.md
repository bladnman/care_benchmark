## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better: 38.0 / 54.0 (70.4%) versus affective 54.0 / 92.0 (58.7%). Architecture/privacy items like S7, F17, F18, and F36 held better than social/relationship items like F22, F23, F33, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 66.0 / 104.0 (63.5%). Weight-2 whys recovered 26.0 / 42.0 (61.9%). The heavier items did not automatically survive; many got partial credit because L2/L3 rationale was compressed away.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was high (85.7%) because the plan repeatedly named its principles. Feature-level fidelity was much lower (57.6%) because specific product exceptions often survived as rules only.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** L1 rules commonly survived; downstream consequences were the common leak. F1/F13 missed silent drift corruption detail, F10 missed the bird-greeting-as-entire-welcome consequence, and F31 missed retroactive relationship loss.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync/audio were strong. Social, notebook, and user-record surfaces were weaker because the plan named constraints without the relationship-level rationale behind them.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** The stricter v06 evidence gate denied plausible v1-style credit for F8, F14, F19, F22, F23, F27, F33, F34, F38, and F39. These were not absent features; they were rule-without-why cases.

The failure shape suggests a planner that is excellent at implementation inventory and architectural correctness, but less reliable at carrying non-obvious affective rationale down to individual feature exceptions.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the F29-F40 targeted headroom additions. They separated broad capture from rationale preservation, especially for stable identity, account deletion, telemetry, sharing, visit logs, and narration cadence.
- Add more feature-level whys around record/relationship boundaries. This run captured export, visit log, read-only notebook, and visitor snapshots, but often missed why those surfaces must stay quiet and non-performative.
- Preserve the evidence-bound operator. It caught the main issue: the plan often had the rule, and the reconstruction often restated the rule, but neither carried the gold why.
- Keep per-layer scoring for weight-3 system whys. S2 and S6 were cross-cutting in the plan but still lost important layers, so a single binary system score would hide useful signal.
- Consider adding a reconstruction-prompt instruction that asks for "why, not just what" per feature. Honest NOT RECOVERABLE rows are useful; plausible mechanism-only rows are harder to distinguish without the current audit.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scorer treated the run as fresh-context, read only the allowed phase-two/run artifacts, and did not modify the frozen reconstruction.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run; there is no variance signal inside slot 001.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Inclusive calls were made for feature 7 (glossary via domain model), 57 (evening palette), 64 (settings/top bar), and 97 (actual aviary via snapshot). They raised planning quality modestly but did not affect the main fidelity conclusion.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The three-feature bar was straightforward for S1, S3, S4, S5, S7, S8, and S9. S2 and S6 were cross-cutting but still partial because layers were missing.
- **Confabulation cases.** No clear ungrounded reconstruction cases were found. Some phrases were interpretive, but they were supported by PLAN passages.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several rows had rule evidence but no gold-rationale evidence; those are listed as rule-without-why rather than recovered.
- **Operational compromise.** TIMING.json supplied phase 1 and phase 2A timing only; phase 2B timing was unavailable and omitted from the timing object.
