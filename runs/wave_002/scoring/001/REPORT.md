## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered at 59.3% (32.0/54.0), affective at 51.0% (50.0/98.0). The plan was especially concrete on server authorship, privacy boundaries, deletion, telemetry, and performance. Affective whys around anti-comparison, anti-stat-management, and anti-engagement were more often compressed into rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 57.4% (62.0/108.0), better than weight-2 whys at 45.5% (20.0/44.0). The high-weight whys were more likely to have at least a primary layer survive, but often lost secondary/downstream rationale.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was 64.3%, feature-level fidelity 51.6%. Broad principles such as privacy, voice split, restraint, and accessibility timing survived better than specific exception whys.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** The most commonly dropped material was layer 2/3: failure scenarios, product-consequence explanations, and why a tempting alternative would change the relationship.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation, privacy, accessibility, and active-first rendering scored well. Social, notebook, export, no-gamification, and no-announcement features often preserved the rule but not the deeper rationale.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Fifteen rows were rule-without-why. Important denied cases included F8, F10, F14, F19, F22, F23, F27, F29, F33, F34, and F37.

What this suggests: the candidate produced an unusually complete implementation plan, but the plan's intent encoding is denser around architecture than around affective exceptions. The reconstructor could rebuild the product surface, but not always why each refusal mattered.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Keep the v06 evidence-bound operator. It separated excellent feature coverage from partial intent carry-through.
- Preserve the targeted headroom additions F29-F40. They exposed meaningful gaps even in a strong plan, especially export, read-only notebook, per-invite sharing, and mood persistence.
- Add or retain more affective exception whys where the rule is easy to state but the rationale is easy to lose: no catalog choice, no stat exposure, no social notifications, no editable notebook, no public comparison.
- Keep multi-layer scoring for high-weight whys. Layer accounting was the strongest signal for partial recoveries that would otherwise look full.
- Clarify whether system-level recovery may cite per-feature reconstruction evidence. I scored S6 conservatively because it was not elevated in the reconstruction's system-level section, even though presence accounting was reconstructed in detail later.

- **Confabulation cases.** I found no scored ungrounded-reconstruction cases. The main losses were omissions or mechanism-only recovery.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several rows that looked semantically adjacent were denied because exact frozen-reconstruction why evidence was missing.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** Mechanism-only recoveries were concentrated in hidden vectors, no welcome toast, settle optionality, anti-streak/anti-gamification, error voice, social notifications, leaderboards, starter birds, notebook read-only, export, and per-invite sharing.
