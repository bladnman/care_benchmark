## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys did better (40 / 54 = 74.1%) than affective whys (60 / 98 = 61.2%). The reconstruction was especially strong on canonical server state, additive deltas, telemetry boundaries, and deletion/export mechanics, while it often compressed affective exception whys into rules.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 72 / 108 (66.7%) and weight-2 whys recovered 28 / 44 (63.6%). High-weight whys did slightly better, but many multi-layer affective whys lost their secondary or downstream layers.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was higher (78.6%) than feature-level fidelity (62.9%). The plan carries philosophy well enough that B found broad principles, but feature-specific whys like F6, F8, F29, F31, F33, and F39 often survived only as implementation rules or not at all.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary mechanism layers were usually recovered first. Secondary contributor layers about temptation, adjacent-product pressure, or user-intention rotation were most often dropped: S2, F10, F14, F27, and F30 show this pattern.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync, deletion, telemetry, and accessibility were strongest. Bird affect and social-sharing whys were more mixed: F21/F22/F38 recovered well, but F29/F31/F39 did not recover their relationship-preserving rationales.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several v1-style plausible recoveries were denied or capped because B had the rule without the why: F6 bird cap, F8 hidden vectors, F11 settle optionality, F19 system-error tone, F29 starter birds, F31 stable identity, F33 read-only notebook, and F39 actual aviary visits.

What this suggests: this candidate plan is unusually complete as an engineering plan, but the reconstruction exposes a common compression failure. Architecture and testable invariants transmit cleanly; poetic/relationship-preserving reasons often need to be made more explicit in the plan if they are expected to survive a fresh-context handoff.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted affective exception whys like F29/F31/F39. This run shows that planners can implement the rule while dropping why the rule matters to the relationship.
- Keep the v06 evidence ledger. It cleanly separated the very high capture score from more moderate why recovery and prevented plan-only rationale from inflating fidelity.
- Consider a sub-score for layer class within multi-layer whys. The recurrent dropped layer here was not the primary mechanism, but the secondary/downstream product-consequence layer.
- Preserve the >=3-feature system cross-cutting bar, but require scorers to list the inherited decisions as this report does. It helped distinguish S7/S9 full recovery from S2/S6 partial recovery.
- For future instances, include a few more non-technical voice/tone exceptions. F19 and S5 show that system-vs-naturalist voice can be present as a rule while its trust rationale disappears.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** I treated the frozen reconstruction as read-only and scored only its text against the PLAN and gold list. I did not modify it and did not use peer slots, other waves, or `prd/`.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one candidate plan and one reconstruction, so there is no within-model variance signal.
- **Borderline capture calls.** The capture count leans inclusive on #43 settle and #55 bird-chosen perch. The two no-capture calls were #7 glossary and #69 color palette spec; neither affects feature-level why reachability.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** The hardest calls were S2, S4, S5, and S6. In each, the PLAN preserved the principle across enough decisions, but B's reconstruction did not recover all rationale layers.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no ungrounded reconstruction cases. The validity audit separately records a PASS verdict.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F6, F8, F11, F19, F29, F31, F33, and F39 are the clearest cases where the operational rule survived but the gold why did not.
- **Timing.** `TIMING.json` contained phase1 and phase2a data for run 001 but no phase2b timing yet, so the score JSON includes only the bounded available phase timing fields.
