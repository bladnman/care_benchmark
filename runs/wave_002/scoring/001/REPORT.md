## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Affective whys recovered better (74.0/98 = 75.5%) than functional whys (35.0/54 = 64.8%). Functional misses concentrated where the plan encoded architecture but not the gold rationale, such as F6, F16, and F20.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered at 80.0/108 = 74.1%, above weight-2 at 29.0/44 = 65.9%. The load-bearing, multi-layer items were usually visible in the plan, but secondary layers were often compressed away.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was high (25.0/28 = 89.3%) because the plan opened with binding invariants. Feature-level fidelity fell to 84.0/124 = 67.7%; many precise mechanisms survived without their deeper why.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary causes were most often recovered. Secondary contributors were dropped repeatedly: F1 tab-open counterexamples, F2 instrument-vs-user calibration, F10 well-meaning-toast temptation, F14 intention rotation, F27 cheap engagement-metric temptation, and F40 same-rhythm-as-visual narration.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Simulation/sync was strong on canonical server state (F17, F18) and monotonic drift (F3), weaker on presence precision nuance (F1/F13). Accessibility kept reduced-motion and day-one shipping (S9/F25) but only partially recovered screen-reader prose/cadence layers (F24/F40). Social controls recovered visit silence and logs (F22/F38), while the show-off-mode rationale (F39) was not recovered.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several rows would look good under rule-only scoring but get no why credit here: F5, F6, F19, F33, F34, and F39. These are important because they show the difference between building the right behavior and carrying the product reason for it.

The failure shape suggests the candidate plan is excellent as an execution spec, but it compresses some held-out rationale into invariant slogans. A fresh reconstructor could recover the broad philosophy, then lost some edge-case motives and downstream consequences.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more targeted single-layer exceptions like F33/F39; they exposed real rationale loss even in a very strong plan.
- Keep multi-layer whys, but score layer loss explicitly in dashboards. The strongest signal here was not total failure; it was secondary/downstream compression.
- Consider a subscore for "rule-only survival". This run had 9 rule-without-why cases, a useful diagnostic distinct from both capture and fidelity.
- Preserve the system-level cross-cutting bar. The plan made system whys unusually recoverable because its first section used binding invariants; the >=3-inheritance test still separated S3/S4/S8 from the fully recovered principles.
- For future products, keep a mix of affective and technical privacy whys. Functional privacy rows (S8/F20/F36/F34) showed a good spread between full technical boundary recovery and missing relationship rationale.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring treated `RECONSTRUCTION.md` as frozen and did not modify it. The reconstruction itself reads plan-derived; see `VALIDITY_AUDIT.md`.
- **Single-run-at-temperature limitation.** This is one run in wave_002; no variance estimate is available from this slot alone.
- **Borderline capture calls.** Feature 7 (domain glossary) was counted captured inclusively because the plan defines the terms operationally across implementation sections. Feature 87 (privacy-policy link) was the only capture miss.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S3, S4, and S8 were partial because the PLAN preserved the principle across multiple decisions, but the reconstruction did not explicitly identify the gold-level rationale as a system principle.
- **Confabulation cases.** I found no clear ungrounded reconstruction claims; the unsupported-looking phrases sampled in the validity audit map back to exact PLAN text.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** F5, F6, F19, F33, F34, and F39 were denied despite captured features because the reconstruction did not recover the gold rationale.
- **Rule-without-why cases.** The largest rule-only cluster was around target headroom and exception whys: cap-seven recognizability, read-only notebook as observer record, account export as relationship copy, and visitor actual-aviary/no-show-off mode.
