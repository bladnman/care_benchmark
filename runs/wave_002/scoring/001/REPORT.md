## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered slightly better by weight (42/54) than affective whys (74/98). The plan encoded architecture very explicitly, so server authority, no-LWW, deletion, telemetry, and accessibility mechanics survived well. Affective social/relationship rationales leaked more often: F29, F33, and F39 kept the rule but not the deeper relationship meaning.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys did not strongly outperform weight-2 whys. Primary mechanisms survived, but secondary temptation and downstream-consequence layers often dropped out: F1, F2, F4, F16, F20, F27, F30, F31, and F40.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** The headline split is stark: system-level fidelity is 100.0%, while feature-level fidelity is 71.0%. The reconstruction understood the product philosophy, but targeted feature whys exposed compression loss.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Layer 1 was usually recovered. Layer 2 and Layer 3 were more vulnerable, especially where the gold why explained a future failure mode rather than an immediate implementation rule.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Accounts/sync architecture and accessibility did well. Social and relationship-shaping surfaces were weaker when the rationale was about curation, public performance, or notification pressure.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** F11 and F19 are good v06 examples: the rules survived, but without exact rationale evidence they score none rather than receiving plausible semantic credit.

What this suggests: the candidate plan is strong enough for implementation coverage, and strong enough to carry system philosophy, but still compresses several non-obvious human-product rationales into operational constraints.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Preserve the targeted headroom additions. They are doing useful work: F29, F33, and F39 separated strong implementation planning from deeper intent preservation.
- Keep multi-layer scoring. The losses were not random; they clustered in secondary contributors and downstream consequences.
- Add more future-maintainer temptation layers. F16 and F27 show that plans can implement the right thing while omitting why future teams must not relax it.
- Retain the system-level cross-cutting bar, but report the split prominently. This run could look saturated if only system whys were inspected.
- Consider a few more feature-level whys in the social/account boundary area, where rules are easy to state and relationship meaning is easy to flatten.

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** The scorer used only the phase-two package, assigned plan, assigned metadata/timing, and frozen reconstruction. No PRD, peer slots, or other waves were read.

- **Borderline capture calls.** Capture leaned inclusive for feature 7 (glossary/domain terms), 23 (species-pool count), 44 (settle opt-in), and 57 (evening palette/call quieting). These did not affect feature-level reachability except where gold whys were already strongly present.

- **Confabulation cases.** No clear confabulation cases were found. The reconstruction reads plan-derived.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** Several v1-style plausible recoveries were denied because exact rationale evidence was missing, especially F11, F19, F29, F33, and F39.
