## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Functional whys recovered better (34.0/54.0 = 63.0%) than affective whys (40.0/86.0 = 46.5%). The plan was strongest where the rationale was architectural: S7, F17, F18, F36. It was weakest where the product depends on quiet affective refusals: F14, F22, F23, F27, F37-F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** Weight-3 whys recovered 54.0/100.0 (54.0%) versus weight-2 whys at 20.0/40.0 (50.0%). High-weight items did slightly better, but many multi-layer rationales lost the downstream consequence layer.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity (64.3%) beat feature-level fidelity (50.0%). The plan preserved philosophy as broad architecture, but local feature whys often collapsed to mechanisms.
- **Multi-layer recovery patterns.** Primary implementation layers often survived; secondary temptation/failure-mode layers and downstream product-consequence layers were most often dropped. F30, F31, F35, and F40 show this clearly.
- **Subdomain patterns.** Sync and simulation were strong; accessibility was mixed but above average; social and quiet-product constraints were mostly rule-only. Return-greeting and welcome surfaces were the most visible planning miss.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Several v1-style plausible recoveries were denied because the PLAN had only a rule, not the gold rationale: F19, F22, F23, F26, F29, F31, F34, and F37-F39. One ungrounded reconstruction concern was F19, where B supplied an operational-clarity rationale not really grounded in PLAN prose.

What this suggests: the candidate is a strong implementation planner but compresses many product-identity whys into implementation exclusions. The broad architecture keeps the aviary coherent; the fragile parts are the quiet, easy-to-add surfaces the product is specifically trying to avoid.
