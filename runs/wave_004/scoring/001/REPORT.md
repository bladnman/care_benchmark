## 3. Diagnostic patterns

- **Affective vs functional.** Recovery was nearly even by type: functional whys recovered 42 / 54 weight (77.8%) and affective whys recovered 77 / 98 (78.6%). The plan's privacy/sync architecture helped functional recovery, while affective losses clustered in small social and motion whys such as F5, F22, F38, and F39.
- **Weight-3 vs weight-2.** High-weight whys survived better: 90 / 108 (83.3%) versus 29 / 44 (65.9%) for weight-2. Load-bearing themes such as F2, F4, F17, F18, F24, F25, and F27 were reconstructed well; small exception whys were easier to compress away.
- **System-level vs feature-level.** System-level fidelity was much higher (25 / 28, 89.3%) than feature-level fidelity (94 / 124, 75.8%). The reconstruction understood the plan's philosophy, but several per-feature rationales became plain implementation rules.
- **Multi-layer recovery.** Primary layers survived most often. Secondary failure-mode layers were commonly dropped in F1/F13 (why laxer presence corrupts drift), F16 (retrofit impossibility), F35 (hard-delete privacy), and F30 (economy-collapse consequence).
- **Subdomain patterns.** Sync, privacy, performance, accessibility, audio, and drift were strong. Social optional features had more mechanism-only recovery: no leaderboards, visit log, and visitor-as-is view were present, but their relationship-protection whys leaked.
- **Evidence-bound effects.** Eight included feature whys were rule-without-why cases: F5, F6, F9, F11, F22, F23, F38, and F39. S4 was plan-only at system level: the plan preserved restraint, but the reconstruction did not name the principle.

What this suggests: the candidate produced an unusually comprehensive engineering plan and the reconstruction recovered the load-bearing philosophy. The remaining loss is mostly compression of small feature-level exception whys into rules.

## 4. Recommendations for v2 hardening

- Add more single-feature exception whys around social surfaces. This run preserved the mechanics of visits and sharing, but lost why F22/F38/F39 matter.
- Keep multi-layer whys for precision-sensitive engineering rules. F1/F13 show that agents can retain the conjunction while dropping the population-corruption rationale.
- Consider requiring the phase-2A reconstruction to keep a compact "why ledger" keyed to plan sections rather than a broad feature paraphrase. This may reduce rule-without-why cases without leaking gold IDs.
- Preserve the current evidence-bound operator. It usefully separated high planning coverage from lower why recovery and exposed where the reconstruction sounded good but was only mechanism-level.
- Revisit the S4 cross-cutting bar in future rubrics. Here restraint was clearly in the plan, but not named in B; the partial score seems fair, but it is the most judgment-sensitive system row.

## 5. Methodology caveats

- **Fresh-context fidelity.** This scoring used only the phase-two package, assigned plan, metadata, timing for run 001, and frozen reconstruction. I did not read PRD files, peer slots, other waves, or prior memories.
- **Single-run limitation.** This is one run; it has no variance signal by itself.
- **Borderline capture calls.** I leaned inclusive on the distributed glossary (#7), settle opt-in (#44), single scene/no panning (#53), snapshot-on-visibility (#76), and visits default-off (#90). These did not affect feature-level reachability because all F1-F40 anchors were still captured.
- **System-level cross-cutting.** S4 is the subjective case: the plan strongly preserves restraint, but B did not identify it as a system intent. S6 was partial because the reconstruction kept precision/non-punishment but lost the laxer-signal population-corruption layer.
- **Confabulation cases.** I did not find ungrounded reconstruction claims that affected scoring; losses were mostly omissions.
- **Evidence-bound denials.** The largest denials were mechanism-only rows where the plan/reconstruction said what to build but not why: F5, F6, F9, F11, F22, F23, F38, F39.
- **Operational compromise.** Timing data existed for phase 1 and phase 2A only; no phase 2B timing was fabricated.
